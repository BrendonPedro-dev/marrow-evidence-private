# Reframe sub-question 8: FREE/CHEAP LICENSING AS A STAGE ("marketing licensing")

**The question (from the stress-test brief):** do small or new IPs (or their agencies) license for free or near-free in exchange for exposure, retail presence, or data, deliberately?
Tally whether creators frame such arrangements as smart growth or exploitation, and who says which.
Also: any precedent of tiered IP strategies by maturity (new IP = exposure terms, proven IP = fee terms)?
This tests whether "overall strategy per IP based on its status" matches market behavior or invents it.

**Compiled:** 2026-07-18 (iteration 11 of the reframe run).
Every NEW quote below was verified verbatim against page source via curl grep on raw HTML unless flagged otherwise.
Instances 3 and 6 reuse citations already verified in findings/reframe-7-control-vs-outcomes.md and findings/reframe-4-segment-split.md.

**Verdict: SUPPORTS the "marketing licensing" concept as matching real market behavior, with one important qualifier about WHO practices it.**
Deliberate free or near-free licensing in exchange for exposure verifiably exists, and the biggest case in the region (熊本熊/Kumamon) is not a marginal hack but the founding strategy of a merchandise economy worth over 100 billion yen a year.
The maturity-tier precedent is also real and clean: Kumamon licensed free domestically while building fame, then switched to 5-7% fees for overseas use in 2018 once the IP was proven; Crypton runs 初音未来 on a free non-commercial tier with consent-gated commercial use; and at the proven end, 貿易雜誌 records Disney explicitly using licensing-fee price as market segmentation because it "no longer needs to build brand image".
So pricing an IP's terms by its lifecycle status is documented market behavior at both ends, not an invention.
The qualifier: every verified practitioner of deliberate free-for-exposure licensing is an INSTITUTIONAL IP owner (a prefectural government, a software company, the region's top illustrator agency, a civic character operator).
No independent creator was found saying first-person "I license free/cheap deliberately to grow", and no creator was found calling free licensing exploitation either.
The exploitation framing exists, loudly, but it lives one door over: in the commission/約稿 market's exposure-as-payment discourse, where the creator-community consensus is 曝光不等於收入 (exposure does not equal income).
Framing tally on the licensing instances actually found: growth/strategy framing 4, goodwill framing 1, exploitation framing 0.
Implication for the founder's idea: "marketing licensing" as a deliberate lifecycle stage has strong institutional precedent, but a product offering it to independent creators must convert the exposure into something bankable (performance data, a track record that later justifies fees) or it will collide with the creator community's deep, justified skepticism of being paid in exposure.

## Part A: deliberate free licensing as growth strategy, at institutional scale

### 1. 熊本熊 (Kumamon): free licensing as founding strategy, then a fee tier once proven (ja industry via zh-CN trade media, third-party, NEW)

- **URL:** https://www.jiemian.com/article/1873717.html (界面新闻 JMedia, 2018-01-11, 《熊本熊年收74亿元，它是如何打造IP人设和营销的？》, syndicated from 三文娱 - trade-media voice relaying official prefecture policy, no first-person official quote).
- **Quote 1 (the policy and its stated purpose):** 「熊本县为了扩大宣传，对于"熊本熊"在日本境内的一切商业授权，均采取免费政策。只要获得县政府的许可，就可以免费获得熊本熊的商用权限。」
  (Gloss: to expand publicity, Kumamoto Prefecture made ALL domestic commercial licensing of Kumamon free; a government permit is the only gate.)
- **Quote 2 (the scale it built):** 「在IP经济当道的现如今，不收授权费的熊本熊每年能带来超1000亿日元（约58亿元）的营收。」
  (Gloss: charging no licensing fee, Kumamon still drives over 100 billion yen of annual revenue.)
- **Quote 3 (the tier switch):** 「2018年1月8日，熊本熊版权新规正式实施。新规中，海外企业有权独立参与"熊本熊"的IP授权申请。申请成功后，支出5%~7%的版权费即可使用。」 with the article adding the motive: 「县政府决定开放海外授权。增加收入的同时，也扩大熊本品牌的世界影响力。」
  (Gloss: from 2018-01-08 overseas companies may apply directly and pay 5-7% of retail price; the government's stated motive is revenue plus wider global influence.)
- **Verification:** all strings curl-verified on raw HTML.
- **Reading:** the cleanest existence proof for "marketing licensing" as a deliberate stage.
  The IP owner explicitly traded licensing fees for distribution while building the asset, and introduced a fee tier only where and when the IP was already proven.
  Flags: third-party trade media, government-owned IP, and the free tier is geographic (domestic) as much as temporal, so it is a strategy precedent rather than a creator-preference instance.

### 2. 初音未来 / Crypton's piapro license: a standing free tier for non-commercial use, consent-gated commercial tier (zh-CN law-firm analysis, third-party, NEW)

- **URL:** https://www.zhonglun.com/research/articles/9280.html (中伦律师事务所, 2022-09-23, 《虚拟偶像背后的著作权》, authors 葛燕、王飞 - legal analysis, third-party).
- **Quote:** 「"初音未来"所属公司Crypton Future Media（"Crypton"）为解决"初音未来"二次创作产生的版权问题设立了一个创作分享网络平台piapro，piapro的角色授权协议约定，用户可以在不以营利为目的且未获得报酬的情况下免费使用角色进行二次创作，使用范围包括在社交媒体发布、免费展示或分发周边衍生品、参加角色扮演活动等。」
  (Gloss: Crypton's piapro character license lets anyone use the character free for non-profit second creation, including posting, free display, distributing derivative goods, and cosplay.)
- **Supporting detail (same page, curl-verified context):** commercial publication of derivative works requires 「取得其他方的书面同意」 (written consent of the other rights-holding parties), via the China agent 上海新创华's poppro agreement.
- **Verification:** curl-verified on raw HTML.
- **Reading:** the tier here is by USE rather than by maturity: free where the use grows the IP (fan creation, visibility), gated and monetized where the use extracts from it (commerce).
  This is the standing-policy version of what Kumamon did sequentially, and it is credited with building one of the largest fan-creation economies in the world.
  Flag: third-party legal description of the license terms, not a Crypton first-person strategy statement.

### 3. 幾米 / 墨色國際: the region's top illustrator agency licensing free for goodwill (zh-TW, agency-principal relayed, REUSED)

- **URL:** https://www.chinatimes.com/newspapers/20101226000736-260301 (旺報 2010-12-26, curl-verified in reframe-7 instance 5).
- **Quote:** 「免費授權她們使用幾米的圖」 - 墨色國際 licensed 幾米's images to the 喜憨兒基金會 (a charity employing people with intellectual disabilities) for their moon-cake gift boxes in 2001, free.
- **Reading:** even the most fee-disciplined, category-exclusive licensing operation in Taiwan (see reframe-7 on 墨色's one-client-per-category rule) runs a zero-fee tier when the payment is goodwill and public warmth.
  Framing in the source is goodwill/公益, not growth tactics - but note the year: 2001 is 墨色's founding year, when 幾米 the licensing brand was itself new.

### 4. 麻吉貓 and the Taiwan-Korea expo circuit: collaboration denominated in exposure, stated by operators (zh-TW, creator-side operator first-person, NEW)

- **URL:** https://www.openbook.org.tw/article/p-69611 (Openbook閱讀誌, 2024-09-23, 《原創角色國際授權II》國家隊集合！前進日韓授權展難點與突破》, interviewing 樂容文創 黃志平 (麻吉貓) and 目子拾參 李芹羽 (godpod.)).
- **Quote 1 (the convening model, article narration):** 「麻吉貓的經營模式，是以圖像角色為號召，共同嘉惠給不同單位，包括政府機構、公益組織與民間企業。」
  (Gloss: the Machi-cat model uses the character as a rallying banner that benefits government agencies, charities, and private companies together.)
- **Quote 2 (first-person, 黃志平):** 「如果不是以『麻吉貓』為號召，不可能有這麼多不同廠商想加入！」 and 「麻吉貓有沒有可能像國外大IP的流量？可能沒辦法；但是要號召企業、政府組織來做好的推廣跟行銷，我們都叫得動」
  (Gloss: without the character as the banner, these vendors would never join; we cannot match foreign mega-IP traffic, but we can summon companies and government bodies for good promotion.)
- **Quote 3 (exposure-swap framing on the international side, article relaying Korean operators):** 「對於韓國的角色業者而言，儘管台灣市場有限......不一定要找到代理商，就可以進行品牌聯名或合作，相互曝光。」
  (Gloss: for Korean character businesses, collabs with Taiwan can proceed without an agent, as mutual exposure.)
- **Verification:** all strings curl-verified on raw HTML.
- **Reading:** a working small-IP operator whose model is explicitly convening-for-mutual-benefit rather than fee-maximization, including charity tie-ins (MRT events selling 喜憨兒 cookies).
  相互曝光 (mutual exposure) appears here as a legitimate, named deal currency between character businesses.
  This is the closest thing found to a creator-side first-person endorsement of exposure-denominated licensing, though the fee terms of specific deals are not disclosed.

## Part B: the pricing menu - near-free entry terms and the proven-IP end

### 5. 零保底版权金 (zero-minimum pure revenue share) is a standard menu item (zh-CN, vendor voice, NEW)

- **URL:** https://www.zcool.com.cn/article/ZMTU3MTU5Mg==.html (站酷, 原仓数据 - a licensing-data vendor - c. 2023, 《IP授权费用是多少?》).
- **Quote:** 「IP商品的授权模式，IP商品授权模式一般有买断制、保底版权金+固定分成、零保底版权金（或称之为纯分成模式）。」
  (Gloss: the standard licensing fee menu is buyout, minimum guarantee plus fixed share, and zero-minimum pure revenue share.)
- **Verification:** curl-verified on raw HTML.
  Flag: VENDOR voice (licensing-data company content marketing).
- **Reading:** near-free ENTRY is institutionalized in the fee menu itself.
  A zero-guarantee pure-share deal costs the brand nothing up front, which is exactly the structure an unproven IP can offer to get on shelves - the cash-flow shape of "marketing licensing" without the name.
  The source does not say which IPs get which terms, so this supports feasibility, not practice.

### 6. The proven-IP end: Disney prices by fee because it no longer needs exposure (zh-TW, trade magazine, REUSED)

- **URL:** https://www.ieatpe.org.tw/magazine/ebook347/storypage03.html (貿易雜誌 347期, 2020, curl-verified in reframe-4 instance 4).
- **Quote:** 「國際知名IP大廠如迪士尼已不需特別塑造品牌形象，而是著重於大而穩定的客戶，以授權金價格做出市場區隔」
  (Gloss: famous IP houses like Disney no longer need to build brand image; they focus on large stable clients and use licensing-fee price as market segmentation.)
- **Reading:** the tier logic stated from the top end down.
  If proven IPs charge fees BECAUSE they no longer need exposure, the corollary the sub-question proposes - unproven IPs conceding fees because they DO need exposure - is the same curve read from the other end.
  The 王永昌 case in the same article (NT$15M break-even on Disney terms) shows the fee end actively prices small buyers out, which is the market gap a free/cheap tier for new IPs would fill.

## Part C: the exploitation frame - real, but living in the adjacent market

### 7. The creator community's standing rule: 曝光不等於收入 (zh-TW, creator-community voice, NEW)

- **URL:** https://vocus.cc/article/691b3cb7fd89780001bd01d6 (方格子, 楓色備忘錄, 2025-12-09, 《繪師平台讓作品變現不再難：從零開始的插畫營運指南》 - advice essay addressed to illustrators by a creator-community author).
- **Quote 1 (section heading and body):** 「1. 曝光不等於收入 IG、Twitter 可以累積粉絲，但要靠它們直接賺錢很難。平台演算法不穩定，單純等「爆圖」根本不是長期策略。」
  (Gloss: exposure does not equal income; you can build followers on IG/Twitter but hardly monetize them directly, and waiting for a viral hit is not a long-term strategy.)
- **Quote 2 (the prescribed role of exposure):** 「利用「曝光平台」導流，而非在上面期待收入」
  (Gloss: use exposure platforms to route traffic to your monetization entry points, never expect income from exposure itself.)
- **Verification:** curl-verified on raw HTML.
- **Reading:** this is about social platforms rather than licensing deals, but it is the clearest statement of the creator community's default accounting: exposure is an input to be converted, not a payment to be accepted.
  Any "license free for exposure" pitch to independent creators lands inside this frame and must answer it.
- **Unverified context, flagged:** zh-CN commission-market discourse is full of exposure-as-payment resentment (借试稿之名白嫖 - using trial drafts to get free work - appears across Zhihu/Weibo commission-dispute threads surfaced in search).
  No specific verbatim instance could be verified on a fetchable page this iteration, so this is recorded as context, not evidence.

## Answer to the brief's test

Free-for-exposure licensing exists in the wild and is framed as growth strategy by everyone found practicing it - but the practitioners found are institutions (a prefecture, a software company, a top agency, a civic-character operator), and the framing tally (growth 4, goodwill 1, exploitation 0 on licensing instances; exploitation dominant in the adjacent commission market) is therefore a tally of institutional voices.
Tiered strategy by IP maturity has direct precedent (Kumamon's free-then-fee lifecycle, Miku's free-noncommercial/paid-commercial split, Disney's fee-as-segmentation at the proven end, zero-minimum share terms at the entry end), so the founder's "overall strategy per IP based on its status" matches observable market behavior.
What the record does NOT contain is anyone running this as a managed offer across a portfolio of independent small IPs - that part would be new, and its main go-to-market risk is the creator community's settled rule that exposure is not income, which the founder's version answers only if the free stage provably banks something (performance data, a fee-justifying track record) the creator can later charge against.
