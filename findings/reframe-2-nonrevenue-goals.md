# Reframe sub-question 2: COUNTER-EVIDENCE - brands doing IP collabs deliberately for NON-revenue goals

**Hypothesis under attack (H1, brand side):** brands judge IP collaborations on ONE question - "will this make us money?"
This file hunts the opposite: brands that state image refresh, audience rejuvenation, or cultural cachet as the deliberate goal.
If such brands are plentiful, H1 is segment-specific, not universal.

**Compiled:** 2026-07-17 (stress-test run, iteration 3).
Every quote below was verified verbatim against the raw page source (curl grep) unless flagged otherwise; AI page summaries alone were not trusted.

**Verdict: WEAKENS H1 as a universal claim - non-revenue-goal collabs are plentiful, but they cluster in ONE segment: famous or heritage brands.**
Three new brand-side first-person voices (大白兔, 茅台, 旺旺) plus three already on file from sub-question 1 (名创优品, 清心福全, 合作金庫) all state rejuvenation/image/mindshare as the deliberate goal, and every one of them is a household-name brand.
No little-known brand was found stating a non-revenue collab goal.
Counter-nuance, honestly logged: the purest non-revenue bet found (茅台冰淇淋) was strategically retreated within three years of its sponsor's departure, suggesting awareness-goal collabs survive only while a senior sponsor protects them - the money question returns eventually.

## Implication for the problem statement

Non-revenue collab goals are not edge cases: China's most collab-famous heritage brands state them openly, sometimes as the SOLE goal (大白兔) and sometimes explicitly above commercial logic (茅台's 战略级产品).
So H1 is segment-specific, and the segment boundary looks like brand fame: every non-revenue voice found is a household name, while the revenue voices on file (台灣高鐵's NT$100M target, our KFT interview) are operators for whom the collab itself must pay.
This hands sub-question 4 a concrete hypothesis to test: fame (or the lack of it) predicts whether a brand buys conversion or buys perception.
The 茅台冰淇淋 retreat adds the sharpest wrinkle: even a chairman-sponsored awareness bet got unwound when the sponsor left, which means non-revenue goals still need an internal defense - and that defense today is a person, not a measurement system.
That is the wedge our product story should aim at, and it is sub-question 3's hunt.
