# Reframe sub-question 6: WHY CREATORS SIGN WITH AGENCIES - and where agencies fail them

**The question (from the stress-test brief):** hunt creator/illustrator/character-IP-owner voices on why they use agencies AND what they complain about.
Rank which complaint leads: PERFORMANCE (no deals closed), TRANSPARENCY (cannot see process/terms/who was approached), or ECONOMICS (fee splits, margins).
Also tally: agencies framed as necessary evil, growth partner, or interchangeable, and note side-hustle vs full-time splits where sources say.

**Compiled:** 2026-07-18 (iteration 9 of the reframe run).
Every NEW quote below was verified verbatim against page source via curl grep on raw HTML unless flagged otherwise.

**Verdict: SUPPORTS H2's outsourcing premise strongly, with a complaint ranking that differs from the expected one.**
Every first-person "why I signed" statement found says the same thing: the creator wants to hand the business layer to someone else and keep drawing (Cherng says it twice, five years apart; the US-agency insider account and even the MCN sales pitch use the identical phrase 專心創作/专心创作).
But the leading VERIFIED complaint in the character-IP record is not performance and not transparency - it is ECONOMICS IN THE NARROW SENSE OF PAYMENT INTEGRITY: the agency collected or owed money and did not pass it on time or at all.
Explicit PERFORMANCE complaints (signed, then no deals came) are documented at scale only in the adjacent MCN/influencer industry; for character-IP creators our own Jolly Gee interview remains the only first-person instance on file.
TRANSPARENCY almost never appears as a stated demand; it appears as the ENABLING CONDITION of the other two failures - creators discover the payment or deal-flow problem late precisely because they cannot see into the agency (Cherng learned from missing pay, MCN creators from fabricated deal excuses).
That pattern - opacity complained about only after a money or performance failure surfaces - matches the "visibility demands appear AFTER being burned" complication from the n=1 interview and feeds directly into sub-question 7.

## Why creators sign: the hands-off rationale, stated first-person

### 1. Cherng (馬來貘), 2014 statement: I signed so I could just draw (zh-TW, first-person, character-IP creator)

- **URL:** https://www.ettoday.net/news/20141230/445254.htm (ETtoday 2014-12-30, 《馬來貘現身回應「倒閉說」 「誰來體恤我們插畫家？」》, full statement reproduced)
- **Who:** Cherng 楊承霖, creator of 馬來貘/LAIMO, then Taiwan's hottest character IP - CREATOR-side first-person statement.
- **Quote (why he signed):** 「我一直是一個單純喜歡畫畫的人，也只想好好安心畫圖跟大家分享，然後持續做著自己喜歡的事。也因為這樣我選擇把經紀事務交由給默默來處理」
  (Gloss: I have always just been someone who loves drawing and only wants to draw in peace and keep doing what I love - and BECAUSE of that I chose to hand all agency business to 默默文創.)
- **Verification:** curl grep on raw HTML returned the exact string; cross-verified on 中時 https://www.chinatimes.com/realtimenews/20141230003726-260404.
- **Reading:** the cleanest possible first-person statement of H2's premise, from the most commercially successful character-IP creator in the market at the time.
  Wanting to stay uninvolved is stated as the REASON for signing, not a regrettable side effect.

### 2. Cherng again, 2024 華研 departure: nine years of protection let me draw in peace (zh-TW, first-person)

- **URL:** https://ent.ltn.com.tw/news/breakingnews/4540010 (自由娛樂 2024-01-02, 《不只江宏傑出走 插畫家馬來貘也宣告「分手」》)
- **Who:** Cherng, announcing his exit from 華研國際 after nine years - CREATOR-side first-person statement.
- **Quote:** 「都因為有公司的照顧與保護，我才能安心做我想做的事，畫我想畫的圖」 and 「覺得自己已經有足夠的力量去面對更多不同的挑戰」
  (Gloss: it was the company's care and protection that let me do what I want and draw what I want in peace; now I feel I have grown strong enough to face new challenges on my own.)
- **Verification:** curl grep on raw HTML returned both strings.
- **Reading:** ten years after being burned by one agency, Cherng re-signed with another and describes the SAME hands-off arrangement as the thing he valued - being burned did not convert him to wanting operational involvement.
  Note the exit logic: he leaves not because the model failed but because he outgrew the need - the "graduation" dynamic the is-law lawyer piece (instance 7) describes as the normal life cycle of agency relationships.

### 3. 黃昱銘 via FLiPER: what US illustration agencies actually sell their creators (first-person industry-insider essay, zh-TW)

- **URL:** https://flipermag.com/2019/02/18/management-company/ (FLiPER 2019-02-18, 《自身經驗帶你了解東西方差異－關於北美插畫經紀公司》)
- **Who:** 黃昱銘, Taiwanese illustrator who interned and worked inside two large US illustration agencies - CREATOR-side first-person (also agency-insider) essay.
- **Quote 1 (who needs an agent):** 「主要是幫助因為時間繁忙，沒有心思經營或是不擅於宣傳自己作品的優質插畫家們，能夠有機會接觸到更多類型的工作」
  (Gloss: agencies mainly help good illustrators who are too busy, have no mind for business, or are bad at self-promotion get access to more kinds of work.)
- **Quote 2 (agency as the buyer's quality signal):** 「當大型廣告或是品牌公司有插畫需求時，因為不想擔憂啟用到程度不如預期的插畫師，就會選擇與這些經紀公司進行合作」
  (Gloss: when big advertisers or brand companies need illustration, they go through agencies because they do not want to risk hiring an illustrator who under-delivers.)
- **Quote 3 (the hands-off deal):** 「經紀人( Agent )會負責接案的詳談與尋找客戶，從合約價碼到收費的所有商業細節一手包辦，並成為插畫家和客戶之間溝通的橋樑。...基本上插畫家們不會遇到任何麻煩，能夠好好的專心的創作」, with commissions 「抽成都是固定在三成 ( 30% )」
  (Gloss: the agent handles every commercial detail from contract price to collection, acting as the bridge to the client; the illustrator basically hits no trouble and can concentrate purely on creating; the US/UK cut is fixed at 30%.)
- **Verification:** all three strings curl-verified in raw HTML.
- **Reading:** supports H2 and adds a mechanism our interviews missed: the agency is not only the creator's outsourcer, it is the BUYER's de-risking device (a quality signal), which is why agented illustrators get the big-brand work.
  Also documents the mature-market price floor: a fixed, non-negotiable 30/70 split framed as fair by the creator-side author - economics complaints are not intrinsic to the model.

- **URL:** https://amma.artron.net/observation_shownews.php?newid=1117295 (雅昌 AMMA repost 2022-12-16, 《对话泡泡玛特签约艺术家熊喵》, 来源:泡泡玛特)
- **Who:** 熊喵 (SKULLPANDA's artist) and 周琳 (POP MART's IP operations lead) - source is POP MART itself, so VENDOR-PUBLISHED; treat as the marketed ideal, not independent evidence.
- **Quote (周琳):** 「“尊重艺术家、保护艺术家的创造力”是一切的前提与基础，IP运营只是扮演一个“领路人、翻译者”的角色，将艺术家的创意更好地传达给受众。」
  (Gloss: respecting and protecting the artist's creativity is the premise of everything; IP operations only plays the role of guide and translator, conveying the artist's creativity to the audience.)
- **Verification:** curl grep on raw HTML returned the string.
- **Reading:** the most successful artist-IP machine in the zh-CN market MARKETS itself on exactly the H2 division of labor: artist owns the 0-to-1 creative act, company owns the 1-to-100 commercialization.
  Weak as evidence of creator preference (the creator's employer published it), but strong as evidence of what the market believes creators want to hear.

## Where agencies fail creators: the complaint record

### 5. Cherng vs 默默文創, 2014: the agency chronically failed to pay through (zh-TW, first-person - the strongest complaint instance on file)

- **URL:** https://www.ettoday.net/news/20141230/445254.htm and https://www.chinatimes.com/realtimenews/20141230003726-260404
- **Who:** Cherng, first-person statement; 默默文創's own collapse announcement quoted in the same coverage - both sides on record.
- **Quote 1 (the complaint):** 「只是公司慣性地積欠插畫家們的款項，他們總是用共體時艱來請插畫家體恤公司的困難，不過我們插畫家等的就是那三個月一結的薪水，若這筆錢沒拿到生活可能也會有問題，那誰來體恤插畫家。為什麼我們努力準時交稿，但卻不能拿到準時的錢？（我去年LINE第一季貼圖的錢我現在一毛都還沒拿到）」
  (Gloss: the company habitually owed its illustrators money, always invoking shared hardship; we illustrators live on that quarterly payout, so if it does not arrive our livelihoods are at risk - who shows consideration for us? Why do we deliver on time but never get paid on time? I have not seen one cent of my LINE sticker revenue from Q1 last year.)
- **Quote 2 (accountability line):** 「公司經營賺錢盈虧與否是經營者的責任，而不該是我們這些被經紀的插畫家該出來承擔的。」
  (Gloss: whether the company profits or loses is the operator's responsibility - it should not fall on us, the represented illustrators, to bear it.)
- **Quote 3 (agency's own admission, from 默默's statement via 中時):** 「公司快速成長展店、不諳經營管理、盲目擴充人員，以致該付給插畫家的費用一再延宕」
  (Gloss: the company expanded stores too fast, did not understand management, blindly over-hired, so payments owed to illustrators were repeatedly delayed.)
- **Verification:** all strings curl-verified on both pages; 中時 additionally records the split - 默默 called Cherng 「一個分潤7成、季領百萬的年輕人」 (a young man on a 70% split drawing a million NT per quarter).
- **Reading:** the defining Taiwan character-IP agency failure case, and it is a PAYMENT-INTEGRITY failure, not a deal-scarcity failure - deals existed and money flowed IN (LINE stickers, licensing), it just never flowed OUT to the creator on time.
  On a 70/30 split the ECONOMICS complaint here is not about the margin percentage but about whether the agency is a trustworthy pipe for the creator's own money.
  Transparency enters only implicitly: Cherng had no forward view of the agency's finances and found out through missing pay - which is exactly the failure a package-tracking-style money/deal status view would have surfaced early.

### 6. The 2024 fan-art middleman revolt: low splits and risk dumping (zh-CN, relayed first-person - flagged)

- **URL:** https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20240514A0ARHH00 (腾讯新闻, 情报姬 2024-05-14, 《一只小小的同人手机壳，引爆了新一轮"反抗资本"的二次元绘圈世界大战。》)
- **Who:** media account relaying a since-DELETED first-person Weibo by the artist - RELAYED first-person; flag: the original post is unrecoverable.
- **Quote 1 (the complaint):** 「由于画师不满其愈发不尊重画师的低劣排版设计和较低的分成，在微博上【避雷】该【手机壳商家】的事情」
  (Gloss: the artist, unhappy with the merchant's increasingly disrespectful shoddy layouts and the low revenue split, posted a warning-off about the phone-case merchant on Weibo.)
- **Quote 2 (the article's own analysis):** 「第一是商家给出的报酬和画师所承担的风险完全不匹配，第二则是定价权的归属问题。」
  (Gloss: first, the pay offered totally mismatches the legal risk the artist bears; second, who holds pricing power.)
- **Verification:** both strings curl-verified in raw HTML.
- **Reading:** at the informal bottom of the market (fan-art merch middlemen), the complaint is again ECONOMICS - split size, pricing power, and risk allocation - not a demand for process visibility or more involvement.
  Adjacent to, not identical with, character-IP agenting: the "middleman" here is a merch producer, not a rights agent, and the licensed rights themselves were legally grey.

### 7. 賴文智 (is-law): the 資源/專業 taxonomy of why creators sign, and why breakups are structural (zh-TW, third-party expert)

- **URL:** https://www.is-law.com/4-1472/ (益思科技法律事務所, 賴文智律師, 2020-12-24, 《為什麼需要找經紀人（公司）？》)
- **Who:** the IP-contract lawyer who literally wrote the book on 經紀合約 (《當文創遇上法律：讀懂經紀合約書》) - THIRD-PARTY expert, not a creator voice.
- **Quote 1 (the taxonomy):** 「尋找經紀人當然不是為了追求流行，而是滿足自身發展的「需求」，這樣的需求大概可以分成二類，一個是「資源」，另外一個則是「專業」。」
  (Gloss: creators seek agents to satisfy development needs of two kinds - RESOURCES [access, deal flow] and PROFESSIONAL CAPABILITY [negotiation, contracts, execution].)
- **Quote 2 (illustrator-specific):** 「有些插畫家與經紀公司合作，是看重該經紀公司旗下有不少插畫家，有機會一起向出版社推介而爭取到繪製插畫的機會」
  (Gloss: some illustrators sign because the agency's roster gets pitched collectively to publishers, winning work they could not win alone.)
- **Quote 3 (the structural conflict, from the companion piece teaser on the same page):** 「理論上經紀人應該與被經紀的對象具有一致、共同的利益，但實際上，經紀人（公司）與被經紀的演藝人員或創作者就是不同的主體，必然會有不同的利益考量」
  (Gloss: in theory agent and client share aligned interests, but in reality they are different entities and will necessarily weigh interests differently.)
- **Verification:** all strings curl-verified in raw HTML.
- **Reading:** gives the clean vocabulary for the "why sign" side (resources + professional capability, both of which are OUTCOME inputs, neither of which is involvement), states that principal-agent misalignment is inherent, and frames departure as normal once the agency's 資源/專業 stop exceeding what the creator can do alone - which is exactly how Cherng narrated his 華研 exit.

### 8. The MCN mirror: "just create, we handle everything" as a sales pitch, then unfulfilled deal flow and fabricated excuses (zh-CN, adjacent industry)

- **URL:** https://www.jiemian.com/article/4630435.html (界面新闻 2020, 《【深度】MCN行业爆发的烦恼：一批网红正忙着和机构解约》)
- **Who:** 界面 reporting with named first-person sources: MCN operator 侃烃, vlogger 林晨 (林晨同学), agency partner 林冬冬, lawyer 张逸瑞 - ADJACENT INDUSTRY (influencer MCN, not character IP); weight accordingly.
- **Quote 1 (the pitch is H2's promise):** 「一些MCN为了吸引更多红人，与其早期接触时会给出以下承诺：公司手握许多商业资源，签约后不愁商单；公司和平台关系好，博主能获得流量倾斜；公司有专门的运营团队，博主只须专心创作等等。问题在于，一些MCN其实无法完成以上承诺，也不会把这些内容写进合同。」
  (Gloss: to attract creators, MCNs promise abundant commercial resources so deals will never be scarce, platform favor, and a dedicated ops team so the creator "only needs to focus on creating" - the problem is some MCNs cannot deliver these promises and never put them in the contract.)
- **Quote 2 (opacity as the cover for non-performance):** 「MCN甩锅商业资源的一种方法是给博主提供很差的商单，博主拒绝合作时，机构就能把责任推到博主身上。另一种方法是虚构一个不存在的商单，博主和对方谈合作时，机构就谎称对接的人弄错了或者已经离职了。」
  (Gloss: one dodge is offering deliberately bad deals so refusal can be blamed on the creator; another is inventing a fictitious deal, then claiming the contact person was wrong or had left.)
- **Quote 3 (an MCN operator's first-person concession):** 「“所以我和博主签约时都说我们机构不保商单，因为这件事可操作空间太大了。”侃烃说。」
  (Gloss: "so when I sign creators I always say we do not guarantee deals, because there is too much room for manipulation in this," says Kanting.)
- **Quote 4 (one-sided economics, 林晨's contract):** 「他表示所签合同中甲方权利有17项，乙方权利0项，甲方义务5项，乙方义务15项；收入由客户决定，没有保底收入」
  (Gloss: his contract gave the agency 17 rights and him 0, him 15 obligations and the agency 5; income was client-determined with no floor.)
- **Verification:** all strings curl-verified in raw HTML.
- **Reading:** the adjacent industry supplies what the character-IP record lacks: PERFORMANCE failure (promised deal flow that never arrives) documented at scale, plus the exact mechanism linking it to opacity - a creator who cannot see the real deal pipeline cannot distinguish "no deals exist" from "the agency is not trying" from "the agency is lying," so the non-performance festers until exit.
  This is the strongest adjacent-industry support on file for the package-tracking hypothesis: what would have protected these creators is not veto power but a truthful view of what the agency is actually doing.
  Caveat: MCN contracts bind the creator's whole persona and output, far more coercive than a character-IP licensing agency mandate; transfer with care.

## The rankings the brief asked for

**Which complaint leads (in the verified record):**
1. **ECONOMICS - but specifically payment integrity, not split percentage.** Both verified character-IP-adjacent complaint cases (Cherng instance 5, fan-artist instance 6) are about money owed or split unfairly, and in the flagship Taiwan case the split itself (70/30 creator-favorable) was never the grievance - late and missing payouts were.
2. **PERFORMANCE - real but under-documented in character IP.** Loudly documented in the adjacent MCN industry (instance 8) and present in our Jolly Gee interview (zero deals closed under an exclusive), but zero public character-IP first-person instances were found under the queries listed below - so H2's "prove the agency is any good" framing rests on adjacent-industry evidence plus n=1.
3. **TRANSPARENCY - almost never the stated complaint, always the enabling condition.** No source demands process visibility up front; opacity is what allows payment failures (instance 5) and performance failures (instance 8) to stay hidden until they become exits.
   This ordering supports the founder's refinement: creators do not ask for involvement, and they do not even ask for visibility until a failure surfaces - but every documented failure would have surfaced earlier with package-tracking-level visibility of deals and money.

**Framing tally (necessary evil / growth partner / interchangeable):**
- GROWTH PARTNER: Cherng on 華研 (instance 2, explicit), FLiPER (instance 3 - having an agent is 「聲望與實力的表徵」, a mark of prestige and capability), POP MART (instance 4, vendor-claimed), is-law's mutual-need framing (instance 7).
- NECESSARY EVIL or worse: the MCN record (instance 8), the fan-art middleman case (instance 6), and 默默文創 post-collapse (instance 5).
- INTERCHANGEABLE: not found anywhere - creators in this record treat the specific agency relationship as high-stakes and identity-adjacent, not commodity.
- Net: the same creator can hold both framings sequentially (Cherng: burned by 默默, then nine grateful years at 華研), so the split tracks AGENCY QUALITY, not creator ideology - consistent with H2's "they just want proof the agency is any good."

**Side-hustle vs full-time:** the record barely speaks to it.
The one direct signal is Cherng in 2014: 「我們插畫家等的就是那三個月一結的薪水，若這筆錢沒拿到生活可能也會有問題」 - full-time dependence on agency-mediated income is precisely what made the payment failure existential.
No source in this iteration states a side-hustler's view; not-found under the queries below.
