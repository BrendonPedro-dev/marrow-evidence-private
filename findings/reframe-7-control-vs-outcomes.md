# Reframe sub-question 7: CONTROL vs OUTCOMES - the Marrow design-philosophy test

**The question (from the stress-test brief):** do creators anywhere express wanting MORE involvement/control in the business process, or is hands-off-but-paid the dominant stated preference?
Distinguish CONTROL (I decide) from VISIBILITY (I can see) from APPROVAL (I can veto), and whether creators ask before or only after being burned.
Test the founder's package-tracking hypothesis specifically, including adjacent-industry precedent (royalty dashboards, freelancer platforms), and whether anyone complains that tracking alone is not enough.
Also test whether creator time-on-business complaints attach to the licensing slice or to running their company generally.

**Compiled:** 2026-07-18 (iteration 10 of the reframe run).
Every NEW quote below was verified verbatim against page source via curl grep on raw HTML unless flagged otherwise.
Instances 1-4 reuse citations already verified in findings/reframe-6-agency-relationship.md.

**Verdict: WEAKENS Marrow's original more-creator-agency philosophy, and SUPPORTS the third position (outcomes + package-tracking visibility) - with one important amendment about what happens after severe burns.**
Across every first-person creator voice on file, hands-off-but-paid is the stated preference both before AND after being burned (Cherng re-signed a hands-off deal after 默默文創 failed to pay him).
Not one source in any language searched asks an agency for deal-approval workflow, negotiation seats, or partner-choice rights - the CONTROL Marrow's original philosophy would grant is demanded by nobody in the record.
VISIBILITY is demanded loudly, but with two qualifiers: it is always about money flows and deal existence (not creative or strategic process), and it always appears AFTER a failure surfaces (Spotify boycotts, 鄧紫棋's six missing years, Cherng's missing payouts, Jolly Gee's zero-deal exclusive).
The escalation path after a severe burn is not "give me more involvement inside the relationship" - it is OWNERSHIP or EXIT: 鄧紫棋 re-recorded her masters, Cherng went independent, and 幾米 pre-empted the problem by co-founding his own brand company.
The package-tracking hypothesis gets the strongest adjacent-industry support found so far: the illustration commission market's dominant trust product is literally stage-gated progress visibility plus escrow (米画师), LINE sticker creators voluntarily and happily analyze their status dashboards, and the music industry's decade-long fight is a demand for 金流透明公開 - but the same industry also supplies the caution: Spotify publishes a transparency report (Loud & Clear) and distrust persists, because the opacity lives in the label-contract layer the report does not cover.
Lesson for Marrow: tracking creates trust only if it covers the whole money-and-deal path the creator cares about; partial-path transparency gets discounted as PR.

## Part C: the VISIBILITY record - demanded loudly, but only over money, and only after failures

  (Gloss: musicians say the huge royalty payments just circulate inside a "money-flow maze" agreed among the giants, and artists still cannot access their fair share.)
- **Quote 3 (why the tracking artifact fails):** 「支付給權利持有者的款項，是根據音樂人和唱片公司之間的個別合約來分配的，Spotify 並沒辦法直接掌控最終款項的流向。」

## Answering the brief's three-way distinction

**CONTROL (I decide):** demanded by nobody in the record as a feature of an agency relationship.
It appears only as OWNERSHIP at the top tier: 幾米 co-founded his own brand company (instance 5), 鄧紫棋 re-recorded to own her masters (instance 4), Cherng left to go independent once strong enough (instance 1).
The middle position - a represented creator asking for a decision seat inside someone else's agency - was not found under any query tried.

**VISIBILITY (I can see):** the demanded artifact, but specifically over MONEY FLOWS and DEAL EXISTENCE (金流透明公開, instance 6; payment status, instances 1/4; real-vs-fabricated deals, instance 7), never over creative or strategic process.
Timing: every documented demand is post-failure; no pre-burn visibility demand was found.
Where the interface simply exists (LINE, instance 9), creators use it avidly without ever having demanded it - suggesting the absence of pre-burn demands reflects unimagined supply, not absent utility.

**APPROVAL (I can veto):** not found as a first-person creator demand toward agencies.
Veto-like practice exists at top-tier IP (幾米's one-client-per-category rule, instance 5) but is exercised by the creator's own manager as brand strategy, not demanded by a represented creator from an external agency.
Jolly Gee's "stay involved in important conversations" (instance 8) is the record's only approval-adjacent voice, and it is post-burn and scoped.

**Before or after being burned:** the pattern from the n=1 complication GENERALIZES for visibility (all public visibility demands follow failures) but NOT for control (Cherng, the cleanest before/after case, re-chose hands-off after being burned).

**Time-on-business attach point:** the record leans toward "running the business generally": FLiPER's who-needs-an-agent framing (時間繁忙，沒有心思經營或是不擅於宣傳, instance 2) and is-law's 資源/專業 taxonomy (reframe-6 instance 7) both describe general commercial workload, of which licensing is one outsourceable slice.
No source was found that itemizes a creator's hours between licensing admin and general company admin; the Jolly Gee ambiguity stands unresolved.
