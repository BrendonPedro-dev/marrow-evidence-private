### 1.1 What the base actually is

CONFIRMED (census): 146 rows, classed 94 PORTFOLIO / 51 INDEPENDENT / 1 UNCLEAR, with 26 of the 51 independent rows marked REPRESENTABLE=yes.
CONFIRMED (census `_VERDICT.md` section 5): this is a **sample census, not an exhaustive count**, with recall bias toward campaigns that got press.

Three things in this list are worth stating plainly, all ASSUMED as readings of the confirmed rows:

**(a) The addressable pattern is the individual illustrator, not the character-property company.**
20 of the 26 rows are a brand pairing with a single named individual artist.
Four more (19, 52, 77, 140) are named rosters of individual artists assembled by the brand.
That leaves exactly two rows where the addressable holder is a company rather than a person: 8 (株式会社エール Yell Inc, a goods-planning company with its own catalogue) and 50 (ドズル社, a creator-owned company).

**(b) Recurring brand-side creator programmes exist and are the most PBC-shaped format found.**
CONFIRMED (census rows 140, 141, 142 and their brand-official sources): Pixio was on its 2nd instalment with four named freelance illustrators, CyberAgent's Pigg Party on its 3rd with 寺田てら, Superbag on its 4th with mame.
CONFIRMED (census rows 21, 77): graniph appears twice, once with a single named illustrator on his 5th graniph collaboration and once with a 12-illustrator roster.
ASSUMED: a brand that has already run a repeating creator programme has an internal process for contracting an individual artist, which is a materially different conversation from persuading a brand to do it for the first time.

**(c) Multi-artist roster formats are a real and repeated Japanese format.**
CONFIRMED: rows 19 (Loft, 18 illustrators), 52 (Don Quijote, 3 illustrators), 77 (graniph, 12 illustrators), 140 (Pixio, 4 illustrators).
ASSUMED: a roster slot is a different ask from a solo campaign and the census shows Japanese brands assembling these rosters themselves.

### 1.7 Channels

Two different senses of "channel" matter and they should not be blurred.

**(a) Retail and venue channels where addressable independents actually appear.**
CONFIRMED (census, from the REP=yes rows): lifestyle and variety retail (Loft 19, Village Vanguard 66, Don Quijote 52, Kiddy Land 16, 3COINS 110), illustrator apparel and stationery (graniph 21/77, WEGO 23, Kokuyo 78, mt 80), convenience-store fairs and packaging (Lawson 6, Ministop 8, FamilyMart 84), the illustrator pop-up circuit (MEDICOS SHOP 渋谷 133, animate 池袋 134, キャラコレ 渋谷 136), national manufacturer packaging (Calbee 39, Morinaga 42), and brand-run creator programmes (Pixio 140, Pigg Party 141).
CONFIRMED (census `_VERDICT.md` post-re-sweep addendum): the illustrator pop-up circuit is a real and reasonably dense Japanese channel that a brand-side search cannot find by construction, because those campaigns are named after the artist rather than after an IP.

**(b) Publication channels where the evidence lives.**
This is the operational finding for anyone doing further Japan research.
CONFIRMED (source-domain bucketing of all 146 census source URLs):

| Publication channel | All rows | INDEPENDENT | REP=yes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brand- or IP-official page | 63 | 16 | 10 |
| Collab aggregator (collabo-cafe.com et al) | 42 | 19 | 6 |
| Press wire (PR TIMES, digitalpr) | 24 | 12 | 10 |
| Trade / lifestyle press | 17 | 4 | 0 |

ASSUMED reading: **20 of the 26 addressable rows are sourced to either a press wire release or the brand's own newsroom.**
Trade and lifestyle press carries 17 rows and **zero** REP=yes rows.
So for the addressable end of the Japanese market specifically, the productive research route is the brand's own news page and PR TIMES, not the trade titles - which inverts the naive assumption that trade press is where licensing news lives.
CONFIRMED (census `_VERDICT.md` section 5, and repeated in the re-sweep note): natalie.mu returns 403, uniqlo.com times out, and some Asahi/Suntory brand pages 403, so aggregators are used as workarounds and are flagged per row.

CONFIRMED (primary, prtimes.co.jp corporate site, fetched 2026-08-01): PR TIMES self-describes as 国内シェアNo.1 and as a platform where 月間1万本を超えるプレスリリース (more than 10,000 press releases per month) are gathered.
ASSUMED: PR TIMES is therefore the single highest-yield index for Japanese campaign announcements, which is consistent with it carrying 24 census rows including 10 of the 26 addressable ones.

**(c) The trade-show channel, which the census does not cover and which is new to this pack.**
CONFIRMED (primary, RX Japan press release on PR TIMES, and the Content Tokyo official site, both fetched 2026-08-01): 第19回 ライセンシング ジャパン ran 2026-06-17..06-19 at Tokyo Big Sight West Hall, organised by RX Japan, held inside コンテンツ東京, with more than 800 characters and brands exhibiting.
CONFIRMED (same sources): the official visitor description names 「メーカー・卸の商品開発・企画」「小売の商品企画・店頭販促」「メディア関連企業」「広告・PR」 as the visiting buyer functions.
CONFIRMED (same RX Japan release): **BUGCAT CAPOO is named among the exhibiting properties**, alongside Doraemon, SUBARU, BELLYGOM, 味ぽん, ロッテイタガム, がおぱわるぅ and 月刊ムー.
CONFIRMED (Content Tokyo official site): the 2027 edition is stated as 2027-06-23..06-25 at Tokyo Big Sight East Hall, with 960 properties stated.

ASSUMED, and this is the most directly relevant new fact in section 1: a Taiwanese creator-owned indie IP (Bugcat Capoo, self-licensed via 卡特島創意 per `findings/ip-timelines/bugcat-capoo.md`) exhibited at Japan's largest licensing trade show in 2026.
That is a documented precedent for a non-Japanese independent IP presenting into the Japanese licensing market through the trade-show route, and it is a precedent PBC can study.
No conclusion is drawn here about whether that route suits any PBC-side IP.
