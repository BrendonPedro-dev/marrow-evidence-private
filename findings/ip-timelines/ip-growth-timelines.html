How proven indie IPs climbed: growth timelines

The visual companion to findings/ip-timelines/ · researched 2026-07-24 · four proven indie IPs. Each has a shape chart (the climb at a glance) and a full dated table (every deal). Every point traces to a source-verified row in the timeline markdown.

The market-building playbook. These are the indie IPs that made it - and their public deal sequences show how they escalated from a first tie-up to headlining institutional and big-brand campaigns. The blunt "small-local → drink-chain → store-wave → institutional" ladder fits none of them cleanly. Two engines recur instead: a fame-first engine (Capoo, LAIMO), where a huge free sticker/social audience pulls big brands - even institutions - in as the earliest deals; and a designer-toy-first engine (Monday Bruce, LuLu), where a collectible blind box plus a pop-up / licensing-expo circuit climbs through experiential venues. Both reach the top tier fast once the "currency" (audience, or collectibility) exists.

HARD LIMIT - read this first. These are PUBLIC collaboration sequences only, reconstructed from campaign announcements. They show what happened and when. They do NOT contain the decision context - who each IP pitched and got rejected by, deal terms, fees, or the creator's reasoning for a given brand. That is private and comes from the interview program (talking to creators directly), never from this desk research. The charts plot only observable, dated data points; gaps in the record are marked as gaps, not filled.
How to read each chart: brand deal inflection (first-major / peak) debut / own-product milestone Vertical position = brand tier (Mid → Major → Institutional, climbing). No colour code to decode - the height is the tier.

Escalation read

Capoo did not start small-and-local and climb slowly — its earliest verified deal (2017, an EasyCard charity/transit card) is already institutional, and its second (2019, an O-Bank card) is a bank. Within the traceable record it was landing finance/institutional partners before a single drink chain or QSR — the reverse of the usual indie ladder. The currency was sticker fame (top-10 LINE by 2017): brands came to the character. A 2020 cluster (a wrapped MRT train, an Eslite/Mitsui pop-up café, a major PC-game collab) moved it into physical retail and gaming; 7-ELEVEN access became repeatable from 2022. The prestige ceiling breaks in 2025 with the national post office and the 7-ELEVEN × 故宮 full-store 集點 wave across 7,100+ stores — after which a listed-FHC shareholder gift, a CTBC public ice-rink, an official Pokemon collab, and a second 90-SKU 7-11 wave cluster rapidly through 2026.

Growth pattern

Fame-first, institution-early. A huge free audience on LINE stickers/Webtoon pulled institutional partners in as the earliest collaborations, well before any drink or QSR tie-up; a 2020 build-out into physical retail and gaming converted that reach into repeatable convenience-store access, then the 2025 故宮 wave broke the prestige ceiling. The defining feature for PBC's story: the whole climb was run in-house through the creator's own studio (Carter Island) with no agency — proof an indie can reach the top institutional tier self-licensed, which is also exactly why Capoo is not itself an addressable PBC client.

Fame-first, major-brand-immediately, then agency-scaled
Debut

Inflection · first major
大同 Tatung (listed appliance maker), Feb 2014 — rice cooker sold out in 1 hour
First-deal → first-major
~1 month from first verified deal (Jan 2014 zoo) to first major brand
Ownership / REP
Individual 楊承霖 (Yang Cheng-lin) — agency-run by listed HIM 華研 2015-2023, then studio-run (承三工作室)
Census REP=yes — but "open" is a recent, post-agency condition (2024+), not original

Escalation read

LAIMO broke into a major brand almost immediately: within the same season (early 2014) it landed a municipal zoo, a listed appliance maker (大同, whose 1,000-unit rice cooker sold out in an hour), and a top convenience chain — roughly 20 months after formal debut and off viral Facebook fame (500k+ followers) plus a first-wave paid-LINE-sticker launch. The 2015 professionalisation is the inflection: signing to listed agency 華研 put a real licensing machine behind the IP and produced, within months, a CTBC bank card — the platform for the dense 2021-2022 run (7-11, momo, MOS ×2, GUCCI, PAZZO, FamilyMart full-store, 茶湯會, PONY). The prestige ceiling breaks in 2025 with CeraVe / L'Oréal, a global beauty conglomerate fronting a Taiwan indie across every major drugstore channel.

Growth pattern

Fame-first, major-brand-immediately, then agency-scaled. Viral illustration + first-wave stickers built the audience; a listed appliance maker, a top convenience chain and a cosmetics brand were its earliest deals; then a listed agency (華研) held the contract through the densest growth decade before the CeraVe peak. The correction to the census, and the point for PBC: unlike Capoo, LAIMO's climb was NOT self-licensed for most of its life — agency-repped 2015-2023, self/studio-managed only from 2024, so its "open" status is recent, not original. Note the through-line: beauty (HEME, 2014) was present a decade before CeraVe — the 2025 deal was a tier-up, not a debut.

Escalation read

The clock is the headline: created 2021, first product (a blind box) Dec 2022, and a flagship institutional landmark — the Taipei 101 observatory — by April 2026: roughly 3.3 years from first product to a national landmark, the shortest of the three Taiwan indies. Its first major/institutional moment arrived as an experiential venue campaign, explicitly framed by 101's chairman as promoting Taiwan original IP. The visible inflection is the 2025 internationalisation-by-pop-up wave — a Hong Kong pop-up, a Japan pop-up, and the Shanghai China Licensing Expo within one quarter, then Tokyo Licensing Japan. There is no fame-first flywheel here: the audience was built through the product and the pop-up circuit itself.

Growth pattern

Designer-toy-first, experiential-and-licensing-led, fast. Born as a blind box, it grew through a collectible-toy cadence and a pop-up-exhibition circuit (domestically with 哈玩具 HOBBY TOY), escalated cross-border via pop-up stores and B2B licensing expos, and topped out (so far) at an experiential landmark — exactly the census's "experiential/venue campaigns skew indie" finding. For PBC, Bruce is the counter-model to the fame-first indies: a young, company-owned collectible IP that reached a national landmark in ~3.3 years through toys, pop-ups and expo B2B — and that has not yet touched the mass-retail convenience/drink/finance channels where Capoo and LAIMO broke into volume, marking the obvious next-stage headroom.

Escalation read

LuLu's public sequence is a geographic escalation stacked on a format escalation, and by the time it hits Taiwan it is already a major IP arriving top-down. Its first third-party moment (T.O.P. mall, HK, Feb 2020) is an experiential mall installation — it broke into brand work venue-first, at 3-metre-inflatable scale from the outset. The engine is Toyzeroplus's licensing-distribution reach, not a lone creator's fame. Its first mass-retail channel came early (a 7-Eleven Hong Kong line, Aug 2022 — years before Taiwan). 2024 is the internationalisation inflection, all on the 5th anniversary: HK mall event, Thailand entry, a 2nd HK 7-Eleven line, then Taiwan entry via a flagship department-store pop-up. Taiwan was entered top-down, then localised via a first-ever Capoo IP-x-IP (Nov 2025) and a domestic QSR + convenience push (May 2026).

Growth pattern

Designer-toy-first, licensing-house-scaled, escalating by geography. A Cici's Story character launched as a Toyzeroplus blind box (2019), it grew into a HK/mainland phenomenon and broke into brand work through experiential mall installations rather than a social flywheel — then internationalised (HK → Thailand → Taiwan) on its 5th anniversary. For PBC, LuLu is the "big regional IP enters Taiwan top-down" case: it does not climb the Taiwan indie ladder, it parachutes onto the top of it (department-store scale on day one) and buys local relevance via a hometown-hero IP-x-IP (Capoo) and mass-retail deals. Its untouched rungs (verified): drink-chain, bank/finance, and transit — the same institutional-finance ceiling Capoo cleared but LuLu, so far, has not.

Cross-IP synthesis - do they share a playbook?

Placed on one shared calendar axis, the four climbs sort into two archetypes with a very different clock. The two fame-first IPs started early (2011-2014) and had long runways; the two designer-toy IPs started recently (2019-2021) and climbed in a compressed window. Hollow dot = origin; filled dots = the escalation milestones.

Archetype A - Fame-first (Capoo, LAIMO)

  • Currency: a huge free audience (LINE stickers, viral Facebook) built before the first big deal - brands come to the character.
  • Institution-early, not institution-last. Capoo's first verified deal is a bank/transit card; LAIMO's is a listed appliance maker within ~1 month of its first tie-up.
  • Channel path: convenience-store waves, hand-shake drink chains, finance cards, beauty - the mass-retail / institutional rungs.
  • Ceiling break = institutional (Capoo's 7-11×故宮 full-store wave; LAIMO's CeraVe/L'Oréal).

Archetype B - Designer-toy-first (Monday Bruce, LuLu)

  • Currency: collectible desirability - born as a blind box; the audience is built through the product and the pop-up circuit, not a social flywheel.
  • Venue-first, licensing-led. Break into brand work through mall / department-store pop-ups and B2B licensing expos.
  • Escalate by experience & geography (Bruce: HK/Japan pop-ups → Taipei 101; LuLu: HK → Thailand → Taiwan, top-down).
  • Ceiling break = experiential landmark (Taipei 101 observatory) - and the mass-retail convenience/drink/finance rungs are largely untouched (headroom).

The honest synthesis. There is no single ladder - the textbook small-local→drink→store-wave→institutional sequence describes none of them (Capoo went institutional first; LuLu parachutes in at the top). What they genuinely share is a shape: build a "currency" (audience or collectibility) → convert it through a signature channel (mass-retail for fame-first, experiential venues for toy-first) → break a ceiling (institutional for fame-first, landmark-experiential for toy-first) → reach IP-x-IP peer status. And a clear tell for PBC: fame or collectible heat, not a slow relationship grind, is what pulled the big brands - three of the four landed a major or institutional partner as (effectively) their first verified deal. The ownership machine underneath varied wildly (self, agency, company, licensing house), which is precisely the seam PBC's representation model addresses.

Method & honesty

  • Every date and deal traces to a timeline .md (which traces to a curl-verified source). No deals or dates were invented for this visual; the charts and tables carry only real, dated rows.
  • Gaps are shown as gaps. Capoo's 2015-2018 small-tie-up record, LAIMO's 2016 & 2019-20, Monday Bruce's 2023-24 pop-up history (JS-paginated archive), and LuLu's 2019-2023 HK/mainland core-market history are all under-covered by the sources reachable this run - absence is a coverage gap, not proof of inactivity.
  • Undated & single-source rows are flagged (⚑) in the tables (LAIMO × Uni-Lions; Monday Bruce × DBS Bank and LUX blind boxes; the LuLu × 7-11 Taiwan ePoint flyer, date approximate).
  • "Earliest deal found under these queries" is stated honestly: the true first tie-up may predate press coverage (all four launched commercially before their first traceable brand deal).
  • Two census date corrections carried from the LuLu file: 麥味登 × LuLu is 2026 (not 2025), and the LuLu × Capoo line debuted 2025-11 at TTF (not 2026-06). The plots use the corrected dates.
  • Tier is a research judgment, not a legal grade. "Major / institutional" reflects brand prominence (listed corporates, banks, national post/transit, landmark venues, top chains); reasonable analysts could move a borderline row one tier.
