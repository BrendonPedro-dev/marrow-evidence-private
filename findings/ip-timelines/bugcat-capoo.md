## 1. ORIGIN

- **Created: 2014**, by Taiwan illustrator **亞拉 (Ara)**, a former IT worker.
- **Started on: 巴哈姆特 (Bahamut) personal blog** as a self-created character, then went commercial via **LINE 原創市集 (LINE Creators Market) stickers in October 2014** - a 40-sticker set that "一炮而紅" (took off immediately).
  Source: https://zh.wikipedia.org/zh-tw/%E8%B2%93%E8%B2%93%E8%9F%B2%E5%92%96%E6%B3%A2 (lead); creator-origin corroborated by https://www.hk01.com/職場/149205/ (IT-worker-to-illustrator profile) and the CNA 10th-anniversary interview https://www.cna.com.tw/news/ahel/202601240055.aspx.
- **The name:** "貓貓蟲" (cat-cat-bug) because the creature looks part-cat, part-bug; "咖波" from 咖 (of 咖啡/coffee, the creator's habit) + 波 (a cute-sounding syllable).
- **Webcomic:** won a **2015 LINE Webtoon short-form contest** and serialized on LINE Webtoon until the series ended **March 2020**.
- **Ownership from day one to now: single-creator-owned.**
  Ara owns the character outright and runs licensing through **his own captive studio 卡特島創意 (Carter Island)** - there is no publisher, committee, agency master-licensor, or conglomerate stake.
  This is the census's ownership finding too (INDEPENDENT, REP=no: self-licensed).
  At the brand's 10th anniversary (2024) Ara formalised operations into a 100+ page licensing manual - a signal the operation professionalised but stayed in-house (CNA interview, above).

## 3. THE ESCALATION READ

**Capoo did NOT start small-and-local and climb slowly - it broke into institutional brands remarkably early, then widened.**

- **First "major/institutional" moment came almost at the start of the visible record:** the earliest verified deal (2017-02, EasyCard × a charity foundation) is already an institutional transit-card collab, and the second (2019-05, O-Bank bank card) is a bank.
  So within the traceable record Capoo was landing finance/institutional partners **before** it did a single drink chain or QSR - the reverse of the usual indie ladder.
- **Time from first verified deal to first big-brand moment: effectively zero** - the first verified deal *is* a big-institutional one (2017 EasyCard).
  A truer read: Capoo's sticker fame (top-10 LINE by 2017) was the currency; brands came to the character rather than the character grinding up through small local shops.
- **The visible inflection point is the 2020 cluster:** in a single year Capoo did a wrapped MRT train (高雄捷運), an Eslite/Mitsui pop-up café, and a major PC-game collab (RO).
  2020 is where it moves from "card-face licensing" to owning physical retail space and game IP-slots - the platform that later made full-store convenience waves plausible.
- **The prestige ceiling breaks in 2025:** 中華郵政 (national post, dedicated 咖波郵局) and then the **7-ELEVEN × 國立故宮博物院 全店集點 wave across 7,100+ stores** - a format normally reserved for Sanrio/Disney/Chiikawa.
  That 2025 故宮 wave is the clearest "unlocked the next tier" moment: after it, 2026 brings a listed FHC shareholder gift (華南金控), a CTBC public ice-rink, an official Pokemon collab, and a second 90-SKU 7-11 wave in quick succession.

**Shape of the climb:** not a slow ramp but an **early-institutional entry (2017-19 finance/transit) → physical-retail + gaming build-out (2020) → repeat convenience-chain access (2022 7-11) → flagship full-store + national-institution tier (2025) → dense listed-corporate/institutional cluster (2026).**

## 4. FORMAT PROGRESSION

Capoo's format path is NOT the textbook "drink → merch → store-wave → institutional."
It ran roughly:

**Digital stickers (2014) → co-branded cards / finance (2017 EasyCard, 2019 O-Bank) → transit + pop-up retail + gaming (2020 MRT / Eslite café / RO) → QSR + convenience-store themed stores (2022 繼光香香雞, 7-11) → hand-shake drink + full-store 集點 waves (2024-25 清心福全, Sushiro, 7-11×故宮) → institutional/corporate premiums + IP-x-IP (2025-26 中華郵政, 華南金控, CTBC, Pokemon, LuLu).**

- **It broke into the big leagues on the FINANCE/INSTITUTIONAL format first** (transit cards and bank cards), not on drinks or merch - unusual for an indie.
- **Drink chains came late** (first verified: 2024 清心福全), *after* it already had bank cards, MRT trains and 7-11 stores - the opposite order from a typical手搖-first indie.
- **The convenience-store 集點 full-store wave (2025 故宮) is the format it used to reach true mass-retail scale** (7,100+ stores), and it repeated the channel in 2026.
- **By 2026 the newest format is IP-x-IP** (Pokemon; LuLu the Piggy) - co-branding with other characters rather than with brands, a sign the IP is now a peer to portfolio properties.

## 5. GROWTH PATTERN SUMMARY (the observable playbook)

This is the **PUBLIC sequence, inferred from campaign announcements** - not the creator's stated strategy.
Capoo's observable playbook was **fame-first, institution-early**: it built a huge free-audience on LINE stickers/Webtoon (2014-17), and that reach - not a ladder of small local deals - is what pulled in institutional partners (a charity EasyCard, then a bank card) as the *earliest* visible collaborations, well before any drink or QSR tie-up.
From a 2020 build-out into physical retail (pop-up cafés, an Eslite/Mitsui store) and gaming, it converted audience into repeatable convenience-store access (7-ELEVEN from 2022), then broke the prestige ceiling in 2025 with a national-post 咖波郵局 and a 7,100-store 7-ELEVEN × 故宮 full-store 集點 wave - after which listed-corporate and institutional premiums (華南金控, CTBC) and IP-x-IP collabs (Pokemon, LuLu the Piggy) clustered rapidly in 2026.
The defining feature for PBC's market-building story: **the whole climb was run in-house through the creator's own captive studio (Carter Island) with no agency or conglomerate** - proof an indie can reach the top institutional tier self-licensed, which is also exactly why Capoo is not itself an addressable PBC client (census REP=no).

## Engine determination

**Call: FAME-FIRST** (the engine as the original timeline research defines it against designer-toy/goods-first: a pre-built free audience pulls brands in, rather than a product circuit building the audience).

Observable evidence for the call:

- **The audience predates every deal.** The record opens with free content at platform scale - Bahamut blog (2014), a LINE sticker set that "一炮而紅" (2014-10), a LINE Webtoon contest win and serial (2015-2020), top-10 LINE sticker chart placements (2017, 2020) - and the Facebook page already read 161,801 likes (2015-03, curl-verified Wayback) two years before the first verified brand deal (2017-02).
- **The deal vehicle is the image, not a collectible.** The early sequence is card-face licensing (EasyCard 2017, O-Bank 2019), a wrapped train, themed stores and 集點 waves - Capoo's likeness on other parties' products and spaces. There is no blind-box line, no toy-show debut, no designer-toy fair circuit anywhere in the 20-row record; merch waves (2025-26 7-11 SKUs) arrive only after the `gt_1m` crossing, as an output of fame rather than its source.
- **The social curve ran ahead of the deal ladder at every measured point.** 161K FB before any deal; 817K-918K FB during the 2019-2020 retail build; the 1M crossing before the prestige tier (故宮, Pokemon, 中華郵政 all follow it). In a goods-first arc the deals run ahead of the social curve (LuLu at 100K holding a 7-Eleven line); here the curve leads.
- **The variant trait: self-licensed throughout.** The whole climb ran through the creator's captive studio (Carter Island) with no agency - where LAIMO's fame-first arc was agency-scaled (華研 2015-2023), Capoo's is the same engine with the licensing machine kept in-house.

Confidence note: **high.**
The ordering (audience first, image-licensing vehicle, curve ahead of deals) is unambiguous and now numerically evidenced at four dated points.
The honesty limit: the currency of the 2014-2017 institutional entry was LINE sticker DISTRIBUTION (chart ranks), which no follower vocabulary measures - the Facebook ladder is the measurable shadow of that fame, not the fame itself, and the first deal's own band is an honest straddle-unknown.
For comp use: **a fame-first prospect comps to this arc - specifically one whose audience is platform-native (stickers/comics) and who intends to stay self-licensed; a goods-first prospect does not.**
