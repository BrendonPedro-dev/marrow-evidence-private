6. Cross-market thesis read

The founder thesis - "indies are shut out" - does not survive any market in its blunt form, but a precise version survives cleanly in all three.

Taiwan refines it. ~27% and demonstrably winning the highest-prestige formats (a listed FHC premium, the national post, a 7,100-store 集點 wave, Taipei 101) - but the dominant domestic indie (Capoo) is self-licensed, so the addressable pool is real yet thin by volume and concentrated in ~5 open creators plus an inbound-foreign channel.

Japan refines it. "Indies are a minor share" survives (30.5% overall; mass/platform formats - gacha, telecom points, card-skins - are near-monolithically corporate), but "shut out" weakens: the barrier is channel concentration, not access. Indies live in lifestyle retail, illustrator apparel/stationery, CVS art capsules, cosmetics - and from there reach national and global brands.

Korea refines it, almost identically - with a distinctive dual portfolio wall: foreign licensors (Sanrio, Pokemon, Disney, plus new Netflix screen-IP: Squid Game, KPop Demon Hunters) and domestic platform owners (Kakao Friends, LINE Friends/IPX, Iconix's Loopy, SAMG's Teenieping, HYBE's TinyTAN). Platform ownership is the decisive line - the most famous "Korean characters" are platform-owned = portfolio, which is why they never swell the indie column.

The one defensible overall read: independent IP is a stable ~quarter of East-Asian co-branding and it does reach the largest brands - so indies are not shut out. What is true: portfolio owns the ~70% majority and near-monopolizes the high-volume, platform-like channels; and the addressable-by-PBC slice (~12-16% of campaigns) is narrower than the raw indie share because much indie IP is self-licensed (Taiwan), captive (Japan), or platform-owned (Korea). The recurring finding: fame does not equal representability - the household-name indies are the closed ones, and PBC's real market is the quieter, broader creator tier, largest and deepest in Japan.

7. Method limits & where the markets aren't perfectly comparable

Shared caveats

  • Sample census, not exhaustive. ~130 curl-verified rows per market - a structured sample, not the full population. Every share is a central estimate.
  • Recall bias toward covered campaigns. Press-friendly campaigns are over-represented; because indie collabs skew press-friendly, this if anything inflates the indie share slightly - the true figure may sit a touch lower.
  • Uneven per-year bases. 2025 is deep everywhere (n = 60/90/80); 2024 and 2026 YTD are thin and format-skewed. Read the trend as "roughly flat at a quarter" and discount both 2024 outliers.
  • REPRESENTABLE is a judgment call, not a legal finding - directional market-entry guidance about whether an IP is captive or open, to be confirmed case by case.

Where the three are NOT perfectly comparable (flagged)

  • Korea excluded 2 public-body rows (Seoul-city Haechi, Daejeon-city Ggumdoli), so its base is 133, not 135. Taiwan and Japan had no public-body IP to exclude. Korea's 19/135 addressable figure uses the full 135 denominator per its own verdict, so it is very slightly conservative.
  • Taiwan v2 is a reclassify, not a re-find. v2 re-scored and enriched the 129 v1 rows on the shared definition and added REPRESENTABLE; it did not run a fresh discovery sweep, so it inherits v1's coverage exactly. (The re-scoring moved exactly 3 rows and left 27.1% unchanged - a robustness signal.)
  • Sampling channels differ by market. Each build opened the veins most legible in that market's press. Overall shares are comparable; per-channel composition is not identical, so channel-level cross-market comparisons need care.
  • UNCLEAR rates & causes differ. Japan ~0.8% (well-documented © lines); Taiwan 3.9% (borderline brand-x-brand); Korea 4.5%, driven almost entirely by the genuinely contestable webtoon/platform ownership line.
  • REP=unclear is not uniform. Japan carries the most (25% of indie rows), so its 16.0% addressable is a conservative floor; Korea's "yes" rows are a plausible-target estimate (agency-managed creators, private terms), and Korea's 2024-01 window misses several indies' biggest institutional collabs from 2021-2023, understating their true footprint.

Net: the indie-share reads (~a quarter everywhere) and addressable reads (~12-16% of campaigns) are robust and comparable; the residual non-comparabilities all push in known directions and none overturn the two headline findings.
