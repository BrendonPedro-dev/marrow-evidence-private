# Malaysia - IP scale bands (independent rows)

Enrichment pass over `malaysia/campaigns.md`. One row here per INDEPENDENT census row.
The census table is the index and is not edited by this pass.

**Band vocabulary (exactly these codes):** `lt_10k` / `10k_50k` / `50k_200k` / `200k_1m` / `gt_1m` / `unknown`.
These match the /start intake's `follower_band` codes character-for-character.

**Band definition:** the IP's PRIMARY channel following at the time of the campaign, a single channel and never a cross-platform total.

**Evidence basis codes:** `at-campaign` (countable figure at or near the campaign window) / `current-proxy` (only a CURRENT count findable; retrieval date recorded, NOT at-campaign truth) / `unknown` (no countable public evidence; Notes record what was checked).

**Coverage:** 31 independent rows (20 REP=yes, 10 REP=unclear, 1 REP=no). 6 at-campaign, 23 current-proxy, 2 unknown.

**Multi-creator convention.** Row 121 carries five separate artists in one census row.
Its Band cell holds the **highest band present** in the row, and per-creator bands live in the *Multi-creator detail* table below.
That detail table is the one to count creator-level distributions from, the same convention the Philippines, Hong Kong and Indonesia files use.

**Malaysia is the first market in this pass where campaign press states a following at all.**
All 25 census source URLs for the independent rows were fetched and grepped; exactly one carries a figure (MARKETECH APAC on Bichi Mao, 2.4 million).
Six markets have now been swept this way and the running score is 1 hit in roughly 130 sources, so the structural scarcity finding holds even with the exception recorded.

**Malaysia is also the market where the TikTok channel became readable**, which is what settles row 85 (see the method notes).
Every Malaysian illustrator in this market has a *smaller* TikTok than Instagram, so the Instagram-primary choice used across this pass is now a verified result here rather than an assumption.

## Method notes for this market

- **Malaysia broke the zero-hit run on campaign press, once.** All 25 census source URLs for the independent rows were fetched and grepped for `follower` / `pengikut`; exactly one (MARKETECH APAC on Bichi Mao) states a figure, and that figure is a cross-platform total rather than a channel count. Five prior markets returned nothing at all, so the corrected finding is that campaign press states a following about 1 time in 130 sources, not never - and when it does, it states a *total*, which is the wrong unit for this band.
- **TikTok IS curl-verifiable, contrary to the pass's own earlier method note.** `curl -A "facebookexternalhit/1.1" https://www.tiktok.com/@<handle>` returns `og:description` = "@handle N Followers, N Following, N Likes - ...". A Googlebot user-agent gets HTTP 403 on the identical URL, which is why the earlier sweep concluded TikTok was closed. This was decisive for row 85 and should be run across the four remaining markets and, on a later pass, back over the six already-banded files.
- **The TikTok read confirms rather than overturns the Instagram-primary convention here.** Every Malaysian illustrator checked has a TikTok an order of magnitude below their Instagram (Loka Made 10.9k vs 62K, Cloakwork 13.7k vs 66K, Kirin Sharom 3,847 vs 40K, Nas Suha 19 vs 28K). The two exceptions are both non-illustrator IPs: BoBoiBoy is TikTok-primary at 6.5m against 1M on Instagram, and Bichi Mao runs the two nearly level (949.7k vs 995K). The pattern to carry forward is that **animation and comic IPs may be TikTok-primary while illustrator IPs are not**.
- **Facebook follower counts remain unverifiable, but Facebook post pages are partly readable.** `facebook.com/<page>` returns no `og:description` and no follower or like count to `facebookexternalhit/1.1`, Googlebot or Twitterbot, tested against the Bichi Mao, Shiba Says and BoBoiBoy pages. This is the binding limit on rows 83 and 85, both of which are foreign or animation IPs whose Facebook page is plausibly their largest channel. A *post* URL including its text slug does return HTTP 200 with body text to Googlebot, which is how row 14's handle was confirmed - so Facebook is usable for handle confirmation and unusable for counts.
- **The Instagram `web_profile_info` API was rate-limited from the first call of this session**, before any call had been made in it. The 401 body is "Please wait a few minutes before you try again" and it persisted across the `i.` and `www.` hosts. The practical consequence is that the unrounded-count tool is **not reliably available at all**, so a rounded figure straddling a band edge may simply be unresolvable - which is what happened to Instagram's "1M" on row 85 and to the 995K on rows 9 and 10.
- **Two new handle-discovery veins, both better than name-guessing.** First, **the IP's own website markup**: `pangroksulap.com` and `lokamade.com` each link their real Instagram in the footer, recovering two handles that eleven guesses between them had missed. Second, **the brand's own LinkedIn campaign post**, which is readable to a Googlebot user-agent and in Bata Malaysia's case writes the artist's handle out in full ("@art.of.lihua") where the trade-press coverage of the same campaign names only her first name. Try the official site first, then brand LinkedIn, then guessing.
- **Malaysian trade press links no artist accounts but sometimes names handles in prose.** Fetching all 25 sources for `instagram.com/` handles returned only publishers' and brands' own accounts. But two Marketing-Interactive roundup entries write the handle inline ("Ame Lukis (@ame.lukis)", "Kirin Sharom (bungadanbintang)"), so grep the body text for parenthesised handles before concluding a source is silent.
- **Squatted and near-miss handles are as pervasive here as in Thailand and would produce confidently wrong bands.** `@yellobanana`, `@yello_banana`, `@bichimao`, `@bichi_mao`, `@lokamade`, `@nassuha`, `@amelukis`, `@perempuanmelawan`, `@katun`, `@lihua`, `@lihuaart`, `@bearbossbuddies` all exist, all look right, and none is the IP. The pattern is specific enough to be actionable: **the real Malaysian handle is very often the one with the extra separator** (a second underscore in `yello__banana`, a trailing underscore in `nassuha_` and `katun_`, a dot in `ame.lukis` and `bichi.mao`, a `the` prefix in `thelokamade`).
- **Both unknowns are Malaysian individuals surfaced through brand programmes, not foreign IPs.** This inverts Thailand's pattern, where every local creator resolved and both unknowns were inbound foreign IPs. Jean Lynn and Danial Kushairi are each credited by a brand roundup that names them without a handle, and Danial Kushairi's case is the sharper one: a same-name account exists at a plausible size and was *still* recorded unknown, because same-name is not identity.
- **The band-edge exposure in this market is a function of proxy gap, not follower count, and rows 90 and 120 prove it.** Both read 12K on Instagram; row 120's gap is 4.5 months and its band is sound, row 90's is 23 months and its band may genuinely be one tier too high. Any downstream weighting of these bands should carry the gap, not just the band.
