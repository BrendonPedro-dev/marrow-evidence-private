# Philippines co-branding census - verdict

Market eight in the region-by-region series (Taiwan v2, Japan, Korea, Malaysia, Thailand, Singapore, Hong Kong precede it).
Identical definition, identical ownership test, identical pinned edge cases, so this market compares row-for-row with the other seven.
Table: `findings/census/philippines/campaigns.md`.
Run date 2026-07-29.
**n = 82 curl-verified rows.** All 117 source URLs cited in the table and its exclusion/rejection sections returned HTTP 200 at build time.

The Philippines restores the WP-REST portal sweep that Hong Kong broke, and both keyword classes ran from the start of this market's sweep rather than being retrofitted.
Two structural facts shape everything below and are stated up front rather than buried in section 5.
First, **the country's single most important lifestyle title for this beat, `spot.ph`, is unreachable at any URL from this client** - not the REST endpoint, not article URLs, not the homepage - so the instrument here is narrower than in any earlier market that used this method.
Second, **the own-mascot exclusion removes more of this market's most visible character marketing than in any of the seven earlier markets**, and the excluded list is itself a finding.

## 1. The ratio

**Overall - the quotable numbers:**

| Class | Rows | Share |
|---|---|---|
| PORTFOLIO | 75 | **91.5%** |
| INDEPENDENT | 7 | **8.5%** |
| UNCLEAR | 0 | 0.0% |

**The tranche caveat, carried from Hong Kong in a much milder form.**
The 82 rows are two adjudication tranches, not one sample.
Rows 1-52 came from the top-278 scored candidates worked in score order and hold **3 independent rows in 52 (5.8%)**.
Rows 53-82 came from a second tranche that deliberately worked the artist-side tag class alongside the IP and brand classes, and hold **4 independent rows in 30 (13.3%)**.
The spread is real but nothing like Hong Kong's 26-fold gap: here it is roughly 2.3x.
**The honest statement is that the Philippines' independent share is around 8.5%, that the figure is mildly biased upward by the second tranche's artist-side emphasis, and that a purely brand-side census of this market would have reported something closer to 6%.**

**Per year** (the per-year caveat carries: 2026YTD is only seven months, and three rows - 51, 78, 82 - are tagged on in-window announcements whose runs begin after the run date):

| Year | Rows | PORTFOLIO | INDEPENDENT | Indie share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2024 | 33 | 31 | 2 | 6.1% |
| 2025 | 32 | 29 | 3 | 9.4% |
| 2026YTD | 17 | 15 | 2 | 11.8% |

The trend line is upward across all three years, but on n=2, n=3, n=2 it is not a trend - it is three small numbers pointing the same way.
What it does support is the weaker claim that the indie layer is **not** shrinking, and that 2026's two rows (Offshore Music x Rob Cham, New Era x four artists) are the two most commercially serious artist-side campaigns in the whole table.

**Where the Philippines sits in the series.** At 8.5% it is a low-middle market: below Hong Kong's biased-upward 14.0%, above the brand-side-only floors that the earlier markets' first passes produced, and closest in shape to Thailand - a market with a dense, visible creator scene that brand campaigns have largely not yet reached.

## 2. The addressable-indie read

**REPRESENTABLE split across the 7 independent rows:**

| REP | Rows | Row numbers |
|---|---|---|
| yes | 6 | 8, 12, 50, 74, 75, 81 |
| no | 0 | - |
| unclear | 1 | 71 |

**No PH independent row is label-captive.** This is a genuine difference from Hong Kong, where the label-captive hypothesis was confirmed and concentrated in a single label. The Philippines has no Toyzeroplus-equivalent: there is no PH agency or label holding a stable of local character IP under exclusive commercial terms that this sweep could find. The one non-`yes` row is `unclear` for the opposite reason - **row 71 (Jollibee x Adarna House)** puts the rights with an independent *publisher* that manages many authors' and illustrators' work, so there is no single identifiable creator to represent.

**The addressable number: 6 rows, 14 named distinct creator entities.**

| Row | Brand | Creator entities (REP=yes) |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | adidas Philippines | Aral Cru (graffiti trio, counted as one entity) |
| 12 | Toki | Sean Go |
| 50 | Offshore Music | Rob Cham |
| 74 | Looking for Juan | Elmer Borlongan, June Digan, Jericho Moral, Jim Orencio, Rodel Tapaya |
| 75 | UNICEF Philippines | Ang INK collective - Iori Espiritu, Al Estrella named; further members unnamed in the source |
| 81 | New Era Philippines | Raco Ruiz, Jethro Olba (Antibiotic Smile), Lei Melendres, Nicolo Nimor (Nick Automatic) |

**14 is the count of distinct named creator entities with at least one evidenced brand campaign in the window and no captive arrangement.**
It is a floor, not an estimate of the addressable pool: Ang INK has more members than the source names, and the whole social-media channel where PH illustrators actually announce brand work is unswept.

**What the addressable fourteen actually look like.** They are not one scene, they are three.
- **Two art-toy / designer-object creators** (Sean Go; and, unattached, Klaris Orfinada's "Maria" - see below) working the same blind-box-adjacent category POP MART occupies at scale in this market.
- **Four streetwear-and-graphics illustrators** (Raco Ruiz, Jethro Olba, Lei Melendres, Nicolo Nimor) whose New Era drop is the single most commercially conventional artist-side campaign in the table - three headwear pieces, a graphic tee, and a **blind pack of illustration cards**, i.e. the exact mechanic the portfolio IPs use.
- **Seven fine-art and children's-book illustrators** (the Looking for Juan five plus the named Ang INK two) reaching consumers through licensed-artwork product lines rather than character licensing.

**The most important name in this section is not in the table.** "Maria", the art toy by **Klaris Orfinada**, is an independently-owned Filipino art-toy character explicitly benchmarked against Labubu and Sonny Angel in its own press coverage - and it has **no brand campaign attached anywhere in this sweep**. It is rejected as a row and recorded as the clearest unattached, apparently representable, commercially-shaped PH indie IP the census found.

| Format bucket | Rows | Share | Independent rows |

| Telco loyalty / rewards | 2 | 2.4% | **0** |

### The closed-formats cut - the Thailand question, answered

The Thailand run asked whether the highest-distribution formats are portfolio-only. **In the Philippines they very nearly are.**

- **Mall operators: 26 rows, zero independent.** SM Supermalls, Ayala Malls, Robinsons, Megaworld, Araneta, Fisher Mall and the resort venues between them account for nearly a third of the census and have licensed **no** creator-owned Filipino character property in the window. Mall-operator density is this market's defining structural feature and it is a completely closed format to indies.
- **QSR / convenience / F&B: 10 rows, one independent, and that one is REP=unclear.** Jollibee, McDonald's, 7-Eleven, Starbucks, Krispy Kreme, Pizza Hut, CoCo and OREO licensed Sanrio, Pokémon, Disney, Nintendo, POP MART and Moonton. The single non-portfolio counterparty is a book publisher, not a creator.
- **Telco / loyalty: 2 rows, zero independent** - and a third telco-adjacent row (68, Globe AT HOME) licensed Filipino artwork, but from the **estates** of deceased National Artists. The one telco campaign that did reach for Filipino illustration reached for dead illustrators.
- **Device: 6 rows, zero independent.** Every one is a global or regional licensor programme localised with PH pricing.
- **The two formats that are open:** licensed retail collections and drops (5 of 7 indie rows) and, marginally, e-commerce (row 12). Every representable Filipino creator in this census reached consumers through an apparel, product or art-object drop.

**The cut for the pitch:** in the Philippines, an independent creator's realistic route to a brand campaign is a retail or apparel drop. The mall, QSR, telco and device formats - which carry the distribution - are closed, and their closure is complete rather than partial.

## 4. Methodology and edge cases

### 4a. Which mechanism the Philippines exhibits - the naming question, answered

**The Philippines is Taiwan-type, decisively.** The taxonomy's prediction was that PH would behave either like Taiwan (press names local creators, small artist-side delta) or like Thailand (brand PR erases them). PH press names the creators - and names them well.

The evidence is direct and it is in the sources, not inferred:
- adobo Magazine put **Rob Cham** in its headline (row 50).
- Clavel Magazine named **all four** New Era collaborators *and* their studio aliases - Antibiotic Smile, Nick Automatic (row 81).
- BusinessMirror named **all five** Looking for Juan artists and described each artist's work individually (row 74).
- adobo named individual **Ang INK** members and quoted them on their own artwork (row 75).
- WhenInManila headlined **Aral Cru** and quoted the crew in Taglish (row 8).
- Interaksyon named **Sean Go** (row 12).

**Six of the seven independent rows carry the creator's name in the headline or lede.** There is no erasure problem in Philippine press.

**But the artist-side delta is not small, and that is the interesting part.**
Three of the four second-tranche independent rows (74, 75, 81) were retrieved by artist-side keywords and would not have surfaced from brand-side terms alone - the brands are Looking for Juan, UNICEF Philippines and New Era, none of which is on any brand-side list this series uses.
**So the Philippines is a market where the press names creators willingly but where a brand-side-only sweep still misses roughly 40% of the indie layer** - not because the coverage is missing, but because the *brands* doing indie work are small, purpose-led or category-specific and are invisible to a brand-name keyword net.
That is a distinct mechanism from both Taiwan and Thailand, and it is this market's methodological contribution: **naming behaviour and retrievability are separate variables.**

**The Tagalog finding stands, unimproved.** *Artista* means screen celebrity in Philippine usage and returns showbiz copy exclusively; *ilustrador* is not the working register, because PH trade and lifestyle press writes "illustrator" in English even in Taglish copy. No row in this census rests on a Tagalog-only source and translation confidence on any Tagalog-sourced call is recorded as **low**. Artist-side sweeping in the Philippines must run in English.

### 4b. The komiks / webcomic scene - where the addressable count was lost

The task predicted this scene would be where the addressable-indie count is won or lost. **It was lost, and the reason is specific.**

The komiks and webcomic vein produced **zero rows**. Tarantadong Kalbo, Zsazsa Zaturnnah (Carlo Vergara), Diwata Komiks and the Komiket / Manila Comics Fair circuit all surfaced repeatedly across the sweep - as festival coverage, San Diego Comic-Con panel appearances, publishing news, an animated-feature development and a musical revival. **Not one of them attached to a brand campaign in the window.**

The nearest thing the census has to a komiks row is **row 68**, where Globe AT HOME licensed **Larry Alcala** - whose "Slice of Life" is a canonical Filipino cartoon property - from his **estate**, 23 years after his death, alongside three other deceased National Artists.

The honest reading: **Philippine komiks is a publishing, festival and convention culture, not yet a licensing culture.** The creators are visible, prolific and press-covered; the commercial bridge into brand campaigns has not been built. The adjacent scene that *has* crossed over is children's-book illustration (Ang INK, row 75) and streetwear graphics (row 81), neither of which is komiks proper.

### 4c. The own-mascot exclusion - the market's largest single edge case

Philippine QSR and retail run proprietary mascots at a strength no earlier market matched, and the exclusion removes campaigns that would otherwise be among the most visible in the table:

- **Jollibee's own stable** (Jollibee, Hetty, Popo, Yum, Twirlie, Bee Happy) across Kids Meal, JolliBINI and anniversary programmes.
- **Jollibee x Good Smile Company Nendoroid** - the Jollibee mascot licensed *out* to a Japanese figure manufacturer.
- **Jollibee Kids Meal x Play-Doh** - excluded twice over: the only characters are Jollibee's own, and Play-Doh contributes a clay product line with no character asset.
- **The outbound leg of Jollibee x MLBB** (the Battle Emote), noted at row 45 and not counted again.
- **Potato Corner x Baby POCO blind boxes** (2026-04, P149, five plush designs, 100 Golden Pocos) - POCO is Potato Corner's own mascot, introduced by the brand in 2024-05. **This is the sharpest case in the series: the exclusion removes a campaign running the exact blind-box mechanic the census exists to count, purely because the brand owns the character.**
- **McDonald's Philippines' own blind box** (2025-12), with row 63 (McDo x Mario Kart) as the counted contrast.

Separately, on the same principle applied to the licensor's own retail rather than the brand's own mascot: **POP MART permanent stores** (SM Megamall 2025-12, SM Aura 2026-01, SM North EDSA 2026-02) and **Disney Store by SM permanent branches** (SM MOA 2024-09, SM North EDSA 2025-09) are excluded, while POP MART and Disney properties hosted by third-party venues as limited-run pop-ups **are** rows (18, 20, 31, 37, 40, 52, 65).

**Why this matters for the ratio.** The excluded mascots are, without exception, brand-owned - so the exclusion removes only portfolio-adjacent campaigns and does **not** suppress the indie count. If anything it flatters the indie share slightly, by removing a class of campaign that would all have been non-independent.

### 4d. Other edge cases applied

- **The anti-inflation merge, used heavily.** One campaign across many branches of one operator is one row: Pokémon Fun Day across five provinces (22), the Keeppley pop-up across three Megaworld malls (44), Bluey Playdates across seven SM malls (79), the vivo V70 / V70 FE / Flip Card / Globe bundle (41), MR.DIY's Stitch promo and collection legs (59), SM Cinema + Megamall + Toy Kingdom for Super Mario Galaxy (43).
- **Mall-hosted properties are rows** (Taiwan-v2 row 126 precedent), and the venue precedent separates hosted pop-ups from the licensor's own permanent retail.
- **Estates are PORTFOLIO** (the Miffy/Mercis rule), which is what places row 68's National Artists on the portfolio side despite being Filipino illustration.
- **Per-IP ownership test** applies even where one creator sits on both sides (Molly Factory precedent); **self-presentation is not ownership**.
- **Government-owned mascots excluded entirely** (Kumamon rule). Row 75 is the flagged near-miss in the opposite direction: the *brand* is an intergovernmental agency (UNICEF Philippines) but the *IP* is creator-owned, so it is counted.
- **A first-tranche rejection was corrected.** HMD x Barbie was rejected on a UK-release source; a dated PH price (PHP7,799) and PH commerce channel were later evidenced, so it is row 60.
- **Row 19 (Space of BTS) is this census's weakest "character" call** and is flagged rather than quietly kept. The second tranche rejected the SEVENTEEN "Artist-Made Collection" pop-up on the ground that member-designed lifestyle goods are not a character IP; row 19 is a HYBE-branded artist-property pop-up that arguably fails the same test. Rows 10 (BT21) and 53 (SKZOO) survive it cleanly, because both license actual character lines. If row 19 were dropped the portfolio count would fall to 74 and the indie share would rise to 8.6% - the ratio is not sensitive to it.

## 5. Coverage-confidence statement

**What this census is.** A sample census of 82 curl-verified rows, every one confirmed against a live PH source returning HTTP 200 with the campaign detail in the page body, with evidenced dates and no outcomes claimed anywhere.

**Honest depth.** The sweep ran 121 keywords across 20 portals - 2,420 site-query pairs, 63,252 raw hits, 33,788 distinct URLs, 1,867 filtered candidates. About **990 candidates (everything scoring >= 4) have been adjudicated across two tranches**. The ~879 unread remainder all sit at score 3, which is the `BRAND`-only and `COLLAB`-only tag classes; between them those classes produced **zero** rows in either tranche, so the marginal yield of reading them is low but not zero.

**Single-source count: 65 of 82 rows (79.3%).** 17 rows carry two or more independent sources. This is a higher single-source rate than most earlier markets and it is a direct consequence of the instrument: PH campaign news is heavily press-release-driven and often runs in exactly one outlet.

**Named holes, in order of size:**

1. **`spot.ph` is unreachable at any URL from this client** - not just the REST endpoint. It is the leading Philippine lifestyle title for this beat and it is entirely absent. This is the single largest hole in the coverage claim.
2. **`rappler.com`, `mb.com.ph`, `inquirer.net`/`lifestyle.inquirer.net`, `philstar.com`, `nolisoli.ph`, `scoutmag.ph`, `preview.ph`, `cosmo.ph`, `esquiremag.ph`, `news.abs-cbn.com`, `philstarlife.com`, `gmanetwork.com` and `booky.ph`** all returned 403, 401 or 404 to the sweep. The census rests on 20 portals of which 14 contributed candidates - the narrowest instrument of any market in this series that used the WP-REST method.
3. **Instagram, Facebook and TikTok are entirely unswept**, and this matters more in the Philippines than anywhere else in the series, because PH illustrators overwhelmingly announce their own brand work on social rather than to press. Section 2's count of 14 addressable creators should be read as a floor set by a press-only instrument.
4. **Regional and provincial press outside Metro Manila is thin.** `sugbo.ph` (Cebu) and `vismin.ph` (Visayas-Mindanao) were swept and between them contributed corroborating sources on two rows and no rows of their own. Provincial mall and QSR activations are almost certainly under-counted.
5. **Three rows (51, 78, 82) carry runs beginning after the 2026-07-29 run date**, tagged 2026YTD on dated in-window announcements or presales. They are flagged in place and no outcome is claimed for any of them. If a stricter reading dropped all three, 2026YTD falls to 14 rows and the overall n to 79 - still above the floor.
6. **BusinessMirror requires retry, not a dead-link verdict.** `businessmirror.com.ph` (a plurality of this census's sources) serves a Cloudflare interstitial returning HTTP 403 to a *randomly rotating* subset of requests. Across both tranches, every 403 resolved to 200 within a few retry rounds, including URLs re-checked minutes apart. A single-pass verifier would produce a misleading dead-link count.

**What this census can and cannot support.**
It supports the claim that the Philippine indie share of evidenced brand-x-character campaigns is **around 8.5%, mildly biased upward**, and that the market's high-distribution formats - mall, QSR, telco, device - are **closed to independent creators**, with a completeness in that closure that no earlier market matched.
It supports the claim that **Philippine press names local creators** and that the retrieval problem is on the brand side, not the coverage side.
It does **not** support any claim about campaign outcomes, sales, footfall or performance - none is made anywhere in this file or the table.
It does **not** support a claim that the addressable Filipino creator pool is 14; that is the floor a press-only, `spot.ph`-less, social-media-less instrument could reach.
