# Philippines co-branding census - campaign log

This is a curl-verified table of identifiable brand-x-IP **co-branding** campaigns in the Philippine market, 2024-01-01 to run date (2026-07-29).
Scope is co-branding only - a brand pairing with a character/illustration/toy IP for products, drinks, food, events, pop-ups or stores.
F&B-led but not F&B-only: retail, convenience, telco, banking, mall, cinema and loyalty campaigns count when a character IP fronts a consumer campaign.
Excluded: plain licensed merchandise at retail, KOL/celebrity endorsements, and brand-x-brand tie-ins with no character IP.

**Status at 2026-07-29 (two tranches, complete): 82 curl-verified rows (33x 2024, 32x 2025, 17x 2026YTD; 75 PORTFOLIO / 7 INDEPENDENT / 0 UNCLEAR).**
Every source URL in this file was re-fetched at build time: **117 distinct URLs, all HTTP 200** - 104 of them cited in the table rows, the rest in the exclusion and rejection sections.
This clears the ~80-row floor. The verdict is at `_VERDICT.md`.

The table is built in **two adjudication tranches**, each internally chronological:
- **Rows 1-52** - the first tranche, adjudicated from the top-278 scored candidates (score >= 5).
- **Rows 53-82** - the second tranche, adjudicated from the 710 candidates at score 4 (the `IP`-only, `COLLAB+BRAND` and `ARTIST` tag classes) plus a cross-filtered brand-x-IP title pass over the full 33,788-URL harvest.

Row numbers are stable across tranches - the second tranche appends rather than re-sorts, so every prose reference to a row number elsewhere in this file stays valid.
The candidate pool holds **1,867 filtered candidates** at the working relevance threshold and **33,788 distinct harvested URLs**; roughly **990** of those candidates (score >= 4) have now been adjudicated, and the ~879 remaining at score 3 are the tag classes (`BRAND`-only, `COLLAB`-only) that produced no rows at all in either tranche.

This is the PHILIPPINES run - market eight in a region-by-region series (Taiwan v2: 129 rows at `findings/census/taiwan-v2/campaigns.md`; Japan: 131 rows at `findings/census/japan/campaigns.md`; Korea: 135 rows at `findings/census/korea/campaigns.md`; Malaysia: 103 rows at `findings/census/malaysia/campaigns.md`; Thailand: 83 rows at `findings/census/thailand/campaigns.md`; Singapore: 81 rows at `findings/census/singapore/campaigns.md`; Hong Kong: 93 rows at `findings/census/hongkong/campaigns.md`); identical definition and rules so all markets compare row-for-row.

## Method (sample census, not exhaustive)

The Philippines restores the WP-REST portal sweep that Hong Kong broke, but on a **different and smaller set of portals than any earlier market**, because the country's flagship lifestyle titles are behind Cloudflare.

**Portals probed and rejected.**
`spot.ph` - the single most important PH lifestyle/consumer title for this beat - returns **HTTP 403 to every request from this client, including individual article URLs and the homepage**, not just the REST endpoint.
It is entirely absent from this census and that is the largest single named hole in the coverage claim.
Also 403 on `/wp-json/wp/v2/posts` with browser headers: `rappler.com`, `mb.com.ph` (Manila Bulletin), `nolisoli.ph`, `scoutmag.ph`, `esquiremag.ph`, `preen.ph`, `cosmo.ph`, `femalenetwork.com`, `candymag.com`, `preview.ph`, `unbox.ph`, `gadgetmatch.com`, `pepper.ph`, `news.abs-cbn.com`.
`inquirer.net` and `lifestyle.inquirer.net` return **401**; `pop.inquirer.net` returns 401.
`philstar.com`, `gmanetwork.com`, `philstarlife.com`, `bandwagon.asia` and `booky.ph` return **404** (no WP REST - non-WordPress or headless stacks).
`wheninmanila.com` resolves only on the `www.` host; the bare `whenmanila.com` spelling does not resolve at all.
`villagepipol.com` returns 504, `metro.style` / `orangemagazine.ph` / `manilastandard.net` return non-JSON bodies.

**Portals that answered, and are the census's actual instrument (20 swept, 14 contributed candidates):**
`www.wheninmanila.com` (mass-consumer lifestyle - the highest-yield vein), `www.adobomagazine.com` (the marketing/advertising trade title), `interaksyon.philstar.com` (Philstar's Interaksyon vertical, whose `hobbies-interests` section is the closest thing PH has to a dedicated character-collectibles desk), `businessmirror.com.ph` (its BMPlus branded-content desk carries a very large share of PH mall and QSR campaign PR), `gadgetsmagazine.com.ph`, `yugatech.com`, `techsabado.com`, `gamingph.com`, `clavelmagazine.com`, `pageone.ph`, `randomrepublika.com`, `dailypedia.net`, `vismin.ph`, `sugbo.ph`, `goodnewspilipinas.com`, `manilanews.ph`, `politiko.com.ph`, `mommymundo.com`, `mommyunwired.com`, `proudkuripot.com`.

**The sweep.** 121 keywords x 20 sites = **2,420 site-query pairs**, each paged to 200 results with `after=2023-12-01`, giving **63,252 raw hits -> 33,788 distinct URLs**.
Titles were then scored against an IP-name / collab-verb / brand-name / artist-term lexicon; **278 candidates scored at the strong threshold and 1,867 at the working threshold**.
Every retained row was confirmed by a `curl` against the live article URL returning HTTP 200 with the campaign detail in the page body; **all 73 distinct source URLs cited below were re-verified at build time and all returned HTTP 200** (see the BusinessMirror verification note at the end for what that took).
The Date column is the evidenced campaign window where the body states one, and the publisher's own publication date otherwise (marked "announced").

**Both keyword classes ran from the start of this market's sweep** - the re-sweep lesson from Taiwan/Japan/Korea/Malaysia/Thailand is baked in here, as it was in Singapore and Hong Kong, not retrofitted.

- *Artist-side (Tagalog):* ilustrador, artista.
**Tagalog returned effectively nothing usable and the reason is a false-friend problem, not an absence of creators:** *artista* in Philippine usage means "screen celebrity", not "visual artist", so the term retrieves showbiz copy exclusively; *ilustrador* is not the working register - PH trade and lifestyle press writes "illustrator" in English even in otherwise Taglish copy.
Translation confidence on any Tagalog-sourced call is therefore recorded as **low**, and no row in this table rests on a Tagalog-only source.

**A market-specific keyword trap, recorded because it cost real filter precision.** *Barbie* is a common Filipino given name.
The brand-side keyword "Barbie" retrieved Barbie Forteza, Barbie Almalbis, Barbie Imperial and Barbie Hsu across dozens of candidates before the IP-name filter was tightened.
Any future PH sweep should treat "Barbie" as a low-precision term and require a Mattel/doll co-occurrence.

**Sites that could not be swept** (recorded as a method limit, not a coverage claim): the full 403/401/404 list above, `spot.ph` above all.
Brand, mall and creator Instagram/Facebook/TikTok accounts, which carry a very large share of PH campaign comms - and which is where PH illustrators overwhelmingly announce their own brand work - are entirely unswept.

**YEAR tag** is by campaign start/run date: `2024` = 2024-01-01..2024-12-31, `2025` = 2025-01-01..2025-12-31, `2026YTD` = 2026-01-01..run date.
Where a campaign was announced in one year and ran in the next, the year tag follows the run.

**IP class** = PORTFOLIO (owned/controlled by a major licensor, studio, media/platform conglomerate, or estate that manages OTHER IP) / INDEPENDENT (living-creator- or small-studio-owned, no conglomerate parent; agency-managed but creator-owned still counts - the Capoo precedent) / UNCLEAR (state why).

**REPRESENTABLE** (INDEPENDENT rows only) = yes / no / unclear: yes = creator-owned, no conglomerate stake, not locked to an exclusive agency/captive arrangement in a way that precludes representation (a plausible PBC client); no = indie by ownership but already exclusively tied up; unclear = can't tell from public sources.
Non-indie rows carry '-'.

## Philippines-specific rules

- **THE OWN-MASCOT EXCLUSION, and it bites harder here than in any market so far.**
Philippine QSR is built on proprietary mascots, and Jollibee is the canonical case.
A campaign where the brand's own mascot IS the character is **not a row**; a third-party character licensed INTO that brand's campaign **is** a row.
Applied: **Jollibee x Sanrio** (row 23) and **Jollibee x Mobile Legends: Bang Bang** (row 45) are rows because the third-party IP comes in; **Jollibee x Good Smile Company Nendoroid** is NOT a row, because there the Jollibee mascot is the licensed-out IP and Good Smile is the manufacturer.
The full exclusion list is in "Excluded own-mascot campaigns" below and is itself one of this market's findings.
- **Mall-operator density and the anti-inflation merge.** SM Supermalls, Ayala Malls, Robinsons and Megaworld Lifestyle Malls run character properties at national chain scale.
One campaign across several branches of one operator = **one row**, venues noted: the Pokémon Fun Day caravan across SM malls in Metro Manila, Rizal, Bulacan, Iloilo and Davao is row 22, not five rows; the Sanrio x Pokémon Keeppley pop-up across Eastwood Mall, Lucky Chinatown and Southwoods Mall is row 44, not three.
Mall-hosted properties ARE rows per the Taiwan-v2 row 126 precedent.
- **Art-toy retail: the licensor's own permanent store is NOT a row.**
POP MART's official stores at SM Megamall (Dec 2025), SM Aura (Jan 2026) and SM North EDSA (Feb 2026), and the Disney Store by SM permanent branches at SM Mall of Asia (Sep 2024) and SM North EDSA (Sep 2025), are all excluded as own-retail openings.
A POP MART or Disney property **hosted by a third-party venue as a limited-run pop-up or installation** IS a row (rows 18, 20, 31, 37, 40, 52), on the Singapore/Taiwan-v2 venue precedent.
- **Evidenced PH activation is required.** PH tech and lifestyle press carries a lot of global product news. A row needs named PH stores/malls, a PH launch date, PHP/₱ pricing, or .ph commerce.
Global announcements that explicitly say a PH launch has not been set are rejected and listed below.
- **Self-presentation is not ownership**, and the per-IP ownership test applies even where one creator sits on both sides (Molly Factory precedent).

## Known holes

1. `spot.ph` is unreachable at any URL from this client. It is the market's leading lifestyle title for exactly this beat.
2. About 990 of the 1,867 filtered candidates (everything at score >= 4) have been adjudicated across the two tranches. The ~879 unread remainder all sit at score 3, which is the `BRAND`-only and `COLLAB`-only tag classes - between them those classes produced **zero** rows in either tranche, so the marginal yield of reading them is low but not nil.
3. **BusinessMirror verification note.** All 117 distinct source URLs cited in this file verified at **HTTP 200 with the campaign body present**, in a single final all-117 pass with retry rounds (73 were first verified in the first tranche, 44 more added in the second).
The retry rounds are not optional, and the second tranche reproduced the first tranche's finding exactly. Reaching that took repeated passes on one publisher and the reason is worth recording, because a single pass would have produced a misleading "dead link" count. `businessmirror.com.ph` (24 of the 73 URLs) serves a **Cloudflare "Just a moment..." interstitial returning HTTP 403 to a randomly rotating subset of requests**. Three separate verification passes each failed on a *different* four-to-five BusinessMirror URLs - and each pass returned HTTP 200 for URLs that a prior pass had 403ed, including URLs re-checked minutes apart. Over four further retry rounds every one of the 24 resolved to 200. The 49 non-BusinessMirror URLs returned 200 on every pass.
