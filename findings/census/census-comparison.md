# Co-branding census - three-market comparison (Taiwan / Japan / Korea)

> **SUPERSEDED (2026-07-29) by [`FIVE_MARKET_COMPARISON.md`](FIVE_MARKET_COMPARISON.md).**
> This page is built on pre-re-sweep numbers (Taiwan 27.1% / Japan 30.5% / Korea 24.8%) and covers three markets.
> The artist-side keyword re-sweep of 2026-07-29 moved every one of those figures upward and added Malaysia and Thailand: the current numbers are Japan 34.9% / Korea 34.0% / Taiwan 27.5% / Malaysia 25.0% / Thailand 20.5%.
> Kept in place unedited for the audit trail. Do not quote figures from this page.

As of 2026-07-24.
This consolidates the three per-market co-branding censuses onto a single page.
All three were built on the **identical definition**: the same ownership test (who owns the IP, not who runs the brand), the same pinned edge cases (estate/deceased-creator, fine-artist, government-owned bodies, platform/webtoon ownership), and the same REPRESENTABLE sub-tag on every independent row.
Because the definition is shared, the markets compare cleanly - the differences below are real, not artifacts of three different rulebooks.

Sources, on record:

- Taiwan v2: `findings/census/taiwan-v2/_VERDICT.md` + `campaigns.md` (129 rows).
- Japan: `findings/census/japan/_VERDICT.md` + `campaigns.md` (131 rows).
- Korea: `findings/census/korea/_VERDICT.md` + `campaigns.md` (135 rows, 2 public-body rows excluded from the split -> classifiable base 133).

Every number on this page traces to one of those three verdicts.
Where the markets are not perfectly comparable, it is flagged inline and again in Method limits.

---

## Headline

**Market-entry read (the number PBC cares about):** across all three East-Asian markets there is a real, addressable pool of independent IP that PBC could plausibly represent - roughly **12-16% of all co-branding campaigns**, or about **one campaign in six-to-eight**.
By campaign volume that pool is largest in **Japan (16.0%, ~1 in 6)**, then **Korea (14.1%, ~1 in 7)**, then **Taiwan (11.6%, ~1 in 9)**.
But Taiwan's low volume figure is almost entirely a **single-IP artifact** - the country's breakout indie (Bugcat Capoo) is self-licensed through its own studio - and once you count by *distinct* indie IP rather than by campaign volume, Taiwan is the **most open** market (about 62% of its distinct indie IPs are representable).
So the honest one-line read is: **Japan has the broadest and deepest open bench, Taiwan has the highest open-rate per distinct IP but a shallow domestic pool with its one star already closed, and Korea has a concentrated but high-reach bench (7 creators) that the sampling window probably understates.**

**Market-structure read (the finding that makes the entry read defensible):** independent IP is a **stable ~quarter of co-branding in every market**, on the same definition, not a per-market quirk.
Overall indie share is Taiwan 27.1%, Japan 30.5%, Korea 24.8%.
In the best-sampled year (2025, the deepest slice in all three) the three markets converge almost exactly: **Taiwan 30.0% / Japan 26.7% / Korea 26.3%**.
That regional consistency - three independent builds landing in a ~5-point band - is itself the strongest evidence that "about a quarter of co-branding is indie" is a structural fact of East-Asian licensing, not a sampling accident.

## 2. The addressable read per market

Two ways to size the addressable pool, and they tell different stories.
**By volume** = REP=yes rows as a share of all campaigns (what fraction of co-branding activity is open indie).
**By distinct IP** = representable distinct indie IPs as a share of all distinct indie IPs (how open is the underlying roster of creators).

| Market | Addressable by volume | ~1 in | By distinct IP | What drives the "no" |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Taiwan** | 15 / 129 = **11.6%** | ~9 | **13 / 21 distinct = ~62%** | **Concentration in one IP.** 8 of the 17 REP=no rows are **Bugcat Capoo**, self-licensed via the creator's own captive studio 卡特島創意 (Carter Island). The rest are creator-owned-but-exclusively-tied: Kanahei (exclusive master licensor, 3), mofusand (Gray Parka Service, 2), 黃阿瑪/Fumeancats (own operation, 2), Kusama, LuLu the Piggy (52TOYS). |
| **Japan** | 21 / 131 = **16.0%** | ~6 | ~18 distinct addressable creators named (no clean total-distinct denominator reported) | **Captive studios / exclusive licensors.** The 9 REP=no rows are the viral SNS characters locked up: Opanchu Usagi (via Bandai), mofusand (Gray Parka Service), Colorful Peach (UUUM), Kaikai&Kiki (Murakami's captive studio), Heralbony's exclusive artist licensing, MOMOREI (locked to LINDA). |
| **Korea** | 19 / 135 = **14.1%** | ~7 | 7 distinct addressable creators | **Platform / conglomerate ownership.** The 8 REP=no rows are platform-controlled: Maru via Naver Webtoon (4), JOGUMAN locked to IPX's global business, Bbangbbangi captive to studio 더그림, Chiikawa committee-managed (2). |

**The market-entry signal, per market:**

- **Japan** offers the **most open indie IP by every volume measure** - the highest addressable share (16.0%) and the broadest bench of distinct open creators (individual fine-artists and illustrators doing non-exclusive brand/capsule collabs: Higuchi Yuko, Nakamura Yusuke, foxco, SHOGO SEKINE, KUNIKA, foxy, Oono Taro, and two multi-illustrator showcases that each front a dozen-plus more).
  Crucially, in Japan *fame does not equal representability*: the household-name viral characters are exactly the captive ones (REP=no), while the addressable pool is the quieter, deeper illustrator tier.
- **Taiwan** looks thinnest by volume (11.6%) but that is misleading - strip out the one self-licensed star (Capoo, 8 of the 17 "no" rows) and Taiwan is the **most open market by distinct IP (~62%)**.
  The catch is depth: the domestic representable bench is only ~5 open Taiwan creators (爽爽貓, 馬來貘 LAIMO, Monday Bruce, 露咖貓, plus 文博會-surfaced illustrators), with the other half of the addressable pool being inbound foreign indies (KR/JP/UK) that entered via drink chains and department-store pop-ups.
  So Taiwan is high-open-rate but shallow, and its single most valuable indie asset is already closed.
- **Korea** sits in the middle by volume (14.1%) but its open pool is **concentrated in just 7 creators** (망곰이 doing 8 of the 19 rows, 무직타이거 4).
  Two honest offsets: the "yes" calls are softer here (almost all addressable Korean creators are agency-managed with private exclusivity terms, so 14.1% is a *plausible-target* estimate), and the window *understates* their footprint - several of these creators ran their biggest institutional collabs (finance-card tie-ups) in 2021-2023, before the 2024-01 window opened.

**Which market has the most OPEN indie IP for PBC?**
By raw addressable volume and bench breadth: **Japan.**
By open-rate per distinct IP: **Taiwan** (but a shallow domestic pool, one star closed).
Korea is a concentrated, high-reach bench (it uniquely reaches pro-sports merch) that the window probably undercounts.

## 3. The consistency finding

This is the load-bearing structural fact.
On one shared definition, three independently built censuses land within a ~5-point band on indie share:

- **Overall:** Taiwan 27.1% / Japan 30.5% / Korea 24.8%.
- **Best-sampled year (2025):** Taiwan 30.0% / Japan 26.7% / Korea 26.3% - a ~4-point spread, effectively identical.
- **Portfolio majority is equally consistent:** ~69-71% in every market (Taiwan 69.0%, Japan 68.7%, Korea 70.7%).

Three separate builds, three different languages of source press, three different channel mixes, one answer: **indie IP is about a quarter to a third of co-branding, and portfolio IP is about two-thirds to seven-tenths, everywhere.**
That is a regional structural fact, not a per-market quirk - which is what lets PBC generalize the thesis across East Asia rather than pitch it one country at a time.

## 4. Standout big-brand indie examples (the anti-"shut out" evidence)

Every market produces indie IP landing marquee brands and flagship formats - the direct refutation of a hard "indies are shut out" reading.
Each row below is on-record in the market's `campaigns.md`.

**Taiwan:**

- **貓貓蟲咖波 Bugcat Capoo x 7-ELEVEN x 國立故宮博物院** - a solo illustrator's cat headlining a flagship 全店精品集點 wave across **7,100+ stores** alongside the National Palace Museum (taiwan-v2 row 51).
- **Capoo x 華南金控** (a listed financial holding company, shareholder-gift premium) and **x 中華郵政** (national postal service, with themed post offices) - indie IP on institutional/corporate premiums (rows 39, 32).
- **馬來貘 LAIMO x CeraVe** (L'Oréal-owned global beauty) and **星期一的布魯斯 Monday Bruce x 台北101 觀景台** (flagship exhibition).

**Japan:**

- **Higuchi Yuko (ヒグチユウコ) x Lawson AND x Morinaga** - a living, self-owned painter running co-branding with two national mass-market brands entirely as an independent artist (japan rows 6 + Morinaga row). The single cleanest refutation of "big brands only work with portfolio IP."
- **foxco x Calbee** (national snack maker, Potato Chips packaging).
- **foxy x CASETiFY** (global accessories) and **KUNIKA x PEACH JOHN** (national retail) - indie illustrators reaching global/national brands in 2025.

**Korea:**

- **무직타이거 (MUZIK TIGER) x Ulsan HD** - a creator-couple's self-owned studio IP on a **first-division K-League match-day kit**, plus **x SPAO** apparel (korea rows 21+). Korea's cleanest refutation.
- **망곰이 (Ruined Bear) x Olive Young** (national health-and-beauty chain, four separate brand lines) **AND x SPAO AND x CU AND x Doosan Bears** - one self-run emoticon IP across five national formats.

## 5. Cross-market thesis read

The founder thesis - **"indies are shut out"** - does not survive any of the three markets in its blunt form, but a precise version survives cleanly in all three.

**Refined thesis, per market:**

- **Taiwan REFINES it:** "indies are a substantial minority (~27%) and demonstrably win the highest-prestige formats (a listed FHC premium, the national post office, a 7,100-store 7-11 集點 wave, Taipei 101), but the single dominant domestic indie (Capoo) is self-licensed - so the addressable pool is real but thin by volume and concentrated in ~5 open creators plus an inbound-foreign channel."
- **Japan REFINES it:** the blunt "indies are a minor share" *survives* (30.5% overall, 26.7% in the best year; mass/platform formats - gacha, telecom points, card-skins, national vending - are near-monolithically corporate), but "indies are *shut out*" *weakens* - the barrier is **channel concentration**, not access. Indies live in lifestyle retail, illustrator apparel/stationery, CVS art capsules, and cosmetics packaging, and from there reach national and global brands.
- **Korea REFINES it, almost identically to Japan** - with one distinctive structural twist: Korea's portfolio wall is **dual**, built from *both* foreign licensors (Sanrio, Pokemon, Disney, plus new Netflix screen-IP: Squid Game, KPop Demon Hunters) *and* domestic platform owners (Kakao Friends, LINE Friends/IPX, Iconix's Loopy, SAMG's Teenieping, HYBE's TinyTAN). The decisive line is platform ownership: many of the most famous "Korean characters" (Ryan, Choonsik, Loopy, BT21) are **platform-owned = portfolio**, which is exactly why they do not swell the indie column.

**The single overall thesis read (defensible version):**
"Independent IP is a **stable ~quarter of East-Asian co-branding** and it demonstrably reaches the largest brands and most prestigious formats - so indies are *not shut out*.
What is true is that (a) portfolio IP owns the ~70% majority and near-monopolizes the high-volume, platform-like channels, and (b) the *addressable-by-PBC* slice is narrower than the raw indie share - roughly **12-16% of campaigns** - because a large part of indie IP is either self-licensed (Taiwan's Capoo), captive to a studio/exclusive licensor (Japan's viral SNS characters), or platform-owned (Korea's webtoon/emoticon-platform stars).
The recurring finding across all three markets is that **fame does not equal representability**: the household-name indies are the closed ones, and PBC's real market is the quieter, broader illustrator/creator tier - which is genuine, reaches national brands, and is largest and deepest in Japan."

## 6. Method limits

The same sample-census caveats apply to all three markets, plus market-specific ones.

**Shared caveats:**

- **Sample census, not exhaustive.** Each market is a structured sample (~130 curl-verified rows), not the full population; treat every share as a central estimate, not a population parameter.
- **Recall bias toward covered campaigns.** Rows come from language-specific lifestyle/food/beauty/fashion press and collab trackers; press-friendly campaigns are over-represented. Because indie collabs skew press-friendly (lifestyle retail, beauty, apparel), this bias if anything **inflates** the indie share slightly - the true indie share may sit a touch below the reported figure in each market.
- **Per-year bases are uneven.** 2025 is deep and representative everywhere (n=60/90/80); 2024 and 2026YTD are thin and format-skewed. Read the trend as "roughly flat at a quarter," and discount the 2024 outliers (Japan's 47.4% high, Korea's 16.7% low) as coverage artifacts.
- **Format skew is itself a finding.** The ratio describes co-branding *as surfaced in the channels sampled*. A differently weighted channel mix would move the number; the ~25% indie figure is the product of sampling both indie-leaning and portfolio-locked veins.
- **REPRESENTABLE is a judgment call, not a legal finding.** REP=yes/no reflects public signals about whether an IP is captive or open - directional market-entry guidance, not confirmation that any creator is contractually free. PBC should confirm case by case.

**Where the three markets are NOT perfectly comparable (flag these explicitly):**

- **Korea excluded 2 public-body rows** (Seoul-city Haechi, Daejeon-city Ggumdoli) from the split, so its classifiable base is **133, not 135**. Taiwan and Japan had no public-body IP to exclude (Taiwan's public *brands* all license privately owned IP). Korea's addressable-by-volume figure (19/135) uses the full 135 denominator per its own verdict, so it is very slightly conservative relative to a 133 base.
- **Taiwan v2 is a reclassify, not a re-find.** v2 re-scored and enriched the 129 v1 rows on the shared definition and added the REPRESENTABLE column; it did **not** run a fresh discovery sweep. So Taiwan inherits v1's coverage exactly - its classification and addressable read are comparable, but its *sample* was not re-widened the way a fresh build would be. (The re-scoring moved exactly 3 rows and left the 27.1% indie share unchanged, which is itself a robustness signal.)
- **Sampling channels differ by market.** Each build opened the veins most legible in that market's press (Taiwan: CVS 集點, drink chains, beauty; Japan: sushi/gyudon/gashapon/kuji/collab-cafe/lifestyle retail; Korea: Olive Young beauty, SPAO, KBO/K-League merch, CVS). The overall shares are comparable, but per-channel composition is not identical, so cross-market *channel-level* comparisons should be made with care.
- **UNCLEAR rates and their causes differ.** Japan's is near-zero (0.8%, well-documented © lines); Taiwan's is 3.9% (borderline brand-x-brand and unpinnable Korean character brands); Korea's is 4.5% and is driven almost entirely by the genuinely contestable **webtoon/platform ownership line** (creator authorship vs platform co-brand control) - reasonable analysts could move some Korean rows by one bucket.
- **REP=unclear is not uniform.** Japan carries the most REP=unclear (25% of indie rows) because exclusivity terms are rarely public there, so Japan's 16.0% addressable is a **conservative floor**; Korea's "yes" rows are a *plausible-target* estimate given agency-managed creators with private terms; and Korea's window (2024-01 on) misses several indies' biggest institutional collabs from 2021-2023, understating their true footprint.

**Net:** the indie-share reads (~a quarter everywhere) and the addressable reads (~12-16% of campaigns) are robust and comparable; the residual non-comparabilities above all push in known directions and none of them overturn the two headline findings.
