# Korea co-branding census - verdict

As of 2026-07-24 (first build, 135 rows).

This verdict reads the campaign table in `campaigns.md` (135 curl-verified brand-x-IP co-branding rows, window 2024-01-01 to run date).
It is a **sample census, not an exhaustive count** - see Section 5 for limits.
This is the KOREA run in a region-by-region series (Taiwan done: 129 rows at `findings/census/campaigns.md`; Japan done: 131 rows at `findings/census/japan/campaigns.md`), same definition and rules so the three markets are directly comparable.
The brief is to attack the founder thesis ("indies are shut out") as hard as the data allows; where the data undercuts the thesis, that is called out plainly.
**The completion target (120 curl-verified year-tagged rows across all three years) is met; the overall split and the deep 2025 slice are the reliable reads, while 2024 (n=25) is the thin, portfolio-skewed slice - see Section 5.**

Two rows are government/public-body IP (Seoul-city Haechi, Daejeon-city Ggumdoli) and are **excluded from the indie/portfolio split** per the pinned rule; they are noted separately, so the classifiable base is **n = 133** (135 minus 2 public).

## 1. The ratio - overall and per year

**Overall (classifiable n = 133; 2 public excluded):**

| Class | Count | Share |
|---|---|---|
| PORTFOLIO | 94 | 70.7% |
| INDEPENDENT | 33 | 24.8% |
| UNCLEAR | 6 | 4.5% |

**Per year (public excluded from each denominator):**

| Year | n | PORTFOLIO | INDEPENDENT | UNCLEAR | Indie share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2024 (full year) | 24 | 18 | 4 | 2 | **16.7%** |
| 2025 (full year) | 80 | 56 | 21 | 3 | **26.3%** |
| 2026YTD (to 07-24) | 29 | 20 | 8 | 1 | **27.6%** |

**Trend read:** the indie share rises across the window - 16.7% (2024) -> 26.3% (2025) -> 27.6% (2026YTD) - but the 2024 figure should be discounted, not read as a genuine trough.
2024 is the thinnest slice (n=24) and was sampled last (finance cards, snack packaging, hotels), veins that are near-monolithically portfolio; the indie-leaning veins (Olive Young beauty rollouts, SPAO apparel, KBO club merch, indie-illustrator pop-ups) are concentrated in 2025-2026, so 2024 is under-weighted on indie-friendly formats rather than genuinely indie-poor.
2025 is by far the deepest and most representative slice (n=80, sampled across the widest format spread - CVS, cafe, beauty, apparel, pop-up, sports, snack, finance) and lands at **26.3% indie**, essentially identical to Japan's best-sampled-year read (26.7%) and Taiwan's complete-2025 read.
The honest synthesis: **indie share sits at about a quarter of Korean co-branding campaigns (24.8% overall, 26.3% in the best-sampled year), flat-to-slightly-growing, not a small share and not a majority.**

## 2. The addressable number (the market-entry read)

Of the 33 INDEPENDENT rows, the REPRESENTABLE tag splits:

| REPRESENTABLE | Count | Share of indie rows |
|---|---|---|
| yes | 19 | 57.6% |
| no | 8 | 24.2% |
| unclear | 6 | 18.2% |

**The addressable-indie number: 19 of 135 campaigns (14.1%) are REPRESENTABLE = yes** - creator-owned, no conglomerate stake, and not locked to an exclusive agency/platform in a way that would preclude PBC representation.
That is the market-entry signal: about **one campaign in seven** in the Korea co-branding sample is run with an indie IP that PBC could plausibly represent - close to Japan's one-in-six (16.0%).
Just under 60% of all indie co-branding activity (57.6%) is addressable; the rest is indie-by-ownership but already closed off - either platform/conglomerate-controlled (8 rows: Maru's licensing runs through Naver Webtoon x4; JOGUMAN locked to IPX's exclusive global business; Bbangbbangi captive to studio 더그림; Chiikawa committee-managed x2) or genuinely unreadable on exclusivity (6 rows: Dinotaeng x4 under IPX with ambiguous exclusivity, Goong manhwa, Kwakcheori via Colley).

**Caveat on "yes":** in Korea the addressable creators are almost all agency-managed (HNF, KBvision, own studios, KakaoTalk-emoticon licensing), and precise exclusivity terms are rarely public. "yes" here means *creator-owned, no conglomerate stake, and licensing openly/non-exclusively across many brands* (the PBC target profile per the brief) - not a verified open mandate. So 14.1% is a **plausible-target estimate**, with the 6 unclear rows as possible additional upside.

## 3. Indie prominence - breadth or a few recycled names?

The 19 REPRESENTABLE=yes rows resolve to **7 distinct addressable creators** - real breadth, but with two names doing much of the volume:

- **망그러진곰 / 망곰이** (creator 유랑/Yurang) - **8 rows**, the single most prominent addressable indie: Olive Young ecosystem (Etude, fwee, isoi, peripera), SPAO apparel, CU convenience, Doosan Bears (KBO), and the Olive Young Hongdae pop-up. A self-run emoticon-origin IP reaching national retail across five formats.
- **무직타이거 / 뚱랑이** (STUDIO MUZIK, creator couple) - **4 rows**: SPAO, Ulsan HD (K-League football), Dongkook Pharma, Anseong City. Own studio with a self-licensing portal.
- **가나디** (짤쓸사람, HNF-managed) - 2 rows: CU banana-milk drinks, COSRX skincare.
- **최고심** (solo illustrator) - 2 rows: LG Twins (KBO), Korea Eundan vitamins.
- **슈야** (성지민 / 크림퍼프스튜디오) - 1 row: Monami stationery.
- **깜자** (KakaoTalk-emoticon artist) - 1 row: SSG Landers (KBO).
- **웜그레이테일** (illustrator) - 1 row: Sockstaz socks.

**Do indies land big-brand collabs? Yes, repeatedly** - the strongest counter-evidence to a hard "indies are shut out" thesis:
- 망곰이 x **Olive Young** (national health-and-beauty chain, four separate brand product lines) AND x **SPAO** (national apparel) AND x **CU** (national convenience chain).
- 무직타이거 x **SPAO** and x **Ulsan HD** (a first-division K-League club match-day kit) - an indie IP on a professional football uniform.
- 최고심 x **LG Twins** and 깜자 x **SSG Landers** - two KBO baseball clubs putting independent creator characters on official uniforms and merch.
- 가나디 x **CU** (national convenience drinks) and x **COSRX** (globally-exported K-beauty brand).

So the refined picture is not "indies are excluded" but **"indies are a stable ~quarter of campaigns (~14% addressable) that DO reach the largest Korean brands - concentrated in beauty (Olive Young), apparel (SPAO), convenience, and, distinctively for Korea, KBO/K-League sports-club merch."**

## 4. The thesis read

**Korea REFINES the founder thesis, and does so almost identically to Japan.**

- The blunt thesis "indies are a minor share of co-branding" **survives**: at 24.8% overall and 26.3% in the best-sampled year, indie IP is clearly the minority, and the high-volume/platform-like formats are near-monolithically corporate. Finance/card, telecom, travel/hospitality, bakery/dessert, mobile-game, snack/beverage packaging, and premium eyewear came back essentially 100% portfolio on targeted search.
- The stronger thesis "indies are **shut out** / can't reach big brands" **weakens** on this data - see the Section 3 counter-examples. The barrier is not access; it is **channel concentration**: Korean indies live in beauty (the Olive Young rollout machine), illustrator apparel (SPAO), convenience-store drinks/food, KBO/K-League club merch, and creator pop-ups - and are largely absent from the corporate-premium and screen-IP-driven channels.
- **The distinctive Korea structure is a DUAL portfolio wall.** Unlike Taiwan/Japan, Korea's portfolio majority is built from *two* stacks: (a) foreign licensors (Sanrio, San-X, Pokemon, Nintendo, Disney, Toei/Shueisha/Kodansha, and - new in 2024-26 - **Netflix screen IP**: Squid Game, KPop Demon Hunters recurring across HiteJinro, Nongshim, Ottogi, Paris Baguette), and (b) domestic platform/media owners (**Kakao Friends, LINE Friends/IPX, Iconix's Loopy/Pororo, SAMG's Teenieping/Wish Cat, HYBE's TinyTAN, Devsisters' Cookie Run**). The platform-ownership line is decisive: many of the most famous "Korean characters" (Ryan, Choonsik, Loopy, BT21) are **platform-owned = portfolio**, which is exactly why they do NOT swell the indie column.
- **Fame does not equal representability in Korea** - the same finding as Japan. The biggest-name Korean-origin viral characters split two ways: the platform/conglomerate-owned ones (Loopy/Iconix, Kakao & LINE Friends, Teenieping/SAMG) are portfolio, while the genuinely addressable pool is the individual emoticon/illustrator tier (망곰이, 무직타이거, 최고심, 가나디, 깜자) doing open non-exclusive brand collabs. PBC's addressable market is that second group, and it is real and reaches national brands.

**Strongest single counter-example to quote:** 무직타이거 - a creator-couple's self-owned studio IP - put its character on a **first-division K-League match-day kit (Ulsan HD)** and on **SPAO** apparel as an independent studio. That one creator refutes "big brands and pro sports only collaborate with portfolio IP."

## 5. Method limits (stated plainly)

- **Sample census, not exhaustive.** 135 curl-verified rows is a structured sample of Korea co-branding 2024-01-01..2026-07-24, not the full population. Treat the ~1-in-4-indie / ~1-in-7-addressable reads as central estimates from this sample, not precise population parameters.
- **Recall bias toward covered/searchable campaigns.** Rows come from ko lifestyle/beauty/brand press, brand-official releases, and collab trackers. Press-friendly campaigns are over-represented; because Korean indie collabs skew toward press-heavy beauty/apparel/sports formats, this bias if anything **inflates** the indie share slightly - the true indie share may be a little below 25%.
- **Per-year bases are uneven.** 2025 (n=80) is deep and representative; 2024 (n=24) is thin and portfolio-skewed (sampled late, on finance/snack/hotel veins) and 2026YTD (n=29) is a partial year. The apparent 2024 "trough" (16.7%) is discounted as a coverage artifact; read the indie trend as "roughly a quarter, flat-to-slightly-rising."
- **Format skew is real and is itself a finding.** The ratio describes co-branding *as surfaced in these channels*. Deliberately opening indie-leaning veins (Olive Young beauty, SPAO, KBO clubs, creator pop-ups) and portfolio-locked veins (finance/telecom, travel, snack/beverage, mobile-game) is what produces the ~25% indie figure; a differently-weighted channel mix would move it.
- **The hardest call in Korea is the webtoon/platform ownership line, and it drives the entire UNCLEAR bucket.** 6 of 133 (4.5%) are IP-class UNCLEAR - all webtoon/MCN/platform borderline cases (Yumi's Cells & Garbage Time co-branded cards where the creator retains authorship but Naver Webtoon controls the co-brand license; Pixelri under Sandbox Network; SSEBONGRAMA co-created under IPX; 춘배/냐한남자). The unclear rate is low because most calls were resolvable, but this specific creator-vs-platform-control split is where classification is genuinely contestable, and reasonable analysts could move some of these UNCLEAR/PORTFOLIO/INDEPENDENT rows by one bucket.
- **REPRESENTABLE is a softer judgment than IP class.** 18% of indie rows are REP=unclear because exclusivity/agency-lock status is rarely public; and the "yes" rows are a *plausible-target* estimate (creator-owned + no conglomerate + open multi-brand licensing), not verified open mandates. The addressable number (19 / 14.1%) should be read as a floor-ish central estimate.
- **Window-boundary caveat (important for the addressable read).** Several of the most clearly-representable indies ran their biggest institutional collabs *before* the 2024-01 window: 우리카드 x 다이노탱 (2021), IBK x 무직타이거 (2021), 우리카드 x 망그러진곰 (2023), 신한카드 x 최고심/잔망루피 (2022). Indie-x-finance co-branding was more common in 2021-2023 than the in-window count shows, so the true addressable footprint of these creators is somewhat larger than this window captures.
- **Single-source rows are flagged in the campaigns.md Notes column** (e.g. the five Spring-2026 hotel packages resting on one Businesskorea roundup; several brand press releases). Fetch failures logged for future passes: namu.wiki / theqoo / popply / sweetspot / nate list-pages returned 403 or JS-only and were worked around via alternate outlets or brand-official pages. Phantom (unverifiable) entries are the known failure class and were kept out.
