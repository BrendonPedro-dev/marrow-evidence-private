# Singapore co-branding census - verdict

Market six in the region-by-region series (Taiwan v2, Japan, Korea, Malaysia, Thailand precede it).
Identical definition, identical ownership test, identical pinned edge cases, so this market compares row-for-row with the other five.
Table: `findings/census/singapore/campaigns.md`.
Run date 2026-07-29.
**n = 81 curl-verified rows.**

Singapore is the first market in the series to run **both keyword classes - brand-side and artist-side - from birth**.
There was no re-sweep, because there was nothing to retrofit.
That makes Singapore's indie number the series' first number that was never corrected upward, and it makes its mechanism read (section 4) a clean measurement rather than a delta.

## 1. The ratio

**Overall - the quotable numbers:**

| Class | Rows | Share |
|---|---|---|
| PORTFOLIO | 74 | **91.4%** |
| INDEPENDENT | 7 | **8.6%** |
| UNCLEAR | 0 | 0.0% |

Zero UNCLEAR rows is worth stating plainly: every IP in this table resolved to a documented owner.
That is a property of the market, not of the effort - Singapore's licensed-character campaigns front IP owned by Sanrio, Disney, POP MART, The Pokémon Company, Shueisha and Warner Bros., all of which publish their ownership.

**Per year** (the per-year-slices caveat carries: 2024 has only 13 rows and 2026 is seven months, so **the overall shares above are the quotable numbers** and the per-year splits are directional only):

| Year | Rows | PORTFOLIO | INDEPENDENT | Indie share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2024 | 13 | 12 | 1 | 7.7% |
| 2025 | 43 | 39 | 4 | 9.3% |
| 2026YTD | 25 | 23 | 2 | 8.0% |

The indie share is flat across all three years inside a band far narrower than the sampling noise on a 13-row slice.
There is no trend here to read, and the flatness is the point: Singapore's indie share does not move because it is not being suppressed by a coverage artefact that eases over time.
It is low because the underlying layer is thin (section 4).

**Where Singapore sits in the series.** At 8.6% this is the lowest independent share of the six markets, below Thailand's post-re-sweep figure and far below Korea (34.0%) and Japan.
Singapore is a *licensing-import* market: a dense, high-frequency consumer-campaign economy running almost entirely on imported portfolio IP.
The density is real - 81 rows from five consumer portals in 31 months, in a country of six million - but the IP is overwhelmingly Japanese, American and Chinese.

Seven INDEPENDENT rows, resolving to **four distinct independent IPs**:

| mofusand | 14, 34 | **no** | Created and owned by illustrator ぢゅの; agency-managed by Gray Parka Service (pinned, Japan rows 11/22/85) | Indie by ownership, exclusively channelled through Gray Parka Service - the pinned REP=no class |
| Play Nation (Kopi & Toast, Abalone Market) | 32, 60 | **unclear** | Singapore company (PN Play Store / Play Nation) creating its own toy and collectible lines; no conglomerate parent identified in the article text or on playnation.com.sg | The property is a food-plushie line rather than a named character cast, and no licensing or representation arrangement is public |
| Mr. Merlion & Friends | 33, 75 | **unclear** | Singapore character brand trading as @mrmerlion_official; described in the SimplyGo source as "locally loved" characters licensed *in*, so not a client's in-house mascot; no corporate parent identified | Ownership is clear enough to bucket as independent; the exclusivity question has no public answer |

**Distinct addressable (REP=yes) creator count: 1.**

That is the number, and it should be quoted as-is rather than softened.
One Singaporean creator - Tobyato - appears in this census on a brand collaboration under an arrangement that public sources show to be non-exclusive.

**The realistic ceiling is 3, not 1.** If Play Nation and Mr. Merlion & Friends resolve to non-exclusive on inspection - and nothing in the sources suggests otherwise - the distinct addressable count is 3 and the addressable row count is 5 of 7.
Both are Singapore-owned properties running venue-scale campaigns with FairPrice, Marina Square, Bugis+, Changi Airport and SimplyGo, which is exactly the profile that matters.
**Resolving these two ownership questions is the highest-value follow-up in this market**, and it is a two-document job (ACRA filings, or a direct ask) rather than another sweep.

**REP split within the indie rows:** yes 1 (14.3%), no 2 (28.6%), unclear 4 (57.1%).
The unclear share is the highest in the series, which is a straightforward consequence of Singapore's indie rows being carried by *young local companies* rather than by *named individual illustrators*: a company's exclusivity arrangements are private in a way an individual artist's brand-collab history is not.

**Physical venue formats are the Singapore signature: 31 of 81 rows (38.3%)** are a mall pop-up, an airport activation, an attraction takeover or a touring pop-up.
Add themed cafés and it is 39 of 81 (48.1%) - nearly half the market.
Singapore runs character IP as *footfall infrastructure*: Changi Airport four times, Wong Fu Fu four times, Bugis+ three times, Aniplus Café three times, Suntec City three times, VivoCity twice, Marina Square twice, and Plaza Singapura, Jewel, Great World, Wisma Atria, The Star Vista, Tampines Mall, United Square, Singapore Flyer, Resorts World Sentosa and Universal Studios Singapore once or more each.
No other market in the series concentrates this heavily in venue formats.

**Year trend** (grouped, non-overlapping, each column sums to that year's n):

| Format group | 2024 (n=13) | 2025 (n=43) | 2026YTD (n=25) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Venue + mall + café | 5 (38.5%) | 21 (48.8%) | 13 (52.0%) |
| F&B product (QSR + BBT) | 2 (15.4%) | 10 (23.3%) | 5 (20.0%) |
| Retail / apparel / FMCG + CVS | 6 (46.2%) | 8 (18.6%) | 4 (16.0%) |
| Loyalty, platform, banking | 0 | 4 (9.3%) | 3 (12.0%) |

Two directional movements, both to be read against thin year slices:
1. **Experiential is displacing shelf.** Retail, apparel, FMCG and CVS formats fall from 46.2% of 2024 to 16.0% of 2026YTD, while venue/mall/café rises from 38.5% to 52.0%.
The 2024 slice is 13 rows, so this is a signal to check rather than a measured trend - but it is consistent with the qualitative picture, in which SG malls and Changi compete on rotating IP tenancy.
2. **Loyalty, platform and banking appear only from 2025.** OCBC x Zootopia, Grab x Sanrio, Shopee x POP MART, SimplyGo x Mr. Merlion & Friends and Aleta x Sanrio all fall in 2025-26; 2024 has none.
Character IP entering SG financial, e-commerce and transit loyalty programmes is a genuinely new format in this window and it is where the one independently-owned SG property reached its largest brand (row 75).

## 4. Methodology and edge cases

### 4a. Which of the four mechanisms Singapore exhibits

The four-mechanisms taxonomy from `FIVE_MARKET_COMPARISON.md` s2 predicted Singapore would behave either like Taiwan (press names creators, so the artist-side class adds nothing - **none of the above**) or like Japan (artist-named campaigns invisible to brand queries - **channel**).

**Singapore returns the Taiwan-class null - the artist-side keyword class added exactly one row (+1.2 points) - but for the opposite reason.**

Taiwan's null is a *coverage* null: zh-TW trade press embeds 插畫家 and 原創IP in ordinary brand-side copy, so the two query classes return overlapping result sets and there is nothing left for the artist-side class to find.
Singapore's null is a *supply* null.
The two query classes do not overlap here at all - the artist-side class returns a completely different corpus - but that corpus is not brand-collaboration coverage.
Running "local artist", "Singapore artist", "illustrator", "homegrown", "designer toy" and "art toy" across the five veins returned: portrait-commission listings, gallery and museum shows, art-fair and comic-convention previews, career profiles of working artists, and international creator news.
It returned exactly one brand x local-illustrator campaign in 31 months (row 3, Omnidesk x Tobyato, 2024).

What the artist-side class *did* surface, and this is the substantive finding, is a set of **Singaporean creator-owned character businesses that appear in the press as founder profiles rather than as licensing partners**: Tasty Toastys (Chanel Lee - "lifestyle and character brand", whimsical toast characters, moved from NFTs into merchandising), Tap Space (Danial Sim - transport-themed character merch, queues and same-day sell-outs), and the two properties that did make the table, Play Nation and Mr. Merlion & Friends.
Every one of them monetises through **its own retail and its own pop-ups**, not by licensing a character into someone else's campaign.

So the honest mechanism statement for Singapore is:

> **Neither erasure nor segregation.
> Closest to "none of the above", but by a distinct route: direct-to-consumer substitution.** Singapore's creator-owned character layer exists and is commercially visible, but it routes to consumers through its own storefronts and pop-ups instead of through brand licensing.
> There is little creator attribution to erase in brand-side coverage because there are few creator-licensed brand campaigns to attribute.
> The two indie IPs that *do* appear in this table (Play Nation, Mr. Merlion & Friends) were found by **brand-side** queries - venue and retailer names - not by artist-side ones, which is the exact inverse of Thailand.

This is a provisional read, and the honest limit on it is named in section 5: Marketing-Interactive, Singapore's main marketing trade title, could not be swept.
If any vein would carry creator-attributed brand campaigns invisible to consumer portals, that is the one, and a channel or erasure correction remains possible from it.
What can be said now is that **the consumer-facing record - the one a Singaporean shopper actually sees - contains one brand x local-illustrator campaign in 31 months.**

### 4b. The regional-HQ trap - the rejections that matter most

Singapore hosts SEA regional headquarters, so the market's specific failure mode is counting an announcement made *from* Singapore as a campaign run *in* Singapore.
The rule applied: a row requires evidenced SG activation - named SG outlets, an SG launch date, an SG venue, S$ pricing, or .sg commerce.

**Rejected under this rule** (full list in `campaigns.md`, section A of the rejections):
- **JFEC / JFOODO x Hello Kitty** (2024-10) - a Japanese export-promotion campaign carried by an SG title with no SG retail, venue or date.
- **Mister Donut x Pokémon** (2025-10) - the source states the launch is in Japan.
- **CASETiFY x Doraemon** (2024-03) and **CASETiFY x Friends** (2024-09) - global drops from a Hong Kong company, no SG channel or price named.
- **Levi's x Gundam SEED** (2024-01) - no SG stockist, date or price.
- **SHEGLAM x Hello Kitty** (2025-02) - a Shein-brand global launch reported by an SG outlet with no SG activation.
- **Genshin Impact x POP MART** (2024-08) - explicitly China-exclusive.
- In-game-only crossovers reported by SG outlets (e.g. Overwatch x Sanrio, 2026-02).

**Retained despite being regional:** row 61, Starbucks x Harry Potter (2026-03), which names Singapore explicitly among 12 activating APAC markets with a launch date.
The test is activation evidence, not campaign geography.
Applying it in both directions is what makes the rejection list credible.

The trap is real and it is bigger than the seven rejections above: an EN-language SG outlet reporting a global brand's global drop is the single most common false positive in this market, and roughly a fifth of raw brand-side keyword hits fell to it or to the out-of-market rule (section G - Johor Bahru, Bangkok, Shanghai, Tokyo, Da Nang, Penang, KL, all of which TheSmartLocal covers under an SG masthead).

### 4c. Edge cases applied

- **Government-owned mascots (the Kumamon rule) - and why it did not bite.** The rule tests the *IP* side.
Singapore's state-owned mascots (Singa the Courtesy Lion and the statutory-board campaign mascots) produced no candidate row in this sweep; had one appeared it would have been excluded.
What Singapore *did* produce is the mirror case: **state-adjacent brands licensing third-party IP**, which is a normal row.
Row 36 (National Library Board x Star Wars, a pop-up library at Changi T3 built with Disney) and row 75 (SimplyGo x Mr. Merlion & Friends, a transit-payments loyalty campaign) are both rows, flagged in Notes.
Row 50 (Singapore Red Cross x POP MART, a blood-donation incentive) is the same call with a humanitarian body on the brand side.
Excluding these would have applied the Kumamon rule to the wrong column.
- **Label-captive indies (the LuLu the Piggy precedent).** Applied to mofusand, rows 14 and 34: creator-owned by illustrator ぢゅの, exclusively channelled through Gray Parka Service, therefore INDEPENDENT with REP=no.
This is the pinned Japan call carried forward unchanged.
Singapore's art-toy retail scene was expected to produce more of this class and did not - POP MART properties dominate it, and those are portfolio by the pinned rule.
- **Licensor's own retail excluded; venue-hosted properties are rows** (the Taiwan-v2 row 126 precedent).
POP MART's SG stores, 52TOYS' first SG outlet, MINISO Land PLQ, Pokémon Center Singapore itself, HoYoFest, D23 Asia and Pop Toy Show are all excluded.
POP MART or Disney Store properties *hosted by a third-party venue* are rows: row 12 (Disney Store at SG Comic Con), row 41 (Labubu Mini Market at VivoCity), row 46 (POP LAND at Bugis+), row 65 (Disney plush at Bugis+), row 67 (DisneyStore.sg at Great World), row 20 (Pokémon Mart at Tampines Mall / Isetan).
- **Anti-inflation merges.** Row 15 is the CRYBABY "Crying For Love" run across five CapitaLand malls - **one row, five venues named**.
Row 73 is Disney "Dessert Besties" across four Allgreen malls - **one row**.
Row 33 is Mr Merlion Hawker Fest across Bugis+ and Changi T3 - **one row**.
Row 44 folds CHAGEE's Hacipupu product launch and its Star Vista pop-up into one campaign.
Row 34 folds Starbucks x mofusand's two merch waves.
Row 42, 56, 67, 75 and 78 each carry a second or third source rather than a second row.
Unmerged, these would have added 13 rows for no new campaigns.
- **The per-IP ownership test even when one creator sits on both sides (the Molly Factory precedent).** Applied at row 54: MOLLY is created by Hong Kong artist Kenny Wong, and the source says so, but the IP is owned and commercialised by POP MART, so the row is PORTFOLIO.
Authorship is not ownership.
- **Self-presentation is not ownership.** Applied to tokidoki (row 26): a creator-founded brand that self-presents through Simone Legno's authorship, but tokidoki LLC is a corporate lifestyle brand holding a multi-character portfolio, so the row is PORTFOLIO on the corporate-ownership test.
Applied in the other direction to Mr. Merlion & Friends and Play Nation: neither self-presents as an artist property, and both are independent on the ownership test anyway.
- **A brand's own plushie line is not a character IP row - the single largest rejection class in this market.** Singapore's 2025-26 "food plushie" wave (MILO, KitKat, Magnolia, Skippy, Tai Sun, Gardenia, Prima Flour, Pizza Hut, Genki Sushi, McDonald's Prosperity Pals and Pocket Pouch, Toast Box x Horlicks, LiHO's own plushies, Haidilao, Beutea, CHAGEE Bestea, Yeo's x FairPrice zodiac) runs to more than twenty campaigns, all excluded because no third-party character licensor is involved.
**This matters for how the 8.6% is read**: Singapore has an enormous collectible-plushie appetite that is being met by brands' own in-house design rather than by licensing a creator's character.
That is the commercial shape of the same finding as section 4a.

## 5. Coverage-confidence statement

**Depth: adequate for the ratio, thin per row.**

- **n = 81** against a stated floor of ~80 and a stretch target of 120-140.
The floor is met; the stretch target is not, and the reason is named below rather than absorbed.
- **73 of 81 rows are single-source (90.1%).** This is the highest single-source rate in the series and it is the main confidence limit on this table.
It is a structural consequence of the vein set: Singapore's five reachable consumer portals cover a campaign once each and rarely cover the same campaign twice, so corroboration is the exception.
The 8 multi-source rows (30, 34, 42, 44, 56, 67, 75, 78) are the ones where two portals happened to overlap.
- **Every one of the 95 source URLs in the table was re-verified at HTTP 200 on 2026-07-29**, each with a machine-readable `datePublished` in the page's JSON-LD.
No row rests on an inferred date.
No outcome claims appear anywhere in the table.
- **Zero UNCLEAR ownership rows.** The ownership half of the table is the strongest part of it.

**Named holes - what would move the numbers:**

1. **Marketing-Interactive is unswept, and it is the biggest hole.** Singapore's principal marketing trade title serves a JS-rendered search that returns no article links to `curl` and exposes no WP REST endpoint.
This is the vein most likely to carry agency-side and creator-attributed campaign detail.
**The mechanism finding in section 4a is provisional on this**: a channel or erasure correction from that title is possible and would raise the indie share.
Nothing else in this run would move the headline number as much.
2. **Brand and mall social accounts are entirely unswept.** Instagram and Facebook carry the primary campaign comms for SG malls and F&B chains - several rows above rest on a portal's reproduction of the brand's own IG post.
A social sweep would add rows across all three years, most likely in the mall and BBT formats.
3. **Mothership and The Straits Times are blocked** (403 on `/wp-json`, `?s=` ignored, JS-only search respectively).
Both cover consumer campaigns.
So do SethLui, Daniel Food Diary, TimeOut Singapore, AugustMan and HoneyKids Asia, all of which returned 403/404 to `curl`.
**Five reachable veins out of roughly a dozen candidate titles is the main reason n is 81 rather than 130.**
4. **The artist-side vein needs a second class of source before the section 4a read is final.** The five portals swept are consumer-lifestyle titles.
Design and illustration press, artist Instagram accounts, Singapore Art Book Fair and Pop Toy Show exhibitor rosters, and the local designer-toy shops are all unswept - and if the indie layer is licensing-facing anywhere, it is there.
5. **Two ownership questions are open and cheap to close**: Play Nation and Mr. Merlion & Friends.
Resolving them moves the distinct-addressable count from 1 to as many as 3 - a threefold move on the number this census exists to produce, on two documents.
6. **2024 is thin at 13 rows** versus 43 for 2025.
Some of this is real (the SG blind-box and pop-up boom is a 2025 phenomenon and the sources say so), but some is archive depth - the portals' 2024 coverage is sparser and their search relevance ranking favours recent posts.
The 2024 slice should not be quoted on its own.

**The honest read.** The 91.4 / 8.6 split is trustworthy as a description of *Singapore's consumer-facing licensed-character economy*: dense, venue-led, high-frequency, and running almost entirely on imported portfolio IP.
It is a weaker description of *Singapore's creator economy*, because one of the two veins that would document creator-side licensing (the marketing trade press) is unreachable and the other (design and artist press) is unswept.
The direction of any correction is one-way - upward on the indie share - and the ceiling on it is bounded by the fact that ten artist-side keywords across five portals over 31 months surfaced exactly one brand x local-illustrator campaign.
