# Taiwan co-branding census - verdict

As of 2026-07-22 (second extension sweep, same day).

This verdict reads the campaign table in `campaigns.md` (58 curl-verified brand-x-IP co-branding rows, window 2024-01-01 to run date).
It is a **sample census, not an exhaustive count** - see Section 4 for limits.
The brief is to attack the founder thesis ("indies are shut out") as hard as the data allows; where the data undercuts the thesis, that is called out plainly.

**Trend read:** across the two complete years the indie share sits at **30% (2024) and 36% (2025) - call it roughly 1-in-3, with 2025 slightly above 2024**.
This is lower than the ~40% the previous 39-row read showed, and the direction of the correction is informative: this sweep deliberately added the mass-retail formats (fast food, convenience-store gaming tie-ins, conveyor-sushi gacha, more 7-11 archive waves), and those formats are portfolio-heavy.
The indie number moves with format coverage: premiums/postal/events pull it up, mass-retail chains pull it down.
2026YTD again shows a lower indie share (15%), but the 2026 sample is a partial year, leans on roundup clusters, and carries all 4 UNCLEAR rows; treat it as thin, not as evidence of decline.

Honest statement for the deck: **in the complete years 2024-2025, roughly 1 in 3 identifiable Taiwan co-branding campaigns used an independent, creator-owned IP; corporate portfolios took about 2 in 3, and the indie share ticked up from 30% to 36% between the two complete years.**
Do not cite 2026YTD alone as a trend.

**Do indies get big-brand collabs or only small ones?** The sample says clearly: they get big ones, and institutional ones.

- 咖波 alone has landed a top-tier drink chain (清心福全), a major international restaurant chain (Sushiro, six themed stores), **the national postal service (中華郵政 - stamps plus themed post offices)**, **a 7,100-store 7-ELEVEN 全店集點 wave fronting the 故宮 100th-anniversary tie-in**, and **a major listed financial holding company (華南金控 shareholder gift)**.

**Breadth caveat:** the indie bench is still concentrated - 咖波 is 5 of 16 indie rows, and the eight IPs are all established, marquee names (multi-year LINE-sticker era stars).
No newcomer indie IP appears in the sample; prominence accrues to a proven few.
Supporting (not counted, IP-x-IP): LuLu the Piggy x 咖波 ran a joint product line from 2026-06 (cava.tw/265108; gnn.gamer.com.tw/detail.php?sn=304779).

## 3. The thesis read

**Verdict: the data does NOT support "indies are shut out" - it supports "indies are a solid minority (~1-in-3 in complete years), concentrated in a proven few names," and each successive sweep has produced stronger indie counter-examples even as the headline share settled lower.**

- The blunt thesis ("indies can't get deals / can't reach big brands") is **contradicted** at every tier the sample covers: national retail (Kanahei, 白爛貓/我不是胖虎, 咖波 and noodoll all inside 7-ELEVEN 集點 waves), major chains (咖波 x Sushiro, 清心福全; LuLu x 麥味登; 爽爽貓 x 丸亀製麵), public institutions (咖波 x 中華郵政 and x 故宮; 白爛貓 x Makung festival), transit infrastructure (Kanahei x 悠遊卡), and listed corporates (咖波 x 華南金; 黃阿瑪 x 富采 twice).
- The defensible refined thesis has two parts.
  **(a) Share:** portfolios take ~2-in-3 of campaigns overall, and they dominate the mass-market chain formats: every fast-food collab logged (麥當勞 x Minecraft/TinyTAN/Mario, 肯德基 x 航海王), every conveyor-sushi gacha (藏壽司 x 柯南 twice, x 吉伊卡哇), and every convenience-store gaming tie-in (全家 x 原神/寶可夢/FFXIV) is portfolio IP.
  Where a campaign must move national volume on a licensed-media fanbase, brands buy portfolio.
  **(b) Bench:** indie access concentrates in ~8 marquee creator IPs; the data says the door is open to proven indies, not to indies as a class.
- Strongest quotes for the counter-thesis: the 7-ELEVEN x 咖波 x 故宮100周年 全店集點 across 7,100+ stores (talkacemedia.com/article/42024; ec.ltn.com.tw/article/breakingnews/5220187), 咖波 x 壽司郎's national six-themed-store campaign, and 華南金's 2026 shareholder gift press cycle (money.udn.com/money/story/5613/9412556).
- Sharpest fact **for** the portfolio-moat argument, updated: it is no longer "7-11 集點 is portfolio-locked" (that fell this sweep); it is that **the fast-food / gacha / gaming tie-in machine - the highest-volume, highest-frequency co-branding formats - ran 100% portfolio in this sample (10 of 10 rows)**.

Net: pitch this as **"the door is open but the bench is thin"** - a ~1-in-3 indie share in complete years (rising 30%→36%), carried by a handful of proven names that now reach even flagship 7-11 waves and national institutions, while the mass-media tie-in formats remain fully portfolio.
That framing is both honest to the data and stronger for a licensing-infrastructure pitch than "indies are shut out": the opportunity is widening the bench, not breaking down a wall.

## 4. Method limits (stated plainly)

- **Sample, not census.** 58 rows from a curated sweep of zh-TW press, official campaign archives, and roundups; absolute counts are illustrative, ratios are the usable signal.
- **The ratio is coverage-sensitive.** The 17-row pass read ~33% indie, the 39-row pass ~40%, this 58-row pass ~33% in complete years; each swing tracked which formats were added (indie-heavy premiums/postal/events vs portfolio-heavy fast food/gacha/gaming). Remaining blind spots - apparel, cosmetics, department-store pop-ups beyond one row, undated 白爛貓/黃阿瑪 merch lines - would likely nudge the indie share up, since the known-but-undated backlog is mostly indie.
- **Chain-archive weight.** 14 of 58 rows are 7-ELEVEN waves and 3 are 藏壽司 gacha; big-chain archives are easy to verify and therefore over-represented relative to one-off brand campaigns that never hit the press.
- **Window.** 2024-01-01 to 2026-07-22; **2026 is a partial year**, its rows lean on roundup clusters, and it holds all 4 UNCLEAR calls - do not cite 2026YTD standalone.
- **Single-source rows.** Rows resting on one roundup or one microsite are flagged in the table's Notes column (including two where the date is inferred from a URL slug or asset timestamps); flagged rows are directional, not independently corroborated.
- **UNCLEAR is honest, not lazy.** Four rows (OBJECT/SOSO ownership, 功夫 film-IP borderline, 乖乖 brand-mascot borderline, OSAMU GOODS estate-management ambiguity) are counted separately and never force-bucketed.
- **Borderline product-launch rows.** Two transit-card rows (Chiikawa and Kanahei x EasyCard) sit on the co-brand vs licensed-product line; both are kept, consistently, and both are disclosed here.
