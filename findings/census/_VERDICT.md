# Taiwan co-branding census - verdict

As of 2026-07-22.

This verdict reads the campaign table in `campaigns.md` (39 curl-verified brand-x-IP co-branding rows, window 2024-01-01 to run date).
It is a **sample census, not an exhaustive count** - see Section 4 for limits.
The brief is to attack the founder thesis ("indies are shut out") as hard as the data allows; where the data undercuts the thesis, that is called out plainly.

**Per year:**

**Trend read:** across the two complete years the indie share is **high and roughly stable - 43% (2024) and 39% (2025), call it ~4-in-10**.
That is materially higher than the ~1-in-3 the first 17-row pass showed, and the increase came precisely from sweeping the under-covered formats (corporate premiums, gaming, postal, home goods) where indies turn out to live.
2026YTD again shows a lower indie share (14.3%), but the 2026 sample is a partial year, leans on drink-collab and 7-11 spring-wave roundups, and carries all 4 of the census's UNCLEAR rows; treat it as thin, not as evidence of decline.

Honest statement for the deck: **in the complete years 2024-2025, roughly 4 in 10 identifiable Taiwan co-branding campaigns used an independent, creator-owned IP; corporate portfolios took about 6 in 10.**
Do not cite 2026YTD alone as a trend.

**Do indies get big-brand collabs or only small ones?** The sample now says clearly: they get big ones, and institutional ones.

- 咖波 alone has landed a top-tier drink chain (清心福全), a major international restaurant chain (Sushiro, six themed stores), **the national postal service (中華郵政 - stamps plus themed post offices)**, and **a major listed financial holding company (華南金控, a shareholder gift for ~470k retail shareholders that drove its own press cycle)**.
- Kanahei (creator-owned) cracked the single most contested co-branding venue in Taiwan retail: a 7-ELEVEN nationwide 集點 campaign (2024) - the format otherwise monopolized by Sanrio/Peanuts/Warner-class portfolios.
- 黃阿瑪 has a repeat institutional client (富采 Ennostar, 2024 and 2025 shareholder gifts) - evidence of renewal, not one-off novelty.
- 白爛貓 spans gaming and a government tourism festival; 爽爽貓 spans a Japanese udon chain and home goods.

**Breadth caveat:** the indie bench is still concentrated - 咖波 is 4 of 12 indie rows, and the six IPs are all established, marquee names (multi-year LINE-sticker era stars).
No newcomer indie IP appears in the sample; prominence accrues to a proven few.
Supporting (not counted, IP-x-IP): LuLu the Piggy x 咖波 ran a joint product line from 2026-06 (cava.tw/265108; gnn.gamer.com.tw/detail.php?sn=304779).

## 3. The thesis read

**Verdict: the data does NOT support "indies are shut out" - it supports "indies are a strong minority, concentrated in a proven few names," and the extension sweep made the counter-case stronger, not weaker.**

- The blunt thesis ("indies can't get deals / can't reach big brands") is **contradicted** at every tier the sample covers: national retail (Kanahei x 7-ELEVEN), major chains (咖波 x Sushiro, 清心福全; LuLu x 麥味登; 爽爽貓 x 丸亀製麵), public institutions (咖波 x 中華郵政; 白爛貓 x Makung festival), and listed corporates (咖波 x 華南金; 黃阿瑪 x 富采 twice).
- The defensible refined thesis has two parts.
  **(a) Share:** portfolios still take ~6-in-10 of campaigns, and they utterly dominate the highest-volume format - of the eight 7-ELEVEN 集點/快閃 waves logged for 2024-2026, seven are portfolio IPs (Sanrio, Peanuts, Warner, Doraemon, anime committees) and only one is indie (Kanahei).
  The premium convenience-store machine is where the portfolio moat is real.
  **(b) Bench:** indie access concentrates in ~6 marquee creator IPs; the data says the door is open to proven indies, not to indies as a class.
- Strongest quotes for the counter-thesis: 咖波 x 壽司郎's national six-themed-store campaign (gnn.gamer.com.tw/detail.php?sn=304779), and 華南金's 2026 shareholder gift where an indie Taiwan IP became the most-discussed 紀念品 of the season (money.udn.com/money/story/5613/9412556).

Net: pitch this as **"the door is open but the bench is thin"** - a ~40% indie share in complete years carried by a handful of proven names, with the biggest retail formats still portfolio-locked.
That framing is both honest to the data and stronger for a licensing-infrastructure pitch than "indies are shut out": the opportunity is widening the bench, not breaking down a wall.

## 4. Method limits (stated plainly)

- **Sample, not census.** 39 rows from a curated sweep of zh-TW press, official campaign archives, and roundups; absolute counts are illustrative, ratios are the usable signal.
- **Recall bias toward covered campaigns.** The first pass over-sampled handshake-drink and breakfast-chain roundups; this extension deliberately added convenience-store archives, corporate premiums, gaming, postal, airline, and home-goods formats - and the indie share rose as coverage widened, which itself suggests remaining blind spots (apparel, cosmetics, department-store pop-ups beyond one row) could move the number again.
- **7-ELEVEN archive weight.** Eight of the 22 newly added rows come from 7-11's own campaign archive; that format is portfolio-heavy, which partially offsets the indie-heavy premium/gaming/postal additions. Both skews are disclosed rather than corrected.
- **Window.** 2024-01-01 to 2026-07-22; **2026 is a partial year**, its rows lean on two roundup clusters, and it holds all 4 UNCLEAR calls - do not cite 2026YTD standalone.
- **Single-source rows.** Rows resting on one roundup or one microsite are flagged in the table's Notes column (including two where the date is inferred from a URL slug or asset timestamps); flagged rows are directional, not independently corroborated.
- **UNCLEAR is honest, not lazy.** Four rows (OBJECT/SOSO ownership, 功夫 film-IP borderline, 乖乖 brand-mascot borderline, OSAMU GOODS estate-management ambiguity) are counted separately and never force-bucketed.
