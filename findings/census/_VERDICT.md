# Taiwan co-branding census - verdict

As of 2026-07-22.

This verdict reads the campaign table in `campaigns.md` (17 curl-verified brand-x-IP co-branding rows, window 2024-01-01 to run date).
It is a **sample census, not an exhaustive count** - see Section 4 for limits.
The brief is to attack the founder thesis ("indies are shut out") as hard as the data allows; where the data undercuts the thesis, that is called out plainly.

## 1. The ratio - overall and per year

**Overall (n = 17):**

| Class | Count | Share |
|---|---|---|
| PORTFOLIO | 10 | 58.8% |
| INDEPENDENT | 4 | 23.5% |
| UNCLEAR | 3 | 17.6% |

**Per year:**

| Year | n | Portfolio | Independent | Unclear |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2024 | 3 | 2 (67%) | 1 (33%) | 0 |
| 2025 | 6 | 4 (67%) | 2 (33%) | 0 |
| 2026YTD | 8 | 4 (50%) | 1 (12.5%) | 3 (37.5%) |

**Trend read:** the indie *share* is **not growing** across the sample - it holds at 33% in 2024 and 2025, then drops to 12.5% in 2026YTD.
But that 2026YTD dip is **fragile and likely a coverage artifact, not a real decline**:

- 2026YTD is only half a year (through 2026-07-22) and leans on two drink-collab roundups plus one festival hit; the 2024/2025 rows were mined from more sources.
- The three UNCLEAR 2026 rows (OBJECT/SOSO, 功夫 film, 乖乖 mascot) inflate the non-portfolio bucket without being Taiwan indie IPs, so they neither help nor hurt the indie count - resolving them would not lift indie share.
- The one 2026 indie row (白爛貓 x Makung festival) was found only by widening beyond drink roundups into events; more of that kind of sweep would probably surface more indie rows.

Honest statement for the deck: **across 2024-2025 the indie share was a steady ~1-in-3; the 2026YTD number is too thin to claim a shrinking trend.** Do not cite 2026YTD alone as evidence indies are fading.

## 2. Indie prominence

Four indie rows span **three distinct Taiwan-origin IPs** - so it is not a single IP recycling, but breadth is still narrow:

| Indie IP | Owner | Appearances | Brand partners | Formats |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 咖波 Bugcat Capoo | creator 亞拉 / 卡特島創意 (Carter Island) | 2 | 清心福全 (2024), 壽司郎 Sushiro (2025) | drink series; food + 6 themed stores + blind-bag merch |
| LuLu the Piggy 露露豬 | creator brand Cici's Story | 1 | 麥味登 (2025) | food + store + merch |
| 白爛貓 Lan Lan Cat | creator-owned Taiwan IP | 1 | 金色雙島藝術祭 / Makung City Govt (2026) | public art-festival event |

**Do indies get big-brand collabs or only small ones?** They get big ones.
The single strongest counter-example to "indies are shut out": **咖波 landed two of the largest chains in the sample** - 清心福全 (a top-tier handshake-drink chain, 2024) and **Sushiro 壽司郎** (major JP sushi chain, a 2025 campaign with six themed stores and blind-bag merch).
That is precisely the big-brand, high-production tier the thesis says is closed to indies.
LuLu the Piggy x 麥味登 (a national breakfast chain) is a second big-brand indie collab.

**Breadth caveat:** three IPs across four rows is thin, and 咖波 alone is half the indie appearances - so within the indie segment there *is* concentration on a small number of proven names.
Supporting (not counted in the table because it is IP-x-IP, not brand-x-IP): **LuLu the Piggy x 咖波** ran a joint product line from 2026-06 (source: cava.tw/265108; gnn.gamer.com.tw/detail.php?sn=304779), showing the top indie names are prominent enough that brands and each other build campaigns around them.

## 3. The thesis read

**Verdict: the data does NOT support a strong "indies are shut out" claim; it refines the thesis into a weaker, more specific form.**

- The blunt version ("indies can't get co-branding deals / can't reach big brands") is **contradicted** by the sample: 咖波 reached 清心福全 and Sushiro; LuLu reached 麥味登; 白爛貓 anchored a government tourism festival. Big-brand indie collabs demonstrably happen.
- The defensible, refined version: **indies are a minority of the co-branding pie (~1-in-3 at best, driven by a handful of proven Taiwan names), and they are outnumbered ~2.5:1 by corporate-portfolio IPs** (Sanrio, Pokemon, Chiikawa, Labubu/POP MART, Care Bears, Fujiya, Papergames). The pipeline is open but crowded, and the indie winners are concentrated in a few marquee IPs rather than a broad bench.
- Strongest quote for the counter-thesis (indies are NOT shut out): 咖波 x 壽司郎 - "自2026... 全台展開... 打造多款聯名餐點與限定週邊，並於全台規劃6家主題店" (gnn.gamer.com.tw/detail.php?sn=304779) - a full national campaign, six themed stores, for a creator-owned Taiwan IP.

Net: pitch this as **"the door is open but the bench is thin"** - opportunity for new indie IPs to break in, not a wall keeping them out. That is a more credible and more fundable framing than "indies are shut out."

## 4. Method limits (stated plainly)

- **Sample, not census.** 17 rows is a curated sweep of zh-TW lifestyle/food press and campaign roundups, not an exhaustive enumeration; absolute counts are illustrative, ratios are the usable signal.
- **Recall bias toward covered campaigns.** Rows come disproportionately from handshake-drink and breakfast-chain roundups (the formats these outlets cover most), so the census over-represents F&B collabs and under-represents apparel, retail, gaming, and events. Indie IPs may be more common in the under-covered formats (the one 白爛貓 row only appeared once we searched beyond drink roundups).
- **Window.** 2024-01-01 to 2026-07-22; **2026 is a partial year** and its 8 rows are the thinnest-sourced, so per-year 2026 splits are the least reliable and should not be cited standalone as a trend.
- **Format skew and single-source rows.** Many 2025-2026 rows rest on a single roundup (flagged in the table's Notes column); flagged rows are directional, not confirmed by independent corroboration.
- **UNCLEAR is honest, not lazy.** Three rows (OBJECT/SOSO ownership unestablished, 功夫 film-vs-character-IP borderline, 乖乖 brand-mascot-vs-character-IP) are left unbucketed rather than force-fit; they are counted separately and never assigned to indie or portfolio.
