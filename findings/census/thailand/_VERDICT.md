# Thailand co-branding census - verdict

As of 2026-07-29 (first build, 83 rows).

This verdict reads the campaign table in `campaigns.md` (83 curl-verified brand-x-IP co-branding rows, window 2024-01-01 to run date).
It is a **sample census, not an exhaustive count** - see Section 5 for limits.
This is the THAILAND run in a region-by-region series (Taiwan v2: 129 rows; Japan: 131 rows; Korea: 135 rows; Malaysia: 103 rows), on the identical definition and the identical pinned edge cases, so the five markets compare row-for-row.
The brief is to attack the founder thesis ("indies are shut out") as hard as the data allows; where the data undercuts the thesis, that is called out plainly.

**The headline: Thailand's indie share is 20.5%, which lands between the Japan/Taiwan/Korea band (25-31%) and Malaysia's 12.6%.**
The second finding matters more than the first: **the indie share moved from 9.3% to 20.5% purely by changing the search keywords, without adding a single new source type.**
Pass 1 swept brand-side Thai keywords and found 4 indie rows in 43. Pass 2 added three artist-side keywords (ศิลปินไทย / นักวาด / ครีเอเตอร์) to the same two trade titles and found 13 more.
That is a methodology finding with direct consequences for how the other markets' numbers should be trusted, and Section 5 states it plainly rather than burying it.

No rows were excluded under the government-mascot (Kumamon) rule as an IP call.
One candidate was excluded on the adjacent own-mascot rule where the brand was a state bank (BAAC's น้องหอมจัง sticker set) - see Section 4.
No row was classed UNCLEAR. The classifiable base is therefore the full **n = 83**.

**Trend read - and the warning that goes with it.**
The per-year line reads as a collapse from 35.7% to 10.3% and a partial recovery to 18.8%.
**Do not report that as a market trend.** It is far more likely a sampling artefact, and the honest read is that this census cannot resolve a year-on-year indie trend for Thailand at all.

Three reasons:
1. The artist-side keyword sweep that produced most indie rows lands disproportionately on 2024, because 2024 was the year Thai trade press ran its "อาร์ตทอย boom" coverage and profiled creators by name. 2025 and 2026 coverage of the same creators is thinner and more brand-led, so their campaigns are recorded without the creator being named - which makes them unclassifiable as indie and drops them out.
2. The absolute counts are 10, 4 and 3 rows. One reclassification moves any year by 3-6 points.
3. 2025 is inflated on the portfolio side by a single IP (below), which mechanically depresses that year's indie share without anything happening to indie licensing.

**The Butterbear distortion.**
Butterbear (น้องเนย) alone accounts for **17 of 83 rows (20.5%)**, and they are concentrated: 1 row in 2024, **12 in 2025**, 4 in 2026YTD.
Butterbear is PORTFOLIO under the pinned Malaysia row 8 call (corporate-owned mascot of the Butterbear brand under Coffee Beans by Dao; designed by the artist lalalhuay but brand-owned).
Strip Butterbear out and 2025 reads 27 rows at 85.2% portfolio - still high, so Butterbear is not the whole explanation for 2025, but it is roughly a third of the gap.
This is a genuine structural feature of the Thai market in the window, not a data error: one corporate-owned local mascot licensed out across FMCG, telco, fintech, banking, electronics, apparel, houseware, QSR, pet food, optics and travel retail at a rate no single IP achieved in any other market measured.

**The addressable-indie number is 11 rows, or 13.3% of the whole census.**

Per year: 2024 = 6 yes / 3 unclear / 1 no; 2025 = 4 yes; 2026YTD = 1 yes / 2 unclear.

The 17 indie rows represent **15 distinct IPs across 15 creators/studios**, two of which recur:
- **BABYBOY / BABY BOY by SNMM** (StupidnoobMacc, อัษฎณัย อรุณคีรี) - rows 10 (LG, 2024) and 81 (Five Star/CPF, 2026).
- **Bad Meaw** (พิ้งค์-เหมือนฝัน ทรัพย์เอนก) - rows 48 (Samsung, 2024) and 51 (AIS, 2024).

Both recurrences are the strongest single piece of evidence against "indies are shut out" in this market: two individual Thai creators each licensed into two separate large corporates (LG, CPF, Samsung, AIS) inside the window, on ad-hoc terms, with no agency named in any of the four sources.

**Nationality split of the indie bucket** - relevant because an agency thesis needs *local* addressable supply:
- **11 Thai creators**: StupidnoobMacc, Bad Meaw (พิ้งค์), Malee Naree, Molly Factory Studio (นิสา ศรีคำดี), JWON (สราวุธ คุระแก้ว), lalalhuay, Gongkan (ก้องกาน), SHEWSHEEP (สุมิตร สีมากุล), Lili Tae (ภิญญ์ฑิตา เตชะมงคลาภิวัฒน์), Chubby Nida, Muebon.
- **4 non-Thai**: LuLu the Piggy (HK/Toyzeroplus), Panda Seven + HIPPO GO (CN/Leo Huang), Angel Boy (KR/DOTOY), ROBBi (CN/ROBBiART).

**10 of the 11 REPRESENTABLE=yes rows are Thai creators.** The addressable-indie layer in Thailand is domestic, individual, and named in the press.

**REP=no is a single row** (12, LuLu the Piggy) on the pinned Taiwan-v2 precedent - indie by ownership but exclusively channelled through the Toyzeroplus label.
**REP=unclear is 5 rows**, and the reason is consistent in four of them: the source names a studio or label rather than a person (Molly Factory Studio, DOTOY, ROBBiART, YIMU ART/Intersteps), so whether the creator is free to be represented cannot be read off the copy.

Each row is assigned to exactly one bucket, read off its Format column.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| Experiential (mall event, exhibition, installation, venue-hosted, brand event) | 9 | 7 | 5 | 21 | 25.3% | **9 (42.9%)** |
| Retail / CVS / e-commerce (loyalty, stamp, redemption, marketplace, travel retail) | 4 | 5 | 2 | 11 | 13.3% | 0 (0%) |
| F&B (QSR, restaurant chain, beverage chain) | 1 | 6 | 2 | 9 | 10.8% | 1 (11.1%) |
| Banking / fintech | 2 | 4 | 0 | 6 | 7.2% | 0 (0%) |
| Telco | 1 | 1 | 0 | 2 | 2.4% | 1 (50.0%) |
| Brand campaign / advertising | 2 | 0 | 0 | 2 | 2.4% | 2 (100%) |

**Reads:**

- **Thailand is not an F&B-led market on this definition - it is a licensed-goods and mall-experience market.** F&B is 10.8%, well behind product licensing (38.6%) and experiential (25.3%). This is the sharpest format divergence from the prior markets in the series and should be flagged in any consolidation.
- **Experiential is where indie IP actually gets bought, and the margin is large.** 9 of 21 experiential rows are indie (42.9%), against 12.5% of product rows, 11.1% of F&B rows and **zero** of the 11 retail/CVS/e-commerce rows and 6 banking rows. A Thai indie creator's realistic first corporate deal is a mall installation or exhibition, not a food tie-in and definitely not a convenience-retail loyalty program.
- **The channels that scale are closed to indie IP.** Retail/CVS/e-comm, banking and fintech - 17 rows, the formats with national reach - are 100% portfolio. Every one runs on Disney, Sanrio, LINE FRIENDS, POP MART, Marvel, Shin-chan or Butterbear. This is the strongest support in the Thai data for the founder thesis, and it is a sharper cut than the headline ratio: indie creators are not absent from the market, they are absent from its highest-distribution formats.
- **Mall operators are the buy side for indie.** Siam Piwat, The Mall Group, Central, ICONSIAM, Future Park and Jungceylon between them account for most of the 9 indie experiential rows. Thai malls are a first-class licensing channel in a way they were not in Malaysia.
- **F&B is thin in 2024 (1 row) and picks up in 2025-26** (6 and 2). The 2024 figure is low enough to suspect coverage rather than market behaviour - QSR character tie-ins are exactly the campaigns most likely to run on brand Facebook rather than trade press.
- **Only one indie row sits in a recurring national-scale format**: row 51, AIS x Bad Meaw, a telco points-redemption campaign. It is the single best existing proof that a Thai indie creator can be bought at scale.
- **Category breadth is unusual.** The window includes water tanks (row 39), gold bars (79), power-bank rental (80), contact lenses (40), pet food (34) and cat litter-adjacent FMCG. Character licensing in Thailand is not confined to youth categories.

## 4. Methodology + edge cases

**Method.** Thai-native keyword sweep of Thai marketing trade press via site search (`?s=`), not English web search, which returns almost nothing for this market.
Two veins swept: Brand Buffet and Marketeer Online. Every row curl-verified against the live URL, HTTP 200, with the date taken from machine-readable page markup (`datePublished` JSON-LD on Brand Buffet, `article:published_time` on Marketeer) or from a campaign window stated in the body.
All 97 URLs cited in the table (row sources plus corroborating and rejected-candidate links) were re-verified at 200 at build time.

**The keyword finding (the most transferable result of this run).**
Brand-side keywords (คอลแลบ, คาแรกเตอร์, ลิขสิทธิ์, มาสคอต, entity names) return brand-side PR. Brand-side PR names the *licensor* and omits the *creator*, so indie rows are structurally invisible to it.
Artist-side keywords (ศิลปินไทย, นักวาด, ครีเอเตอร์) return the same campaigns written from the creator's side, with the creator named and often with an explicit ownership phrase (เจ้าของคาแรกเตอร์ = "owner of the character").
Pass 1, brand-side only: 4 indie in 43 rows (9.3%). Pass 2, artist-side added: 17 indie in 83 rows (20.5%).
**The indie bucket more than doubled in share without changing source type, geography or definition.** Artist-side keywords should lead the keyword list in every future market run and in any re-run of the prior four.

**Also load-bearing:** the two Thai spellings of "character", คาแรกเตอร์ and คาแรคเตอร์, return materially different result sets. Sweeping one loses rows.

### Edge cases and how the pinned rules resolved them

**The sticker-creator ownership rule - the call the task flagged, and the honest result.**
The pinned rule (creator owns the character even though LINE hosts the stickers; LINE Friends' own characters stay portfolio) was written to decide the Thai indie bucket. **In practice it decided almost nothing, because the sticker-native creator scene barely surfaced.**
- It resolved *one* row cleanly on the portfolio side: **row 49, Big C Mini x LINE FRIENDS** - LINE created and owns Brown/Cony/Sally, so PORTFOLIO. Same call for **row 64, Lay's x BT21**.
- Row 43 (Malee Naree, "Sticker Emotion") is the only sticker-adjacent indie row, and it is an art exhibition rather than a sticker licence.
- The one genuine sticker-licensing case found, **BAAC's LINE set "น้องหอมจัง x Fluffy Gangster"** (row rejected), was excluded on the *own-mascot* rule, not the sticker rule: the body describes the characters as created for the bank rather than licensed in. Note this is also the case where the brand is a state-owned bank; the Kumamon rule bars government-owned *mascots as IP*, and would not by itself have barred a state bank licensing a third-party character - so the exclusion rests on own-mascot, and is recorded that way.

**The conclusion to carry forward: Thailand's visible indie licensing economy is an illustrator-and-art-toy economy, not a sticker economy.** Every one of the 11 Thai indie creators in the census is an illustrator, street artist, digital artist or art-toy designer. None was recorded licensing out from a sticker-first position. That is a finding about what trade press can see, not proof the sticker economy is commercially inactive - LINE Creators Market rosters remain unswept (Section 5).

**The hardest ownership call: row 52, Siam Piwat x Molly Factory Studio (CryBunny / CryTeddy).**
The same creator, นิสา ศรีคำดี ("Molly"), appears in this census on both sides of the portfolio/indie line:
- **CRYBABY** is PORTFOLIO in rows 8, 17 and 72, because POP MART controls its commercialisation (pinned, Malaysia row 13).
- **CryBunny and CryTeddy** are INDEPENDENT in row 52, because the Thai source names *her own studio*, Molly Factory Studio, as Siam Piwat's counterparty and does not mention POP MART.

This is not an inconsistency - it is the ownership test applied per IP, as the definition requires, rather than per creator. It also illustrates the known-entity rule working as intended in the other direction: **the census records what the public source says, even where a reader's background knowledge of the POP MART relationship might argue for a different call.** REP is set to `unclear` on row 52 precisely because that relationship is unevidenced either way.

**Butterbear and the licensed-out corporate mascot.** The Malaysia row 8 treatment held and was applied 17 times: a corporate-owned mascot licensed IN by a third party is a row and is PORTFOLIO; the own-mascot exclusion applies only to the mascot's own parent. Jolly Bears (rows 36, 83) took the same call.

**Own-mascot exclusions applied:** Bar B Q Plaza's Gon and น้องกะหล่ำ, Thaitheparos's Je Chilly, Cafe Amazon's Baby Zonzon, Thai Airways' Junior Sky Explorers, Starbucks' Bearista, blueplus+'s Nonplus, KFC's Colonel/Baby Sanders, NocNoc's น้องนอค, Mister DIY's ปันดี, Dream World's มิสมาดาม, KPI's Baby CARE Boy, แม่ประนอม's new mascot, BAAC's น้องหอมจัง.

**Licensor's own retail excluded:** all POP MART Thailand store, flagship, concept-store and pop-up-store openings, its anniversary event, its artist visit and its CSR activity; Toyzero+'s own first store. Venue-hosted POP MART properties ARE rows (11, 62, 66) on the Taiwan-v2 row 126 precedent. Row 5 (Hirono Bangkok) is retained under protest as a ticketed public exhibition whose host venue was not confirmed in the copy read.

**Multi-IP concepts excluded:** Thai Art Toy Fest, Siam Center's "Magical Art (of) Toy Celebration" and Mappy concept store, Disney Toy Expo Thailand (the expo itself - SB Design Square's own licensed range inside it IS row 47), Hong Kong Art Toy Story 2025, ICONSIAM x 10 artists.

**Illustration IP admitted as in-scope**, per the definition's "character/illustration/toy IP" wording: rows 58 (Gongkan naga on a race helmet), 75 (Basquiat on Singha Soda packaging - PORTFOLIO on the estate rule, same line as Miffy/Mercis), 76 (Lili Tae silk scarves), 77 (Chubby Nida biscuit tins). Flagged because a stricter "named recurring character only" reading would drop 4 rows, 3 of them indie, and would cut the indie share from 20.5% to 17.7%.

**One judgement call worth contesting:** Watsons x Artstory by Autistic Thai (12 artworks, 12 artists, foundation programme) was **excluded** as a CSR art programme rather than a brand-x-character-IP licence. A different reading would admit it as an indie row. It is named in the rejected list in `campaigns.md` so the call is visible rather than silent.

**Franchise-local rule applied** (regional/global deals need evidenced TH activation): admitted - rows 18 (Bellygom), 37 and 63 (McDonald's), 68 and 72 (Lazada x POP MART). Rejected - Hyundai IONIQ 5 Disney100, which had no evidenced TH activation.

**Anti-inflation merges applied:** Osotspa's two-brand Butterbear program (row 9), K PLUS's four-IP theme launch (row 1), ttb's Disney card program (row 15, with the separate cinema campaign at row 67 and the September debit variant folded in), Jungceylon's duplicate articles (row 53), Jubilee x POP MART's two articles (row 60), Singha Soda x Basquiat's two articles (row 75), D-nee's two IPs in one program (row 83), 137 Degrees' two articles (row 16), Buttery World's two articles (row 13).
**One duplicate was caught and merged during this build:** a Marketeer piece on the Siam Paragon "Buttery World" exhibition was initially treated as a new row before being identified as the same campaign as row 13; it was folded in as a second source and used to correct row 13's venue, promoter and window rather than counted again.

**Zero UNCLEAR rows.** Every IP resolved to portfolio or independent on the evidence available. This is not a claim of certainty - it reflects that ambiguity in this market showed up as *low translation confidence on a detail* (8 rows, flagged) rather than as unresolvable ownership.

## 5. Coverage-confidence statement

**The honest depth read: this is a credible 83-row sample of a market that would support 120-140 rows, and it is the least deeply sourced run in the series.**
83 rows against Taiwan's 129, Japan's 131, Korea's 135 and Malaysia's 103. Thailand is the thinnest market in the set, and that is a statement about this census's reach, not about Thailand's activity level.

**Single-source count: 73 of 83 rows (87.9%).** Only 10 rows carry a second corroborating URL. This is the weakest evidentiary feature of the run. Single-sourcing is inherent to the method - trade press covers a campaign once - but it means any individual row rests on one publisher's account, most of them PR-sourced.

**Translation-flagged count: 8 rows carry `TH-src (low conf)`.** In every case the low-confidence detail is recorded conservatively rather than guessed:
- rows 4, 60, 66: specific character line not stated, so the IP is recorded at licensor level;
- row 5: host venue not confirmed;
- row 36: corporate owner of Jolly Bears not named;
- row 53: the individual artist behind DOTOY not named, so the indie call rests on the small-label precedent;
- row 59: ROBBiART's corporate structure not evidenced, same reliance.
**Two of the 8 flags sit on indie class calls (rows 53, 59).** If both were reclassified, the indie share would fall from 20.5% to 18.1%. The finding survives that stress test.

**All 83 rows rest on a Thai-language source. Zero rows are recorded from an English-only source.** One row (29) has EN corroboration.

**What is NOT covered, stated as holes rather than smoothed over:**
1. **Brand Facebook pages - completely unswept.** The task named FB as first-class for this market because Thai brand comms live there. Neither pass touched it. This is the single largest known gap, and it is the gap most likely to contain small-brand x indie-creator campaigns that never reached trade press - i.e. it probably suppresses the indie share rather than inflating it.
2. **Two of four trade titles could not be swept with the available tooling.** Positioning Mag serves a JS-rendered search that returns no result links to `curl`; Marketing Oops returns HTTP 403 to `curl`. MARKETING-INTERACTIVE TH is unswept. Running on two titles rather than four is the main reason the row count is 83 and not 120+.
3. **LINE Creators Market rosters - unswept.** The sticker-native creator segment the task identified as the indie bucket's heart is therefore evidenced by one adjacent row. Section 4's conclusion that Thailand's indie economy is illustrator-led rather than sticker-led is a statement about trade-press visibility and should not be reported as a market fact until this vein runs.
4. **Lotus's contributes zero rows.** For a national chain of that size across a 31-month window, zero is not a plausible true value. 7-Eleven (3 rows), Big C (1) and CJ MORE (1) are also almost certainly under-counted. The CP/conglomerate retail vein is present but thin.
5. **Two campaigns are known to exist and are deliberately not counted** because no dated source was found: AIS "อุ่นใจ x Timo & Tintin" (referenced inside row 51's source) and "UNBOXING PRIDE" season 1 (implied by row 66's "season 2"). Both would likely be rows - one of them an indie row - under a deeper sweep.

**Confidence by claim:**

| Claim | Confidence | Why |

**A true smaller number beats a padded one.** Two rows were removed during this build rather than carried: a KFC "Baby Sanders" blind box (KFC's own mascot, excluded by rule) and a numbering placeholder that was not a distinct campaign. The count went from a claimed 85 to a verified 83. Every exclusion made in pass 2 is itemised in the rejected list at the foot of `campaigns.md` so the boundary of this census is auditable rather than asserted.
