# Vietnam co-branding census - campaign log

This is a curl-verified table of identifiable brand-x-IP **co-branding** campaigns in the Vietnamese market, 2024-01-01 to run date (2026-07-29).
Scope is co-branding only - a brand pairing with a character/illustration/toy IP for products, drinks, food, events, pop-ups or stores.
F&B-led but not F&B-only: retail, convenience, telco, banking, mall, cinema and loyalty campaigns count when a character IP fronts a consumer campaign.
Excluded: plain licensed merchandise at retail, KOL/celebrity endorsements, and brand-x-brand tie-ins with no character IP.

**Status at 2026-07-29 (second tranche, IN PROGRESS): 37 curl-verified rows (15x 2024, 14x 2025, 8x 2026YTD; 34 PORTFOLIO / 3 INDEPENDENT / 0 UNCLEAR).**
This is **below the ~80-row floor** and the verdict is deliberately deferred until the table is finished.
Tranche 2 added 13 rows and re-worked the whole candidate pool through a new accented-title scoring pass (see Method).
It also found the market's **first domestic independent character IP row** (row 7, Thỏ Bảy Màu), which the tranche-1 artist-side net had missed.
Every source URL cited below was fetched at build time and returned HTTP 200.

This is the VIETNAM run - market nine in a region-by-region series (Taiwan v2: 129 rows at `findings/census/taiwan-v2/campaigns.md`; Japan: 131 rows at `findings/census/japan/campaigns.md`; Korea: 135 rows at `findings/census/korea/campaigns.md`; Malaysia: 103 rows at `findings/census/malaysia/campaigns.md`; Thailand: 83 rows at `findings/census/thailand/campaigns.md`; Singapore: 81 rows at `findings/census/singapore/campaigns.md`; Hong Kong: 93 rows at `findings/census/hongkong/campaigns.md`; Philippines: 82 rows at `findings/census/philippines/campaigns.md`); identical definition and rules so all markets compare row-for-row.

## Method (sample census, not exhaustive)

Vietnam broke both of the instruments the eight earlier markets ran on, and then handed over a better one.

**What failed.**
The WP-REST portal sweep (`/wp-json/wp/v2/posts?search=...`) that built Taiwan, Japan, Korea, Malaysia, Thailand, Singapore and the Philippines is effectively dead in Vietnam.
Probed and rejected: `kenh14.vn`, `cafebiz.vn`, `cafef.vn`, `thanhnien.vn`, `tuoitre.vn`, `afamily.vn`, `soha.vn`, `vietnamnet.vn`, `marketingai.vn`, `nguoiduatin.vn`, `yan.vn`, `24h.com.vn`, `eva.vn`, `kilala.vn`, `doanhnhansaigon.vn`, `vietcetera.com`, `style-republik.com` (404 - non-WordPress or headless); `vnexpress.net` (406); `elle.vn` and `harpersbazaar.vn` (Cloudflare interstitial); `idesign.vn` and `www.lofficielvietnam.com` (403).
Only `bazaarvietnam.vn` and `rgb.vn` answered a WP-REST query cleanly, and neither is a general consumer-campaign title.

The Hong Kong replacement - Google News RSS - **indexes Vietnamese fine but cannot be resolved**.
`news.google.com/rss/search?q=<query>&hl=vi&gl=VN&ceid=VN:vi` returns rich Vietnamese result sets, but article links are Google-encoded and the `batchexecute` resolver that Hong Kong used now returns **HTTP 429 to a single hand-issued request**, consistent with the permanent block recorded in the HK method note.

Native site search is closed too.
`kenh14.vn/tim-kiem.chn?keyword=...` 301-redirects to the homepage, and the VCCorp search backend it points at (`bm6.cnnd.vn`) does not resolve from this client.

**What worked, and it is the strongest instrument any market in this series has had: the sitemap archive sweep.**
The VCCorp/Epi publishing stack that runs most of the Vietnamese consumer press exposes the **entire back archive** as five-day sitemap chunks (`/sitemaps/sitemaps-2024-3-11-15.xml`), and Vietnamese article slugs are unaccented ASCII transliterations, so a keyword lexicon can be matched directly against the slug with no search API at all.

Ten portals were walked from their sitemap indexes: `kenh14.vn`, `cafebiz.vn`, `cafef.vn`, `afamily.vn`, `soha.vn`, `thanhnien.vn`, `tuoitre.vn`, `dantri.com.vn`, `vnexpress.net` and `vietnamnet.vn`.
That produced **3,322 sitemap chunks fetched (1.64 GB), 1,729,767 distinct article URLs** covering 2024-01-01 to run date, of which **68,534 URLs matched the census lexicon** and **1,064 scored at the strong threshold** after the false-friend filter below.
Every retained row was then confirmed by fetching the live article URL and reading the campaign detail out of the page body.
The Date column is the evidenced campaign window where the body states one, and the publisher's own `datePublished` otherwise (marked "announced").

**Four Vietnamese false-friend traps, recorded because each one cost real filter precision.**
Vietnamese unaccented slugs collapse distinct words onto identical ASCII, and three of these collisions are severe enough that the naive lexicon produced a majority-noise queue.
- `san-x` (the licensor **San-X**) is a substring of **sản xuất** ("production"), which appears in a very large share of Vietnamese business copy. Unusable as a token.
- `kinh-do` (the bakery brand **Kinh Đô**) is a substring of **kinh doanh** ("business"). Unusable as a token; the Kinh Đô rows in this table were reached through the `Solite` and `Mondelez` name instead.
- `lien-quan` (the game **Liên Quân Mobile**) is identical to **liên quan** ("related to"), one of the commonest words in Vietnamese news copy. Only usable as the full string `lien-quan-mobile`.
- `hoa-si` (**họa sĩ**, "illustrator" - the single most important artist-side term in this market) is a **substring of `khoa-sim`**, the slug form of **khóa SIM** ("SIM lock"). Vietnam ran a mass SIM-reauthentication story through 2026 and it swamped the artist-side queue: of 61 artist-tagged candidates at the working threshold, 22 were SIM-lock news. Any future VN sweep must match `hoa-si` on token boundaries, not substring.
A fifth, milder one: `minh-hoa` (**minh họa**, "illustration") is a substring of the common given name **Minh Hoài**.

**The tranche-2 instrument: score on the ACCENTED title, not the slug.**
Slug matching is high-recall and irreducibly ambiguous, because Vietnamese orthography collapses distinct words onto identical ASCII.
The fix is to fetch each candidate's `<title>` and `og:description`, which carry **full diacritics**, and rescore there.
"họa sĩ" and "khóa SIM" are never confusable once the tone marks are present, and the same holds for San-X / sản xuất, Kinh Đô / kinh doanh and Liên Quân Mobile / liên quan.
Only the first 180 KB of each response is read, because `<title>` and the JSON-LD `datePublished` both live in `<head>`, which keeps a 4,681-URL pass to about seven minutes.
The pass also **deduplicates by normalised headline**, which is what finally tamed the VCCorp republication problem: 3,486 fetched titles collapsed to 2,436 distinct campaigns, so roughly **30% of the Vietnamese candidate pool is one PR carried by sister titles**.
This two-stage design - recall on the unaccented slug, precision on the accented title - is the transferable lesson from this market.

**Two further false-friend classes found in tranche 2, both large.**
A third (`meo-moc`) is recorded further down with the domestic-independent findings, where it did its damage.
- **`linh vật` almost never means a brand mascot in Vietnam.** It overwhelmingly means the **Tết zodiac statues** installed on municipal flower streets (đường hoa Nguyễn Huệ and its provincial equivalents), which Vietnamese consumer press covers obsessively every January and February. Of the `linh-vat` candidates in the corpus, the clear majority are public-square Tết statuary; these are commissioned by provincial People's Committees and are therefore **excluded outright under the Kumamon rule** even where a named designer is credited. The term is close to useless as a co-branding signal in this market.
- **`nhân vật` means "figure/personage" as often as "character".** Headlines like "một nhân vật Việt Nam giành HCV Olympic" are about people, not IP.

**A tranche-2 corpus upgrade.**
Wave 1 kept only lexicon-matching URLs, so any lexicon it did not anticipate - the Vietnamese indie character names below, above all - required a full 1.6 GB re-fetch.
The planned sitemap chunks were therefore re-walked with **every URL persisted** to a flat file, so future lexicons are a grep rather than a re-download.
That second walk returned **1,518,439 distinct URLs from 3,327 chunks**, against wave 1's 1,729,767, and the gap is a real coverage caveat rather than a rounding difference: `dantri.com.vn` and `tuoitre.vn` both degraded during the run (see below), so their chunks are thinner in the persisted corpus than in wave 1.
The persisted corpus is what surfaced the domestic-indie findings below, including Thỏ Bảy Màu, which the wave-1 lexicon had no name for and reached only accidentally, through the `lien-quan-mobile` brand token.

**A verification caveat that appeared during this tranche.**
`dantri.com.vn` stopped answering this client partway through the run: 269 of its URLs failed the title pass, and at final re-verification all three cited `dantri.com.vn` sources timed out at 120 seconds where they had returned HTTP 200 in tranche 1.
`tuoitre.vn` degraded the same way under concurrency and had to be re-fetched at five threads.
**76 of the 79 URLs cited in this file re-verified at HTTP 200 at tranche-2 build time**; the three exceptions are all dantri.com.vn and are all either secondary sources on rows that carry another live source (rows 16 and 27) or a rejection note (Bảo Anh Taruki).
No row in this table rests on a dantri.com.vn source alone.

**A second false-positive class specific to this market, and it is large.**
Vietnamese business press writes every B2B memorandum as **"hợp tác chiến lược"** or **"bắt tay"** - the same verbs a character collaboration uses.
Of the 720 candidates carrying a brand name plus a strong collaboration verb but no IP name, the overwhelming majority are bank/telco/pharmacy partnership announcements (Long Châu, Viettel, MobiFone, VPBank, Techcombank).
The collaboration verb alone is close to worthless as a signal in Vietnam; the IP name is doing the work.

**Sites that could not be swept** (recorded as a method limit, not a coverage claim):
- **`brandsvietnam.com` and `advertisingvietnam.com` - the two Vietnamese marketing trade titles, and the single largest named hole in this census.** Brands Vietnam returns 200 on its bare homepage but **403 on every path carrying a query string and on all four of its own advertised sitemaps** (`sitemap_news_2024/2025/2026.xml`), and the homepage HTML that does return carries no article links. Advertising Vietnam returns 403 to every request including the homepage. These are exactly the titles that would carry creator-credited campaign write-ups, and their absence is the reason the mechanism call in the verdict will have to be made on consumer-press evidence alone. This is the Vietnamese form of the Thailand Positioning-Mag lesson.
- `vnexpress.net` historical daily sitemaps (`articles-2025-sitemap.xml?m=3&d=15`) **302-redirect to the homepage**; only the most recent days resolve. VnExpress is therefore present in the harvest at a small fraction of its true archive.
- `vietnamnet.vn` exposes only a rolling `sitemap-news.xml` (1,090 URLs), not an archive.
- Not yet swept, all confirmed to have working sitemaps and queued for the next tranche: `znews.vn` (941 daily chunks), `marketingai.vn` (186 chunks - the closest working substitute for the two 403'd trade titles), `eva.vn` (193), `phunuvietnam.vn` (186), `vietcetera.com` (14), `doisongphapluat.com.vn`.
- Brand, mall and creator Facebook / Instagram / TikTok accounts are entirely unswept, and in Vietnam these carry a very large share of campaign comms.

**Translation confidence.** Every row in this table rests on a Vietnamese-language source read in Vietnamese. Rows where the ownership or activation call turns on a Vietnamese nuance carry a translation-confidence flag in Notes. No row rests on machine translation alone.

**YEAR tag** is by campaign start/run date: `2024` = 2024-01-01..2024-12-31, `2025` = 2025-01-01..2025-12-31, `2026YTD` = 2026-01-01..run date.
Where a campaign was announced in one year and ran in the next, the year tag follows the run.

**A dating trap worth recording.** VCCorp article IDs normally encode the publication date (`215` + `yymmdd` + sequence), and that is how the year tags were pre-sorted.
The `236`-prefixed aFamily IDs do **not** follow this rule: `...236261001110829491` carries `261001` but the article's own `datePublished` is 2026-01-10.
Year tags in this table follow the article's `datePublished` or the stated campaign window, never the URL id.

**IP class** = PORTFOLIO (owned/controlled by a major licensor, studio, media/platform conglomerate, or estate that manages OTHER IP) / INDEPENDENT (living-creator- or small-studio-owned, no conglomerate parent; agency-managed but creator-owned still counts - the Capoo precedent) / UNCLEAR (state why).

**REPRESENTABLE** (INDEPENDENT rows only) = yes / no / unclear: yes = creator-owned, no conglomerate stake, not locked to an exclusive agency/captive arrangement in a way that precludes representation (a plausible PBC client); no = indie by ownership but already exclusively tied up; unclear = can't tell from public sources.
Non-indie rows carry '-'.

## Vietnam-specific rules

- **THE OWN-MASCOT EXCLUSION.** Vietnamese brands increasingly front campaigns with proprietary mascots. A campaign where the brand's own mascot IS the character is **not a row**; a third-party character licensed INTO that brand's campaign **is** a row. Applied: **Vietjet's "Amy" panda aircraft mascot** is excluded (see exclusions below).
- **Art-toy retail: the licensor's own permanent store is NOT a row.** POP MART has opened at least seven own-brand stores in Vietnam over the window (Crescent Mall, Sun World Bà Nà Hills, Saigon Centre, Vạn Hạnh Mall, Hà Nội, Thiso Mall Sala and others). These are own-retail openings, not co-branding campaigns, and are listed in the exclusions. A POP MART or licensor property **hosted by a third-party venue as a limited-run pop-up or installation** IS a row, on the Singapore/Taiwan-v2 venue precedent - which is why the Bandai Namco Asia pop-up at AEON Tân Phú (row 29) counts and the POP MART store openings do not.
- **Mall and chain density, and the anti-inflation merge.** One campaign across several branches of one operator = **one row**, venues noted. Where the same campaign is covered by three VCCorp sister titles (kenh14 / cafef / soha / afamily frequently republish one PR), that is **one row with multiple sources**, not three rows. This bit hard in Vietnam: the VCCorp network republication rate is the highest of any market in this series.
- **Evidenced VN activation is required.** Vietnamese consumer press carries a very large volume of global entertainment and licensing news. A row needs named Vietnamese stores or malls, a Vietnamese launch date, VND pricing, or .vn commerce. Global announcements with no Vietnamese activation are rejected and listed below.
- **Self-presentation is not ownership**, and the per-IP ownership test applies even where one creator sits on both sides (Molly Factory precedent).

## The domestic independent character question - what the corpus actually contains

This is market nine's defining question, so the negative evidence is recorded here rather than left implicit, and it will carry into the verdict.
Across **1.5 million-plus Vietnamese article URLs from 2024-01-01 to run date**, swept with a lexicon that includes named Vietnamese indie character properties as well as generic artist-side terms, the corpus yields exactly **one** domestic independent character IP inside a brand campaign: **Thỏ Bảy Màu x Liên Quân Mobile (row 7)**.
Two further independent rows exist (rows 14 and 30) and both are **Vietnamese fine artists licensing an artwork**, not character IP.

Three named domestic properties were found in the corpus and produced **no** brand-campaign row, which is itself the finding:
- **Wolfoo (Sconnect Việt Nam)** - the largest Vietnamese-owned character property by reach, with a theatrical release in Vietnamese cinemas on 2025-07-11. Corporate-owned (Sconnect) rather than creator-owned, so it would score PORTFOLIO in any case, but the point is that it appears in the corpus only as film and litigation news, never as a licensed partner in a third-party brand campaign. https://tuoitre.vn/phim-hoat-hinh-wolfoo-va-cuoc-dua-tam-gioi-ra-rap-20250710052342412.htm
- **Pikalong (họa sĩ Bùi Đình Thăng / Thăng Fly)** - a creator-owned Vietnamese character with a genuine public profile, covered as a culture feature and not as a campaign partner. https://thanhnien.vn/pikalong-chu-ronggiai-thieng-rong-185240128122315369.htm
- **Thỏ Bảy Màu (họa sĩ Huỳnh Thái Ngọc)** - one campaign, row 7.

The artist-side keyword net, once repaired with token-boundary matching and rescored on accented titles, returns a large and consistent result set that is **almost entirely fine-art gallery exhibitions**: solo shows, group shows, museum retrospectives and painting competitions.
The Vietnamese `họa sĩ` press vein is real, active and well covered, and it is a **fine-art scene, not a character-licensing scene**.
That distinction is the substance of the mechanism call the verdict has to make, and it is not the TH/MY erasure pattern or the KR segregation pattern in its pure form.
A further false friend belongs on the record here: `meo-moc` does not find Mèo Mốc, the Vietnamese webcomic character, it finds "siêu mỏng siêu **méo mó**" ("ultra-thin, ultra-misshapen"), the standing Vietnamese phrase for the sliver houses left behind by road-widening compulsory purchase.
