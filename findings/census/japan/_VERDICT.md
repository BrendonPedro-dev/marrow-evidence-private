# Japan co-branding census - verdict

As of 2026-07-24 (first build, 49 rows).

This verdict reads the campaign table in `campaigns.md` (49 curl-verified brand-x-IP co-branding rows, window 2024-01-01 to run date).
It is a **sample census, not an exhaustive count** - see Section 5 for limits.
This is the JAPAN run in a region-by-region series (Taiwan done: 129 rows, at `findings/census/campaigns.md`), same definition and rules so the markets are directly comparable.
The brief is to attack the founder thesis ("indies are shut out") as hard as the data allows; where the data undercuts the thesis, that is called out plainly.
**This is an early build (49 of a 120-row target); every number below is provisional and will move as the sweep widens.**

**Trend read:** at 49 rows the overall indie share reads **32.7%** - close to where Taiwan landed (27.1% at 129 rows).
The per-year line is noisy because the year buckets are still tiny: 2024 (n=8) reads 50/50, but that is 4 indie rows on a thin base, not a real "half the market is indie" signal - it is an artifact of which formats got swept first (convenience-store and lifestyle-retail veins, where illustrator IP concentrates).
2025 is the only bucket with enough rows (n=32) to trust directionally, and it reads **28.1% indie**, squarely in the Taiwan range.
Do not cite the 2024 or 2026YTD splits yet; they are under-populated. The overall 33/16 split and the 2025 line are the only defensible reads at this stage.
The 0% unclear rate is itself notable: Japanese IP ownership was cleanly establishable for every row so far, unlike Taiwan (which carried a 5.4% unclear tail from Korean-brand and film-license ambiguity).

## 2. The addressable number (REPRESENTABLE)

Of the 16 INDEPENDENT rows, the REPRESENTABLE tag splits:

| REPRESENTABLE | Count | Share of indie rows |
|---|---|---|
| yes | 9 | 56.3% |
| no | 3 | 18.8% |
| unclear | 4 | 25.0% |

**The addressable-indie read:** 9 of 49 campaigns (**18.4%**) are creator-owned AND plausibly representable - the market-entry number PBC actually needs.
The "yes" rows: Higuchi Yuko (x2 - Lawson, Morinaga), Yell Inc's Tsubura na Hitomi, Kumamine's Shigotoneko, the Loft "Creator meets" 18-illustrator roster, Nakamura Yusuke (graniph), studio UG (WEGO), foxco (Calbee), and SHOGO SEKINE (Dr. Ci:Labo).
Deduplicated by owner, that is **~8 distinct representable indie IPs plus the 18-creator Loft showcase** across 49 campaigns.
The 3 "no" rows are the represented-but-closed cases - mofusand (x2, locked to Gray Parka Service) and Suntory's Opanchu Usagi (creator-owned but tightly managed, Itochu holds the overseas license).
The 4 "unclear" rows are indies whose agency-exclusivity could not be read from public sources (Esther Bunny, Lawson's Opanchu Usagi, Tomodachi wa Kuma, Yu Nagaba).
**The pattern that matters:** the most cleanly representable Japan indies are individual fine-artist/illustrator names doing open, non-exclusive brand-packaging collabs (Higuchi, Nakamura, foxco, SEKINE), not the SNS-character-goods IPs (mofusand, Opanchu), which tend to be locked to a management company early.

## 3. Indie prominence

**Breadth, not a handful of recycled names - but two tiers.**
The 16 indie rows carry ~13 distinct IPs: Higuchi Yuko (x2), mofusand (x2), Opanchu Usagi (x2, across Lawson and Suntory), plus single appearances from Esther Bunny, Tsubura na Hitomi, Tomodachi wa Kuma, Shigotoneko, the Loft roster, Yu Nagaba, Nakamura Yusuke, studio UG, foxco, and SHOGO SEKINE.
**Do indies land big-brand collabs?** Yes, and this is the strongest counter to "indies are shut out":
- Higuchi Yuko co-brands with **Lawson** (national convenience chain) and **Morinaga** (major confectioner).
- **UNIQLO UT** ran a capsule with illustrator Yu Nagaba; **Suntory** put Opanchu Usagi on 12 mainstream drink SKUs; **Calbee** gave illustrator foxco a Lawson-exclusive chip package.
- studio UG got a **WEGO**-exclusive apparel line; Nakamura Yusuke is on his **5th graniph** capsule.
**Format skew is the real story:** indie IP concentrates in convenience-store fairs/kuji, lifestyle-retail pop-ups (Loft, Kiddy Land), apparel capsules (graniph, WEGO, UT), and packaged-goods illustrator packages.
It is nearly absent from the national cafe/fast-food menu-and-toy vein: 9 of 10 rows there were Pokemon/Sanrio/Bandai/Square Enix/Cygames/HoYoverse - the mass-volume formats route almost entirely to big licensors and game publishers.
This is the same experiential-vs-mass-volume split Taiwan showed.

## 4. The thesis read

**Provisional verdict at 49 rows: Japan REFINES rather than confirms "indies are a minor share."**
By raw count the thesis holds directionally - portfolio IP is ~2/3 of co-branding campaigns (67.3%), and the mass-volume formats (national fast-food, big-drink, cosmetics) are almost monolithically corporate.
But "shut out" is too strong: **one in three campaigns is indie-owned, and one in five is a plausible PBC client**, with individual illustrators repeatedly landing collabs with Lawson, Morinaga, Suntory, Calbee, UNIQLO and graniph.
The strongest counter-examples to quote: *"Higuchi Yuko - a single living painter - co-brands with both Lawson and Morinaga in the same window"* and *"the Loft 'Creator meets' program showcases 18 independent illustrators at once,"* both evidence of real addressable breadth, not a token indie or two.
The refinement Japan adds over Taiwan: the indie share is **cleaner to classify** (0% unclear so far) but **more locked up** - Japan's SNS-character indies (mofusand, Opanchu) hit management/licensing exclusivity earlier than Taiwan's illustrator IPs did, pushing several indie rows to REPRESENTABLE=no/unclear.
Hold this verdict loosely: 49 rows is 40% of the target sample and the format mix is not yet balanced.

## 5. Method limits, stated plainly

- **Sample census, not exhaustive.** 49 rows built in one sweep across five format veins (convenience store, cafe/fast-food, apparel, pop-up/event, food-beverage-cosmetics). Recall is biased toward campaigns that got ja press coverage and toward the specific chains named in the sweep briefs.
- **Format skew is not yet corrected.** The cafe/fast-food vein is over-weighted toward big-publisher game/licensor tie-ins; the pop-up and lifestyle-retail veins over-weight indie. The true market ratio sits somewhere between the vein extremes, and balancing formats in later iterations will move the overall split.
- **Window:** 2024-01-01 to 2026-07-24. 2024 (n=8) and 2026YTD (n=9) are under-populated; only the 2025 bucket (n=32) is large enough to read directionally. Do not cite per-year 2024/2026 splits yet.
- **Ownership was establishable everywhere so far (0% unclear)** - but that is partly because the first sweep favored well-documented IPs. The unclear rate will likely rise as more obscure characters enter the table; a 0% unclear rate on a 49-row sample is not a stable estimate.
- **Source quality:** most rows rest on brand-official domains (sej.co.jp, family.co.jp, lawson.co.jp, calbee.co.jp, coca-cola.com, gongcha.co.jp) or PR TIMES. A minority rest on aggregators (collabo-cafe, charalab) or, in two cases, a single fan blog / PR-repost - these are flagged in the Notes column. Two brand pages (Asahi Calpis, Suntory-Opanchu) returned HTTP 403 and were verified via aggregators that quote the release text; natalie.mu and uniqlo.com repeatedly 403/timeout on fetch, so some real rows (UNIQLO UT x Ai Yazawa, x Nagano) were left OUT rather than logged unverified - candidates for a future pass via alternate sources.
- **REPRESENTABLE is a public-source read, not a legal one.** "yes" means no conglomerate stake and no visible exclusive lock; it is a plausibility signal for PBC outreach, not confirmation that a creator is contractually free.
