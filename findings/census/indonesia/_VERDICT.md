# Indonesia co-branding census - verdict

Market ten, and the last market of this campaign.
Run date 2026-07-30, window 2024-01-01 to 2026-07-30.
Evidence base: **102 curl-verified rows** in `campaigns.md`, every source URL fetched at build time and returning HTTP 200 (174 distinct URLs, covering the 102 rows plus every excluded, held and corroborating item cited).
Identical definition, identical ownership test and identical anti-inflation rules as the nine markets already closed, so the rows compare one-for-one.

Built over three tranches, and the tranche structure matters for reading the numbers: 32 rows on native search plus WP-REST term queries, 61 after full-archive enumeration, 102 after the youth-lifestyle portal network's native search was tested and found to work.
Two of the three tranches produced their rows from an instrument the previous tranche had recorded as unavailable, and in both cases the record was an untested assumption rather than a measurement.
That is the single most important caveat on everything below.

---

## 1. The ratio

**Overall: 13 of 102 rows are INDEPENDENT, 12.7%.**
88 rows are PORTFOLIO (86.3%) and 1 is MIXED (row 2, Shopee's Garfield x Si Juki collection, which pairs a global portfolio IP with a creator-owned domestic character).
Counting the MIXED row on its Si Juki side gives **14 of 102, 13.7%**, and that is the upper bound of any reasonable counting convention here.
No row in this census is classed `unclear` on ownership: every IP resolved to a corporate holder or to a named individual creator, which is unusual across this batch and is a consequence of Indonesian press naming its creative counterparties directly.

**Per year.**

| Year | Rows | Independent | Share | Independent rows |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2024 | 35 | 7 | **20.0%** | 6, 7, 10, 65, 66, 68, 71 (plus MIXED row 2) |
| 2025 | 50 | 4 | **8.0%** | 14, 15, 20, 77 |
| 2026 YTD | 17 | 2 | **11.8%** | 29, 60 |

**The per-year caveat, carried unchanged from every prior market and with one addition specific to Indonesia.**
2026 is seven months of data on a denominator of 17, and no trend should be read from it.
The Indonesia-specific addition is that the per-year split here is partly an artefact of *which instrument found which year*, not only of what the market did.
The 2024 independent rows cluster because Instrument G, the vein that carries independent-artist coverage, reaches back through the whole window with equal depth, while the portfolio-heavy trade-press veins (Instruments E and F) are denser on 2025 than on 2024.
The honest reading of the per-year table is that the market's independent share is somewhere in the 8 to 20 per cent band with no established direction, not that it fell by half in 2025.

**The direction of drift across tranches is itself evidence, and it points the same way each time.**
21.9% at 32 rows, 13.1% at 61 rows, 12.7% at 102 rows.
Every widening of the instrument surfaced more portfolio licensing than independent licensing, including the widening that was specifically aimed at the independent-artist channel and that added five independent rows in one tranche.
The ratio is converging, and it is converging low.

## 2. The addressable read

**REPRESENTABLE split across the 13 INDEPENDENT rows: 11 yes, 0 no, 2 unclear.**
The MIXED row is `yes` on its Si Juki side.
Zero `no` is a real finding rather than an absence of scrutiny: no Indonesian independent row in this census sits behind a label, platform or studio that captures the IP, which is the opposite of the Toyzeroplus-style label-captive pattern that produced `no` in earlier markets.
The two `unclear` rows are row 60 (Kadek Wiwindari, whose representation arrangements are not stated anywhere found) and row 77 (the PT KAI programme, where three creator IPs are named but only one creator is).

**Distinct independent creators: 12.**
Muklay (4 rows: 6, 10, 20, 71), Darbotz (2 rows: 7, 14), Hari Prast, Abenk Alter, Arkiv Vilmansa, Kadek Wiwindari, Ykha Amelz, ARDNEKS (Kendra Ahimsa), Faza Meonk (row 68 outright plus the MIXED row 2), and the three creators behind Komik Gajelas, Si Bedil and Tuti and Friends on row 77, of whom only Jasmine of Komik Gajelas is named in the source.

**Distinct REP=yes creators: 8.**
Muklay, Darbotz, Hari Prast, Abenk Alter, Arkiv Vilmansa, Ykha Amelz, ARDNEKS and Faza Meonk.

**That is the addressable number for this market on this evidence: eight named, reachable, creator-owned Indonesian IP holders.**
Two things qualify it in opposite directions and both belong in the number's interpretation.

*Downward.* The concentration is extreme.
Muklay alone accounts for 4 of the 14 independent-or-mixed rows and Darbotz for 2, so two creators hold 43% of the independent side of this market's press-visible activity.
Seven of the twelve appear exactly once.
And eleven of the fourteen independent-or-mixed rows are an **individual artist licensing artwork onto a product or a space**, not a character IP with a name of its own that a brand programme can be built around.
The only creator-owned *character* IPs in the census are Si Juki (rows 2, 68) and the three comic IPs on row 77, and none of them has a repeat brand programme inside the window.

*Upward.* Every independent row here came from press, and press is demonstrably not where this activity mostly lives.
Tranche 3 found five independent rows in a single tranche by asking eleven publishers that the two earlier tranches had never asked, and the same eleven publishers hold at least six further independent-artist brand campaigns that fall **outside** the window (Coach x Muklay 2022, Acer Predator x Darbotz 2020, bateeq x Monez 2022, Specs x Stereoflow 2022, Botanical Essentials x Darbotz 2023, ATARU x Muklay 2023).
Those six are not rows, but they establish that the independent channel has been running continuously since at least 2020 and that its press footprint is a fraction of its real volume.
**Eight is a floor, and the artist-side non-press channels named in section 5 are where the rest of the number is.**

Mall and venue activation (3 of 28: Barcode Gokart x Muklay, Bibit.id x Abenk Alter, Alba x Arkiv Vilmansa), collectibles and lifestyle product (3 of 8: Shopee's Si Juki collection, Digimap x Muklay, RRQ x Si Juki), automotive product (2 of 8: Polytron x Darbotz, TBW x Muklay), FMCG and alcohol packaging (2 of 11: Jägermeister x ARDNEKS, WRP x Kadek Wiwindari), transport (1 of 4: PT KAI), apparel (1 of 11: Mooi Heartmade x Muklay and Hari Prast), electronics (1 of 1: Advan x Darbotz), retail store design (1 of 7: Lacoste x Ykha Amelz).
The pattern is that independents get commissioned by **mid-size Indonesian consumer brands that need a look**, and portfolios get licensed by **large brands that need a face**.

## 4. Methodology and edge cases

### The mechanism: two-channel licensing

Indonesia was predicted to show erasure-plus-thin-press, offered as a fifth-pattern candidate: campaigns invisible not because press omits creators but because campaigns barely reach indexed press at all.
**The prediction is wrong on both halves, and the answer to the fifth-pattern question is yes but not that pattern.**

Indonesian consumer press is enormous, fully open to HTTP, and names creators readily and often in the headline: `Advan Gandeng Darbotz`, `Barcode Gokart Gandeng Seniman Muklay`, `Alba Gandeng Arkiv`, `TBW Berkolaborasi dengan Muklay`, `Jägermeister Indonesia x ARDNEKS`, `Lacoste Kolaborasi dengan Ykha Amelz`.
Creator-naming is markedly better here than in the Thailand and Malaysia erasure pattern.
The domestic character scene is real and productive, so this is not Vietnam's pre-formation either.

What Indonesia exhibits is **two-channel licensing**, and it is a distinct shape from the four carried forward.
The same market runs two licensing economies that barely touch:

1. **The portfolio channel.** Global IP (Sanrio, Pokémon, Disney, Pop Mart, Shueisha titles) plus domestic IP that has been *aggregated into a corporate holder* (Tahilalats and Hai Dudu inside INFIA Corp, Sekuya inside its own "IP House"). This channel reaches the national carrier, a state aviation and tourism holding, a state railway, the Jakarta metro, a taxi group, blue-chip FMCG, banks, carmakers and fourteen mall operators, and it is reported by mainstream news portals, business press and brand trade press.
2. **The independent channel.** Individually practising Indonesian illustrators and art-toy artists (Muklay, Darbotz, ARDNEKS, Ykha Amelz, Abenk Alter, Arkiv Vilmansa, Hari Prast, Kadek Wiwindari) licensing artwork onto products and spaces for mid-size consumer brands. This channel is reported **only in the youth-lifestyle portal network** (IDN Times and its verticals, Popbela, Popmama, Duniaku, Grid), continuously since at least 2020, and its coverage does not cross into the portals that carry channel 1.

The two channels differ in IP form (character IP versus artwork), in brand size, in publisher, and in whether the counterparty is a company or a person.
They overlap in exactly one place in 102 rows: row 2, where Shopee put Si Juki next to Garfield.

**Why this is not just the erasure pattern with extra steps.**
Erasure means the campaign is reported and the creator is not named.
Here the creator is named, the campaign is reported, and the *report itself* is filed in a publication tier that a brand-side or trade-side sweep never opens.
The invisibility is bibliographic, not editorial, and it is invisibility to the researcher rather than to the consumer.
This distinction is load-bearing for anyone acting on the number: in Thailand the fix is to ask the creator because the press will not tell you, and in Indonesia the fix is to ask a different publisher.

**Consolidation is the second half of the mechanism and it is now well evidenced.**
Tahilalats appears on **ten rows** spanning airline livery, aircraft interior, carmaker campaign, metro retail store, mall installation, FMCG packaging, footwear, motorcycle, state tourism activation and taxi brand.
No independent creator in this census appears on more than four, and none appears in more than three formats.
The market's one domestic character IP capable of a repeat cross-format programme is the one held by an IP investment company, and Sekuya (rows 54, 74) is a second instance of the same structure.
Domestic character IP in Indonesia is commercially strong, reaches the top of the market, and gets there **after** being aggregated.

### Edge cases and the calls that decide the numbers

**The INFIA call is the single most consequential ownership decision in this census and it is worth ten rows.**
Tahilalats is created by Nurfadli Mursyid and Indonesian press consistently calls him a `komikus`, which under the 咖波 precedent would make it creator-owned.
It is classed **PORTFOLIO** on four independent pieces of evidence: InJourney's release describing `karakter khas Infia seperti Tahilalats, Dudu, dan Funcican`; INFIA Corp's own portfolio page listing Tahilalats under `IP CHARACTER` among "40+ Intellectual Properties"; INFIA's own milestone timeline describing a `Full Transition to IP Business` in 2020 and `Investing in Emerging IPs` in 2021 with outside investors on its Board of Commissioners; and IDN Times independently describing Tahilalats as `salah satu properti intelektual (IP) INFIA Corp`.
Row 70 adds a fifth angle: the character Hai Dudu is spoken for in press by a `Head of Hai Dudu`, a salaried officer, not by an author.
**The residual doubt is recorded rather than hidden.** No document examined states the size of INFIA's stake, the pinned rule that self-presentation is not ownership cuts both ways, and it remains possible that the creator retains majority ownership while INFIA licenses.
If that turned out to be so, ten rows move to INDEPENDENT, the overall share becomes 23/102 = 22.5%, and Indonesia reads as the healthiest independent market in this batch rather than a consolidated one.
The whole verdict hangs on this call and it should be flagged as such to anyone using the number.

**The contrast case that shows the call is not anti-creator.** Si Juki is licensed through Pionicon, and Pionicon is creator Faza Meonk's own studio, so Si Juki is INDEPENDENT (rows 2, 68). A creator-founded studio is the creator; a third-party IP investment company holding the asset is portfolio.

**Other calls applied.**
Miffy PORTFOLIO (row 82) and Snoopy PORTFOLIO per the pinned list.
Government-owned mascots excluded under the Kumamon rule, with the distinction that Indonesian ministries appear repeatedly as *campaign partners* (rows 1, 13, 17, 74, 77) rather than as IP owners, which does not exclude those rows.
OWN-MASCOT exclusion applied and tested at row 72, where KFC's own Chaki would not count but the licensed-in Bobo character does.
Platform-own-retail excluded (Pop Mart's own Bandung, Bali and Makassar stores) while third-party mall commissions are included (rows 11, 88).
IP owners' own campaigns excluded, including Mattel's batik Barbie, Marvel Indonesia's Fantastic Four programme and The Pokémon Company's own dangdut MV.
Character-free brand collaborations excluded, including both INFIA `:Dagelan` x 3Second collections, which would have been the cheapest available way to inflate this table.
BT21 PORTFOLIO despite its co-design history with BTS members, because LINE Friends holds and licenses it.
One campaign, one row, with merges recorded on the rows: row 88 merges two mall hosts of one Christmas programme, row 93 merges two chains of one operator's campaign, row 102 merges two waves of one brand-IP programme, and rows 90 and 96 are split because a distinct brand applied the IP to its own product inside a shared installation.

## 5. Coverage confidence

**Depth.** 102 rows, 174 distinct URLs, all HTTP 200 at build time, across roughly 30 publishers and four instrument families (native search on detik and Kompas; WP-REST archive enumeration on six portals for 45,230 in-window URLs; sitemap archive walks on hypeabis.id for 15,409 URLs and infobrand.id for 20,044; native search across eleven youth-lifestyle and business portals for 1,969 portal-term pairs).
No paywalls, no interstitials, no publisher degradation under concurrency.

**Single-source count: 72 of 102 rows rest on one source (70.6%).**
30 rows carry two or more independent sources: 12 were written multi-source, and 15 further rows written as single-source in tranches 1 and 2 gained an independent second or third source in tranche 3, listed URL by URL under "Tranche 3 corroboration" in `campaigns.md`.
Three rows carry three sources.
This is a high single-source rate and it is the main quality limitation of the Indonesian table.
It is partly structural: Indonesian brand PR is distributed to many outlets that reproduce it near-verbatim, so a second source is often the same press release rather than independent confirmation, and the corroboration list deliberately prefers *different publisher groups* over sister titles.

**Translation confidence: high on all 102 rows, and no row is flagged lower.**
Bahasa Indonesia collaboration verbs (`gandeng`, `menggandeng`, `berkolaborasi`, `kerja sama`, `merangkul`) are unambiguous, authorship language (`karya`, `kreator`, `komikus`, `seniman`) is unambiguous, and the language has no diacritics, so Vietnam's whole class of accent-collapse false friends does not exist.
The Indonesian risk is semantic, not orthographic, and it was handled at the filter rather than at the row: `karakter` usually means personality, `kolaborasi` is the default word for any institutional partnership, and the daily promo-listicle stream uses the census vocabulary constantly and means nothing by it.

**ID-activation flags: 7 rows carry an explicit flag (2 weak, 5 moderate).**
Rows 8 (Coca-Cola x Marvel) and 76 (innisfree x Sanrio) are weak; rows 31 (Clarins x MOLLY), 32 (SK-II x CRYBABY), 51 (GODIVA x LABUBU), 69 (ZEN Tableware x Minions) and 92 (UNIQLO x Zootopia) are moderate.
A stricter reading of the franchise-local rule would demote up to 7 rows, all PORTFOLIO, which would *raise* the independent share to 13/95 = 13.7%.
The permissive and strict readings therefore bracket the headline number between 12.7% and 13.7%, which is narrow enough not to matter to the conclusion.
Six global campaigns were held out entirely for lack of any Indonesian activation evidence, and a permissive reading of those would add six portfolio rows and lower the share to 13/108 = 12.0%.

**Named holes, in order of how much they would change the numbers.**

1. **Artist-side non-press channels (Instagram, TikTok, Behance, creator newsletters) - unswept, and this is the hole that matters most.** Every independent row in this census came from a brand's or a publisher's announcement. Tranche 3 demonstrated that adding one publisher tier multiplied the independent row count fivefold, and that six further independent campaigns sit just outside the window in the same tier. The 12.7% figure is a floor for the press channel and the true independent share is very likely higher; how much higher is not measurable from press alone.
2. **The INFIA ownership question - open, and worth ten rows.** See section 4. This is the one hole that could move the headline number by more than ten points in a single stroke, and closing it needs a share register or a licensing agreement, not more press.
3. **`tribunnews.com` and `kumparan.com` - unswept.** Tribunnews serves its sitemap index normally while 403-ing `/search`, so a sitemap walk is the route in and was not attempted. Kumparan resists both routes. Both are large general portals, so the expected yield is more portfolio rows, which would lower the ratio slightly.
4. **Mall operators' own channels - unswept.** Fourteen operators appear in this table through press; smaller activations live on their own Instagram accounts. Expected yield is more portfolio mall rows.
5. **INFIA's own pipeline - unworked.** Its newsroom names a Tahilalats x PUBG Mobile skin drop, a Jakarta Fair Kemayoran 2026 merchandise partnership, a One Piece Cafe and a Jujutsu Kaisen venue, none corroborated by third-party press and none counted. Expected yield is more portfolio rows for the same holder.
6. **Alfamart specifically - measured empty across three instruments.** Indomaret produced a row on the fourth attempt via a publisher the earlier tranches had not asked, so Alfamart's zero should be treated as unproven rather than established.
7. **`mix.co.id`, `swa.co.id`, `industry.co.id`, `marketeers.com` - marketing trade press, still partly or wholly unreachable.** Expected yield is portfolio rows.
8. **Regional and non-Jakarta activity - thin.** The table reaches Bandung, Surabaya, Makassar, Semarang, Yogyakarta, Depok, Tangerang, Bali and Mandalika, mostly through IDN Times' regional verticals, which were only lightly sampled. This is the hole most likely to hide *independent* rows other than the artist-channel hole, because regional brands commission regional artists.

**What the coverage-confidence statement comes to.**
The portfolio side of this market is well measured and further sweeping would mostly add more of it.
The independent side is measured only in the channel that press happens to index, that channel was discovered in the final tranche, and it immediately produced five of the census's thirteen independent rows.
**Read 12.7% as a floor with a soft ceiling near 20%, read the addressable count of eight as a floor, and treat the INFIA call as the load-bearing uncertainty that it is.**
An honestly thinner reading of this market would have been to stop at 61 rows and report 13.1% as settled; the reason to prefer this one is not that it is bigger but that it names the channel it is measuring.
