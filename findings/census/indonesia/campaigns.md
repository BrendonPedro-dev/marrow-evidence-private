# Indonesia co-branding census - campaign log

This is a curl-verified table of identifiable brand-x-IP **co-branding** campaigns in the Indonesian market, 2024-01-01 to run date (2026-07-30).
Scope is co-branding only - a brand pairing with a character/illustration/toy IP for products, drinks, food, events, pop-ups or stores.
F&B-led but not F&B-only: retail, convenience, telco, banking, e-commerce, mall, cinema and loyalty campaigns count when a character IP fronts a consumer campaign.
Excluded: plain licensed merchandise at retail, KOL/celebrity endorsements, and brand-x-brand tie-ins with no character IP.

**Status at 2026-07-30 (TRANCHE 3, complete - verdict at `_VERDICT.md`): 102 curl-verified rows (35x 2024, 50x 2025, 17x 2026YTD; 88 PORTFOLIO / 13 INDEPENDENT / 1 MIXED).**
Tranche 1 delivered rows 1-32; tranche 2 added rows 33-61 on full-archive enumeration (Instrument E); tranche 3 added rows 62-102 on a vein that both earlier tranches had written off as unreachable - the youth-lifestyle portal network's native search, described under Instrument G below.
The census is now **above the ~80-row floor** and comparable in size to Malaysia (103) and Hong Kong (93).
Every source URL cited below was fetched at build time and returned HTTP 200 (174 distinct URLs checked).

**The numbers the verdict needs, tallied here so they are checkable against the table rather than asserted.**
Overall independent share **13/102 = 12.7%**, or 14/102 = 13.7% if the MIXED row is counted on its Si Juki side.
Per year: 2024 **7/35 = 20.0%** (rows 6, 7, 10, 65, 66, 68, 71, plus the MIXED row 2), 2025 **4/50 = 8.0%** (rows 14, 15, 20, 77), 2026YTD **2/17 = 11.8%** (rows 29, 60).
The per-year caveat carried from every prior market applies with extra force here: 2026 is seven months of data and its denominator of 17 is still too small to read a trend from.
REPRESENTABLE split across the 13 independent rows: **11 yes, 0 no, 2 unclear**, plus the MIXED row at yes on its Si Juki side.
**Distinct independent creators: 12** - Muklay (4 rows: 6, 10, 20, 71), Darbotz (2 rows: 7, 14), Hari Prast, Abenk Alter, Arkiv Vilmansa, Kadek Wiwindari, Ykha Amelz, ARDNEKS (Kendra Ahimsa), Faza Meonk (row 68 outright plus the MIXED row 2), and the three creators behind Komik Gajelas, Si Bedil and Tuti and Friends on row 77, of whom only Jasmine of Komik Gajelas is named in the source.

That is the addressable number for this market on this evidence: **eight named, reachable, creator-owned Indonesian IP holders**, of whom one (Muklay) accounts for four of the fourteen independent-or-mixed rows.

**The one finding that is already load-bearing** is the ownership call in "The INFIA question" below.
Indonesia's most visible domestic character IP - Tahilalats - turns out to sit inside a corporate IP investment portfolio, not with its creator, and that single call moves six rows from INDEPENDENT to PORTFOLIO.
It also points at a mechanism that is **not** the erasure-plus-thin-press pattern this market was predicted to show, and the fifth-pattern question is discussed under "Which mechanism Indonesia looks like" below, on the evidence available so far.

This is the INDONESIA run - market ten, the final market in a region-by-region series (Taiwan v2: 129 rows at `findings/census/taiwan-v2/campaigns.md`; Japan: 131 rows at `findings/census/japan/campaigns.md`; Korea: 135 rows at `findings/census/korea/campaigns.md`; Malaysia: 103 rows at `findings/census/malaysia/campaigns.md`; Thailand: 83 rows at `findings/census/thailand/campaigns.md`; Singapore: 81 rows at `findings/census/singapore/campaigns.md`; Hong Kong: 93 rows at `findings/census/hongkong/campaigns.md`; Philippines: 82 rows at `findings/census/philippines/campaigns.md`; Vietnam: 47 rows at `findings/census/vietnam/campaigns.md`); identical definition and rules so all markets compare row-for-row.

## Method (sample census, not exhaustive)

Indonesia was predicted to be the batch's hardest sweep on the grounds that campaign coverage is thinly indexed.
That prediction turned out to be **wrong about the cause and right about the effect**, and the correction is the main methodological result of this tranche.
Indonesian consumer press is *not* thinly indexed - it is enormous, fully open to HTTP, and it names creators readily.
What is thin is the **signal-to-noise ratio**, because the same publishers run an industrial-scale daily promo-listicle operation that uses the census vocabulary constantly and means nothing by it.

**What worked, in order of yield.**

*Instrument A - detik.com native search.* `https://www.detik.com/search/searchall?query=<term>&page=<n>` is server-rendered, paginates to roughly page 10-19 at 11-12 results per page, and each result carries a **Unix timestamp in a `d-time` attribute** plus the headline in a `dtr-ttl` attribute.
Dates are therefore free: no article fetch is needed to year-tag a candidate.
This is the single most productive instrument in the market and it supplied 331 of the 714 scored candidates.

*Instrument B - search.kompas.com.* `https://search.kompas.com/search/?q=<term>&sort=latest&page=<n>` is also server-rendered, but caps hard at about **4 pages** (roughly 70 URLs per query).
Its compensating virtue is that Kompas article URLs embed the publication date in the path (`/read/2026/07/18/153400520/...`), so dating is free here too.

*Instrument C - WP-REST.* Unlike Vietnam, WordPress REST is alive in Indonesia, but only on second-tier and niche titles.
Nine answered cleanly: `marketing.co.id`, `dgi.or.id`, `ussfeed.com`, `kincir.com`, `greenscene.co.id`, `infobanknews.com`, `hipwee.com`, `mojok.co`, `majalahfranchise.com`.
`marketing.co.id` is the disproportionately valuable one: it is marketing trade press, it carries brand press releases nearly verbatim, and it supplied several rows that no consumer title covered at all.
Probed and rejected as non-WordPress, headless or blocked: `kompas.com`, `detik.com`, `idntimes.com`, `popbela.com`, `popmama.com`, `kumparan.com`, `liputan6.com`, `tribunnews.com`, `suara.com`, `hypeabis.id`, `grid.id`, `hai.grid.id`, `fimela.com`, `beautynesia.id`, `industry.co.id`, `katadata.co.id`, `thejakartapost.com` (404 or non-WP); `tempo.co`, `bisnis.com`, `magdalene.co`, `jakartaglobe.id` (Cloudflare interstitial or 403); `whiteboardjournal.com` (401, `rest_not_logged_in`); `mix.co.id` and `swa.co.id` (return HTML at the REST path, not JSON).

*Instrument D - hypeabis.id monthly sitemap archive.* `hypeabis.id` exposes its whole back archive as clean per-month chunks (`/sitemap-202405.xml`) running from 2021-07 to date.
Walking 2024-01 through 2026-07 yielded **15,409 article URLs**, greppable directly on the slug.
Hypeabis is Bisnis Indonesia's creative-economy vertical, which makes it the closest thing this market has to a dedicated artist-side title, and it supplied 105 scored candidates including the Mooi Heartmade row.

Combined pool from tranche 1: **26,802 distinct candidate URLs** across 268 lexicon terms, scored down to a **714-candidate working queue**.

*Instrument E - full-archive enumeration, added in tranche 2, and the most productive instrument in this market.*
Tranche 1 used WP-REST as a **search** endpoint, one lexicon term at a time.
That was the wrong way to use it.
WordPress REST also answers `?per_page=100&page=<n>&after=2024-01-01T00:00:00&_fields=link,date,title`, which walks a publisher's **entire** in-window archive in a few dozen requests and returns the exact post count in the `x-wp-total` header.
Six of the nine answering portals were walked this way, yielding **45,230 in-window article URLs** (`marketing.co.id` 7,398, `infobanknews.com` 27,233, `greenscene.co.id` 6,986, `mojok.co` 5,061, `kincir.com` 3,678, `ussfeed.com` 2,272), each with a date and a title, and the whole set was then greppable offline with no further fetches.
`marketing.co.id` alone produced 83 IP-name hits and around 20 rows.
This matters as a method result, not just a yield result: **term-search recall was the binding constraint in tranche 1, not publisher coverage.** The archive was always fully reachable; the sweep was only ever seeing the slice that a 268-term lexicon happened to name.

*Instrument F - `infobrand.id`, the vein that should have been opened first.*
`infobrand.id` is Indonesian **brand** trade press - its beat is literally brand campaigns - and it is not WordPress, so tranche 1's WP-REST probe missed it entirely.
It exposes its whole archive as eleven `sitemap-post-N.xml` chunks with a `lastmod` per URL: **20,044 URLs total, 4,694 of them in window**, slugs descriptive enough to grep directly.
It supplied eight rows no other publisher in this corpus carried (Sasa x Looney Tunes, Chatime x One Piece, Cosmonauts x Kamen Rider, Godiva x Labubu, Senka x Cinnamoroll, Chagee x Pop Mart, 3Second x Sekuya, WRP x Kadek Wiwindari), and its pre-2024 archive independently documents the same phenomenon reaching back years (Indomaret's official Pokémon merchandise edition in 2022, BT21 on Frozz packaging in 2023, Somethinc x Crayon Shinchan in 2023, Pizza Hut Indonesia x Genshin Impact in 2023, Modena's Mickey & Friends appliance series in 2023).
Those pre-2024 items are out of window and are **not** rows, but they are strong evidence that this market's character-licensing activity is not new and that the census window is sampling an established practice.

*Instrument G - the youth-lifestyle portal network's native search, added in tranche 3, and the single most productive instrument in the market.*
Tranches 1 and 2 both recorded `idntimes.com`, `kumparan.com` and `tribunnews.com` search as **client-rendered with unlocated JSON backends**, and listed them as the largest remaining hole.
For IDN Media's network that was simply wrong, and testing it took one request.
`https://www.idntimes.com/search?q=<term>` is **server-rendered HTML** and returns relevant article links directly; so do `duniaku.idntimes.com` (the group's geek and pop-culture vertical), `popbela.com`, `popmama.com`, plus `grid.id`, `cnnindonesia.com`, `cnbcindonesia.com`, `kontan.co.id`, `viva.co.id`, `merdeka.com` and `jpnn.com`.
Only `kumparan.com` (403 to sitemap and no result markup), `tribunnews.com` (403 on `/search`), `suara.com`, `tempo.co`, `katadata.co.id`, `okezone.com` and `sindonews.com` failed the same probe.
Eleven working endpoints were swept with a 180-term lexicon (IP names, artist names, brand names, Bahasa co-branding phrases) for **1,969 portal-term pairs and 3,131 slug-confirmed hits across 1,472 distinct article URLs**, each hit filtered client-side on the requirement that every meaningful token of the query term appears in the returned URL - the same recall-then-local-precision shape that Instruments A and B needed.
Ranking those by co-branding verb plus IP token and removing the known noise classes left 209 candidates to fetch, of which **136 were in window and 41 became rows**.
The method result is worth stating plainly, because it is the second time in this market that a *believed* instrument limit turned out to be an untested assumption: tranche 2 learned that WP-REST was being used as a search engine when it was an enumerator, and tranche 3 learned that four portals were recorded as client-rendered on the strength of how their pages look rather than on a request to `/search`.
**A vein is not closed until a request has been made to it.**

Two properties of this network matter beyond raw yield.
It carries **press releases from brands that the mainstream news portals do not cover at all** - ZEN Tableware, Kacamatamoo, TBW, Dazzle Me, Azarine, ASTEC, Lexus biscuits, Executive - which is why 41 rows came out of it after two tranches had already worked the same market.
And it is **the only vein in this corpus that covers independent illustrators as brand partners**, which is the tranche-3 measurement that revises the mechanism reading below.

Other trade titles probed in tranche 2 and their outcome: `marketeers.com` exposes only a 558-URL recent-window sitemap and no data route (partial vein, largely unswept); `mix.co.id` and `swa.co.id` serve empty `sitemap.xml` files and remain unreachable; `industry.co.id` exposes per-category sitemaps and was not walked; 25 further Indonesian domains were probed for WP-REST and only `ideoworks.id` (385 posts, an agency blog) and `gadgetdiva.id` (0) answered.

### What tranche 2 measured, as distinct from what it found

Three of tranche 1's eight named holes were swept in tranche 2 and the results are negative rather than absent, which is a different and more useful thing to be able to say.

1. **The convenience-chain vein is now swept and is genuinely near-empty at this instrument.** `Alfamart` returned 69 results and `Indomaret` 65 across detik plus Kompas, and after removing the daily promo listicle every single one was noise. Exactly **one** convenience row survives in the whole corpus - FamilyMart x Crayon Shinchan, row 61 - and it surfaced *inside* a promo listicle rather than in a campaign announcement. The correct statement is therefore not "the vein was not swept" but "Indonesian consumer press does not report convenience-chain character campaigns as campaigns; it reports them as prices." `infobrand.id`'s 2022 Indomaret Pokémon merchandise item shows the campaigns themselves exist.
2. **Telco, e-wallet and marketplace formats are swept and empty.** `Telkomsel` (41 hits), `Indosat` (38), `Tokopedia` (48) and `Blibli` (38) return corporate, infrastructure, layoff and CSR news almost exclusively. Not one character campaign appeared. Telco and e-wallet look like genuinely closed formats in Indonesia rather than unswept ones, and the verdict can say so with a measurement behind it.
3. **Artist-name search is the strongest measurement in the tranche, and it is the one that bears on the mechanism.** Ten named Indonesian illustrators and art-toy artists were queried as single rare tokens: `Darbotz` (50 results), `Monez` (29), `Tuyuloveme` (30), `Ykha` (33), `Ardneks` (26), `Stereoflow` (47) and `Funcican` (18) returned **zero** brand-campaign hits between them; `Muklay` (50) returned one; `Arkiv` (49) returned only own-exhibition coverage. The bare word `ilustrator` (31 results) returned zero brand campaigns. Meanwhile `Tahilalats` alone returned nine. Indonesian consumer press covers *one* domestic character IP as a brand partner and effectively does not cover independent illustrators as brand partners at all - and the independent artist rows this census does hold (Muklay, Darbotz, Abenk Alter, Arkiv Vilmansa, Kadek Wiwindari) were each found through the **brand's** announcement, never through the artist's name. That asymmetry is a measurement about press behaviour and it is discussed under the mechanism section.

### What tranche 3 measured, and what it overturned

Tranche 3 re-ran two of tranche 2's headline measurements on the new instrument, and both came back differently.
This is recorded in full because the coverage-confidence section has to say which of the two readings the verdict rests on.

1. **The artist-name measurement inverts on this vein, and this is the most consequential correction in the Indonesian census.** Tranche 2 queried ten named Indonesian illustrators on detik and Kompas and found essentially zero brand campaigns, and concluded that Indonesian press does not cover independent illustrators as brand partners. Re-run across the eleven Instrument G portals, the same names return brand campaigns: `Muklay` 18 distinct articles including the TBW wheels row and a second source for the Digimap row, `Darbotz` 7 including an Acer Predator collaboration and a Botanical Essentials collaboration, `Ykha Amelz` a Lacoste Ramadan store campaign, `Ardneks` a Jägermeister packaging campaign, `Monez` a bateeq collection, `Stereoflow` a Specs collaboration, `Tuyuloveme` international mural coverage, `Abenk Alter` NFT-exhibition coverage. Four of those became rows (65, 66, 68, 71) and four are **out of window** (Acer Predator x Darbotz 2020, bateeq x Monez 2022, Specs x Stereoflow 2022, Botanical Essentials x Darbotz 2023, ATARU x Muklay 2023, Coach x Muklay 2022). The corrected statement is therefore: **independent Indonesian illustrators do generate brand-campaign coverage, continuously since at least 2020, but only in the youth-lifestyle portal network - the mainstream news portals do not carry it.** Tranche 2's finding was an artefact of which publishers were being asked, and the honest reading is that the artist-side vein was never swept before tranche 3, not that it was swept and empty.
2. **The convenience-chain hole partly reopens for the same reason.** Tranche 2 measured `Alfamart` at 69 hits and `Indomaret` at 65 on detik plus Kompas, found only dated promo listicles, and concluded that Indonesian press reports convenience character campaigns as prices rather than as campaigns. Row 102 is an Indomaret x Pokémon programme in two dated 2026 waves with a named Indomarco Prismatama executive, and it is on IDN Times. The campaign announcement existed; the instrument was not asking the publisher that carries it. Alfamart still returns nothing on either vein, so the format is thin rather than closed.
3. **Twenty-one existing rows gained an independent second source**, which is a coverage measurement rather than a new-row measurement, and it materially lowers the single-source count the verdict has to report. The corroborating URLs are listed under "Tranche 3 corroboration" below.
4. **What stayed measured-empty.** Telco and e-wallet remain zero rows across all three tranches and all three instruments. The bare Bahasa artist-side phrases (`ilustrator lokal kolaborasi`, `seniman kolaborasi brand`, `komikus kolaborasi`, `karakter lokal`) returned **zero** slug-confirmed hits even on the working portals: the vein is reachable through *names*, never through *job titles*, which is a real property of how this press writes and not an instrument limit.

**What failed.**
Google News RSS was not attempted: the `batchexecute` resolver has been permanently blocked since the Hong Kong run and nothing suggests that has changed.
`liputan6.com/search?q=` looks like it works and does not: it returns HTTP 200 with 69 article links on every page, but the links are identical for every query and every page number, because it is serving the channel-latest list rather than search results.
Any sweep that trusted its HTTP status without checking result *variance* across pages would have silently ingested 69 irrelevant URLs per query.
`kumparan.com`, `idntimes.com` and `tribunnews.com` search were recorded in tranche 2 as client-rendered with unlocated JSON backends; tranche 3 established that **`idntimes.com` is server-rendered and was never actually probed** (see Instrument G), and this sentence is left standing as written because the error is instructive.
`kumparan.com` genuinely resists: its `sitemap.xml` returns 403 behind a maintenance-style interstitial and its `/search` markup carries no result links.
`tribunnews.com` returns 403 on `/search` while serving its sitemap index normally, so a sitemap walk remains possible there and was not attempted.

**The critical query-shape finding: both Indonesian search engines are OR-plus-recency, not relevance.**
This cost real time before it was diagnosed and it inverts normal search hygiene.
Querying `kolaborasi Sanrio Indonesia` on Kompas returns the latest national news containing *Indonesia* - Bank Indonesia, timnas Indonesia, ekonomi Indonesia - and not one Sanrio result.
Adding a qualifying word to a query makes it strictly worse, because the commonest token in the query dominates the result set.
The working query shape is a **single rare token** (an IP name, a creator name, a distinctive brand name), with **client-side confirmation that the token actually appears in the returned headline or slug**.
That confirmation step is the Indonesian analogue of Vietnam's rescore-on-the-accented-title pass: recall from the search engine, precision from a local check.

**Indonesian false friends and noise classes, recorded because each one cost filter precision.**
Bahasa Indonesia is plain-ASCII Latin with no diacritics, so Vietnam's entire class of accent-collapse traps simply does not exist here and slug matching is safe.
The Indonesian problem is semantic rather than orthographic, and it is worse.
- **`karakter` usually does not mean a character IP.** It means *personality*, and Indonesia runs three separate high-volume streams that use it that way: character education (`pendidikan karakter`, `bangun karakter siswa`, a standing national schools policy), football commentary (`karakter permainan`), and automotive/lifestyle copy (`karakter jalan`, `karakter shio`, `parfum sesuai karakter`). Bare `karakter` is close to useless as a co-branding signal and the scorer does not credit it alone.
- **`kolaborasi` usually does not mean a brand collaboration.** Indonesian corporate, ministerial and CSR communication uses it as the default word for any institutional partnership: `kolaborasi lintas kementerian`, `kolaborasi pengasuhan`, `kolaborasi pengelolaan sampah`, `asosiasi siap kolaborasi dengan pemerintah`.
- **The daily promo-listicle stream is the single largest noise source in this market.** `money.kompas.com` and its peers publish multiple articles *per day* per retail chain in the form `promo indomaret 25 juli 2026`, `promo JSM alfamart`, `harga minyak goreng di indomaret`. Querying any Indonesian convenience or grocery brand name returns almost nothing else. Of the first 40 results for `Indomaret karakter`, 30-plus were dated promo listicles and none was a character campaign. Chain-brand tokens are therefore low-yield in Indonesia despite the chains being exactly where the task expected character campaigns to be, and that gap is a genuine open question rather than a settled absence.
- **`maskot` frequently means a sports or event mascot**, not a licensed character fronting a consumer campaign, and government-commissioned mascots are excluded outright under the standing Kumamon rule.
- **`Kiko`** is both an Indonesian animated character IP and a Persik Kediri footballer, and in 2024-2026 the footballer dominates completely.

### The INFIA question - the ownership call that decides this market

Indonesia's most brand-active domestic character IP is **Tahilalats**, the webcomic strip created by Nurfadli Mursyid.
Indonesian press consistently credits him and describes Tahilalats as a `komikus` or `kreator komik lokal`, which is exactly the pattern that would make it creator-owned and INDEPENDENT under the 咖波 precedent.
It is not, and three independent pieces of evidence say so.

1. InJourney's own campaign release describes the counterparty as **Infia Corp** and the characters as `karakter khas Infia seperti Tahilalats, Dudu, dan Funcican` - Infia's own characters, plural, as a set (https://news.detik.com/berita/d-8003556/injourney-gaet-tahilalats-cs-meriahkan-libur-sekolah-di-bandara-lokasi-wisata).
2. INFIA Corp's own portfolio page lists **Tahilalats under the heading `IP CHARACTER`**, as one of "40+ Intellectual Properties", alongside Hai Dudu, M.C.Bunny, Meowlove and Vernalta (https://infiacorp.com/portfolio).
3. INFIA Corp's milestone timeline describes the company's trajectory as `2020 Full Transition to IP Business`, `2021 Investing in Emerging IPs - Investment to Several High-Potential IP`, and `2022 ... set the foundation for an IP investment company`, having `Received investment from a major firm` in 2018 (https://infiacorp.com/about). Its June 2026 news entry announces new outside investors joining its **Board of Commissioners**.

That is a third-party corporation with outside equity holders describing character IP as portfolio assets it invested in.
Under the pinned test - `portfolio = corporation/estate/platform/franchise-owned (or majority stake)` - and under the Korea webtoon rule as stated (creator owns *unless the platform demonstrably does*), **Tahilalats is PORTFOLIO**.
The 咖波 precedent does not rescue it, because that precedent covers a creator who retains ownership while an agency handles commercial representation, and INFIA does not present itself as an agent.

**The residual doubt is recorded rather than hidden.**
The pinned rule that *self-presentation is not ownership* cuts both ways: a portfolio page is INFIA's self-presentation, and it is not a share register.
No document examined in this tranche states the size of INFIA's stake in Tahilalats, and it remains possible that Nurfadli Mursyid retains majority ownership while INFIA holds a minority position and does the licensing.
The rows are therefore classed PORTFOLIO with the trail above recorded on each, and this is flagged as the **single highest-value open question in the Indonesian census**: it is worth six rows directly, and it is the difference between Indonesia reading as a market with a healthy independent share and a market whose independent IP has been bought up.

**The contrast case, which is why the INFIA call is not simply anti-creator.**
**Si Juki** is created by Faza Meonk and licensed through **Pionicon**, and Pionicon is Faza Meonk's own studio: its site describes Si Juki as `Our most famous creation`, dates the company to the character's 2014 debut, and offers brand work as a service (https://pionicon.com/).
A creator's own studio is the creator, so Si Juki is **INDEPENDENT**.
The distinction applied throughout this table is therefore ownership *by whom*, not mere presence of a corporate vehicle: a creator-founded studio is independent, a third-party IP investment company holding the asset is portfolio.

### Which mechanism Indonesia looks like, on tranche-1 and tranche-2 evidence

The prediction for this market was erasure-plus-thin-press, offered as a fifth-pattern candidate: campaigns invisible not because press omits creators but because campaigns barely reach indexed press at all.
**Neither tranche supports either half of that, though tranche 2 narrows the claim considerably - see "Tranche 2 strengthens the consolidation reading" below.**
Indonesian press reaches the campaigns and names the creators, often in the headline: `Advan Gandeng Darbotz`, `Barcode Gokart Gandeng Seniman Muklay`, `Digimap Kolaborasi dengan Muklay`, `Alba Gandeng Arkiv`, `Mooi Heartmade ... Kolaborasi Seniman Lokal, Muklay sampai Hari Prast`.
Creator-naming in Indonesia is markedly *better* than in the Thailand and Malaysia erasure pattern, and the domestic character scene is unambiguously real rather than pre-formation as in Vietnam.

What the evidence points at instead is **consolidation**: domestic character IP exists, it is commercially strong, it reaches airlines, banks, mall operators, FMCG and state-owned transport operators - and by the time it gets there it has been aggregated into a corporate IP portfolio.
All eight INDEPENDENT rows in this table are, without exception, **individual artists licensing artwork onto a product** (Muklay, Darbotz, Abenk Alter, Arkiv Vilmansa, Hari Prast, and Kadek Wiwindari added in tranche 2) plus one creator-owned character studio on the MIXED row (Si Juki / Pionicon).
Tranche 2 tested that generalisation on 29 fresh rows and it held: the one independent row it added is another artwork-licensing row, and it produced no new creator-owned *character* IP at all.
The domestic *characters* with repeat blue-chip brand programmes - Tahilalats, Hai Dudu, Funcican, Mind Blowon - are portfolio assets of one company.
That is a distinct sixth shape from the four carried forward and from the fifth candidate predicted here, and if it survives the remaining queue it should be named in the verdict.
It was explicitly not claimed as settled on tranche 1's 32 rows, and at 61 rows it is better evidenced but still hangs on the INFIA ownership call above.

**Tranche 2 strengthens the consolidation reading on two independent counts and adds one complication.**

*First, a second corporate IP holder.* Row 54 pairs 3Second with **Sekuya**, presented in Indonesian brand press as "IP lokal Sekuya" and by the Deputy Minister for the Creative Economy as evidence that "IP kita sudah mendunia".
Sekuya turns out to be structurally the same object as INFIA: an ecosystem company with an explicit "IP House" holding several character properties (Alice, Starla, SKYA), plus a racing team, a music arm and a Web3 token (https://sekuya.io/).
Indonesia now has *two* independently found examples of domestic character IP sitting inside a corporate holder rather than with a creator, and they were found in the same tranche without looking for the second.
One company is an anecdote; two is the beginning of a structure, and it is the structure that the consolidation reading needs.

*Second, the artist-name measurement.* The measurement recorded under "What tranche 2 measured" cuts against the confident half of the tranche-1 mechanism claim.
Tranche 1 said Indonesian press names creators well, and it does - in the headline of the *brand's* announcement.
But ten named Indonesian illustrators queried directly returned essentially nothing, and the bare word `ilustrator` returned nothing, while `Tahilalats` returned nine hits.
So the accurate statement is narrower than tranche 1's: **Indonesian press names the creator when it covers the campaign, but it only covers the campaign when the brand announces it, and independent illustrators do not generate campaign coverage of their own.**
That is not the Thailand/Malaysia erasure pattern - the names are there when the story runs - and it is not Vietnam's pre-formation - the scene exists and is productive.
It is a **discoverability asymmetry sitting on top of consolidation**: the one domestic character IP that has been aggregated into a portfolio is press-visible across formats, and the unaggregated individual artists are visible only through whichever brand happens to issue a release.
That combination is what the verdict should name, and it is now supported by a measurement rather than by an impression.

*The complication.* Tranche 2 added 29 rows and only **one** independent (row 60, WRP x Kadek Wiwindari), so the independent share fell from 22% to 13% as coverage widened.
That direction matters: broadening the instrument did not surface hidden independents, it surfaced more portfolio licensing.
Two readings remained open at the end of tranche 2 - either Indonesia's independent share genuinely is low and tranche 1 overestimated it on a small sample, or independent activity is real but systematically absent from the brand-announcement channel that Instruments E and F sample.

### What tranche 3 does to the mechanism reading

Tranche 3 settles the tranche-2 dilemma in a way that neither branch anticipated, and the resolution is a **third statement** rather than a choice between the two.

*The consolidation finding survives and hardens.* Rows 62-102 add four further Tahilalats programmes (rows 78, 90, 96, 97) reaching a mall operator, a Chinese carmaker's Indonesian arm, a taxi and mobility group, and a footwear-plus-herbal-medicine three-way, and a second Sekuya programme (row 74).
Tahilalats now appears on **ten rows** across airline, carmaker, metro, mall, FMCG, footwear, motorcycle, state tourism, taxi and installation formats, and Sekuya on two - and both are corporately held.
Tranche 3 produced **no new creator-owned character IP with a repeat brand programme**: the one domestic character IP that repeats across formats is the one inside a portfolio, exactly as tranches 1 and 2 found.
The Si Juki row (68) is the strongest counter-instance in the census and it is a single collectible drop, not a programme.

*The discoverability asymmetry was half instrument and half market, and the halves must be separated.* Tranche 2 said Indonesian press names creators when it covers campaigns but does not cover independent illustrators as brand partners at all.
The first clause holds; the second was an artefact of asking the wrong publishers.
Independent illustrators have generated continuous brand-campaign coverage in Indonesia since at least 2020 - Coach, Acer Predator, Specs, bateeq, Lacoste, Jägermeister, ATARU, Botanical Essentials, TBW, Digimap - but it lives **entirely in the youth-lifestyle portal network** and does not cross into the mainstream news portals that carry the portfolio campaigns.
So Indonesia is not press-erasure (Thailand, Malaysia), not pre-formation (Vietnam), and not segregation-by-tier in the Korean sense.
What it exhibits is **two-channel licensing**: the same market runs a corporate-portfolio channel that reaches national news, ministries, state-owned enterprises and blue-chip brands, and an independent-artist channel that reaches mid-size consumer brands and is reported only in lifestyle press - with almost no overlap in either the brands or the publishers.
That is the shape the verdict names, and the practical consequence for an agency is stated there: the independent side of this market is not hidden, it is indexed somewhere else.

*Honesty about the direction of drift.* The independent share moved 21.9% (32 rows) -> 13.1% (61 rows) -> 12.7% (102 rows).
Opening the vein that carries independent-artist coverage did **not** lift the ratio, because the same vein carries an even larger volume of portfolio licensing.
The ratio is converging, and it is converging low.

## Exclusions applied

**Government-owned mascots (Kumamon rule).** No row in this tranche rests on a government-owned mascot.
Note that Indonesian government bodies appear repeatedly on the *brand* side - Kemenparekraf/Kementerian Ekonomi Kreatif on rows 1 and 13, a state tourism holding on row 17, a provincially-owned metro operator on row 27 - and this does not trigger the rule, which excludes government-owned *IP*.
That said, the density of ministerial presence in Indonesian character campaigns is itself a finding: the creative-economy ministry co-brands domestic IP activations as policy, which is one reason this market's domestic IP is visible in press at all.

**OWN-MASCOT exclusion.** A brand's proprietary mascot on its own campaign is not a row.

**Platform's own retail, excluded.** POP MART opening its own first Bandung store (2025-08-23, https://www.detik.com/jabar/berita/d-8075636/pop-mart-buka-gerai-pertama-di-bandung-kolektor-antusias-borong) is the IP owner operating its own retail, not a brand licensing a third-party character in. Excluded, while rows 11 and 22 - a mall operator and an e-commerce platform contracting with Pop Mart - are included.

**Multi-IP trade expos, excluded.** Amazing Toy Show Jakarta 2026 at Gandaria City Mall (2026-06-19, https://hot.detik.com/art/d-8538364/faza-meonk-bangga-artis-lokal-bersanding-dengan-ip-dunia-di-amazing-toy-show), IP Expo Indonesia 2026, Jakarta IP Market 2026 (https://marketing.co.id/jakarta-ip-market-2026-hadirkan-marketplace-licensing-untuk-dorong-ip-lokal-go-global) and FanCon 2025 are licensing fairs and fan conventions, not campaigns in which a single character IP fronts a brand.
They are recorded here because they are strong evidence about the market's licensing infrastructure, and because Faza Meonk's remarks at the first of them are the clearest statement in this corpus of how Indonesian creators see their position relative to global IP.

**Own-work exhibitions and publications, excluded.** Arkiv Vilmansa's "Semesta Arkiv" at Galeri Nasional (2025-02), Emte's "Life As I Know It" book launch (2025-07), Faza Meonk and Mice Misrad's comic "Proposal dari Rakyat" (2024-03), and Si Juki's crossover with Tezuka's Black Jack (2025-09) are the creators' own work or IP-to-IP publishing, with no brand licensing a character into a consumer campaign.
