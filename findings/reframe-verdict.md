## Honest confidence line

Overall confidence is **medium-high on the brand side and medium on the creator side**, and the difference is structural, not effort.
The brand-side verdicts rest on roughly twenty curl-verified brand or practitioner voices across zh-CN and zh-TW, with the segment pattern showing zero counter-examples (no small brand stating a perception goal, no brand publishing incrementality or repeat-purchase numbers).
The creator-side verdicts rest on fewer character-IP first-person voices (Cherng is the load-bearing case), padded with adjacent-industry evidence (MCN, music royalties, commission platforms) that transfers imperfectly, and the performance-failure complaint category still rests on the MCN record plus our own n=1 Jolly Gee interview.
Known biases: stated-goal evidence is public PR by CMO-level people and plausibly under-reports the internal money question; the measurable-revenue record is survivorship-filtered 战报 culture; two search-summary phantom quotes were caught and excluded this run, so anything not curl-verified here should be assumed unverifiable.
Nothing found contradicts the two hypotheses' refined forms; what died were their universal forms.

## (a) Is "brands only care about collab profit" universally true, segment-true, or over-generalized - and what should the problem statement say?

It is an over-generalization from n=1 that turns out to be approximately true for a definable segment.
Every small brand, franchisee, and merch-P&L operator in the record speaks in break-even math (a NT$15M Disney break-even, gross-margin payback, renewal decided on last collab's sales), and zero small brands state a perception goal; meanwhile China's most collab-famous heritage brands state rejuvenation as the sole or first goal, so the famous-brand marketing seat is approximately the opposite of H1.
The deeper finding is that the stated goal collapses toward what the speaker can PROVE: gross campaign-SKU revenue is measurable to the cup within a ~30-day attribution window, while incrementality, repeat purchase, and halo are measured by nobody, so revenue is less what brands uniquely want than the only number that exists.
The problem statement should therefore not say "brands only care about money."
It should say: for brands and operators where the collab itself must pay (little-known brands, franchise systems, merch P&L owners - our first customer), the collab is a spreadsheet decision made with no evidence base; for famous-brand marketing seats, perception goals are real but defensible only via a famous IP nobody questions or a senior sponsor who personally shields the bet (茅台's program died when its chairman left) - and both segments lack a measurement system that would make the choice justifiable upward.

## (b) What do creators actually buy from agencies - outcomes, visibility, or margin - and in what order?

Creators buy OUTCOMES first, and the record is unusually unanimous about it: every first-person "why I signed" statement is a version of "so I can just draw" (Cherng twice, five years apart; the US-agency insider essay; even the MCN sales pitch weaponizes 专心创作), and the lawyer's taxonomy of what agencies supply - 資源 (deal flow) and 專業 (execution capability) - names two outcome inputs, neither of which is involvement.
Second, and only revealed when things break, they need the agency to be a trustworthy PIPE for their own money: the defining character-IP failure cases (Cherng's unpaid LINE revenue, G.E.M.'s six years of zero royalties) are payment-integrity failures on splits nobody disputed, which is why "margin" ranks last - the flagship Taiwan grievance happened on a 70/30 creator-favorable split.
Visibility is never purchased up front and never demanded up front; it is the thing whose absence lets payment and performance failures stay hidden until they become exits.
So the order is: outcomes, then payment integrity, then visibility (post-failure), with margin percentage effectively absent from the verified complaint record - and "proof the agency is any good" is the plausible product wedge precisely because creators currently have no way to buy it.

## (c) Does the evidence support Marrow's more-creator-agency philosophy, a hands-off-but-provably-good model, or the third position?

The evidence weakens the more-creator-agency philosophy and points clearly at the third position: outcomes plus package-tracking visibility, without control burden.
No creator anywhere in the record asks an agency for deal approval, negotiation seats, or partner choice; the middle position Marrow's original philosophy occupies - a represented creator holding decision rights inside someone else's agency - is demanded by nobody, and creators who are severely burned skip it entirely, escalating to ownership (幾米 co-founding 墨色國際, G.E.M. re-recording her masters) or exit (Cherng going independent) rather than to involvement.
Visibility, by contrast, is demanded loudly - but specifically over money flows and deal existence (金流透明公開, real-vs-fabricated deals), always after a failure, and the LINE sticker dashboard case suggests the absence of pre-burn demands reflects unimagined supply rather than absent utility: where a tracking interface exists, creators engage avidly without ever having asked for it, and the commission market's dominant trust product (米画师's stage-gated escrow) is literally package-tracking.
Two amendments from the record: the tracking must span the FULL deal-to-payout path, because partial-path transparency gets discounted as PR (Spotify publishes Loud & Clear and musician distrust persists, since the opaque label-split layer sits outside it); and the highest-value single view is probably payment status, not deal pipeline, because payment integrity is the failure mode that actually burns character-IP creators.

## (d) Do free-for-exposure licensing arrangements exist in the wild, and are they framed as growth or exploitation?

They exist, at institutional scale, and every practitioner found frames them as deliberate growth strategy (tally on licensing instances: growth 4, goodwill 1, exploitation 0).
Kumamoto Prefecture made all domestic Kumamon licensing free 为了扩大宣传, built a 100-billion-yen-a-year merchandise economy on zero fees, then introduced a 5-7% overseas fee tier in 2018 once the IP was proven - the exact free-then-fee lifecycle the founder's "marketing licensing" idea hypothesizes; Crypton runs the same logic as a standing policy (free non-commercial tier, consent-gated commercial tier), Disney states the proven-IP end (fees as market segmentation because it no longer needs exposure), and 零保底纯分成 sits in the standard fee menu as the near-free entry structure.
The qualifier: every verified practitioner is an institution (a prefecture, a software company, a top agency, a civic-character operator); no independent creator was found practicing or denouncing free licensing first-person, while the creator community's settled rule in the adjacent commission market is 曝光不等於收入 - exposure is an input to convert, not a payment to accept.
So a product offering marketing licensing to independent creators would be genuinely new rather than a copy of observed behavior, and it must make the free stage bank something provable (performance data, a fee-justifying track record) or it will land inside the community's justified exposure-skepticism.
