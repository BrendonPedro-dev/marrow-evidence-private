## The chain, in derivation order

1. Both sides of licensing want the same thing: profit. But the mechanisms are
   opposite - creators profit PASSIVELY (the asset earns; they approve and
   watch), brands profit ACTIVELY (they spend, execute, and must prove it
   worked). One machine, two faces: tracking for the passive side, evidence
   for the active side.
2. Ads vs IP, functionally: the ad BUSINESS sells distribution (getting the
   product in front of people); what persuades is the content - the reason
   inside. An IP is a PRE-BUILT REASON the audience already loves. The ad
   industry industrialized distribution; nobody industrialized reasons.
3. Collab value splits asymmetrically: the IP wins from every impression
   (seen = fandom grows, paid either way); the brand wins only if seeing
   becomes buying. The brand carries the conditional half of the deal - yet
   fees are priced on the IP's fame (the seller's half). "Price on fit, not
   fame" = price the buyer's half.
4. Therefore the only thing a brand is actually buying is CONVERSION
   PROBABILITY with their audience: "will this IP increase the chance people
   buy?" That is the question the company exists to answer with a number.
5. Why budgets flow to ads and not licensing: not the goal (both vague, both
   profit-seeking) but the JUSTIFICATION SYSTEM. Ads have shared metrics
   (reach, GRPs, ROAS, brand lift) - none prove profit, all make spend
   defensible; a manager can say yes safely. Licensing has no receipt, so it
   gets judged on brutal direct results. The score's job is not to close the
   deal - it is to protect whoever said yes.
6. The template sentence (the product spec): the ad pitch is "a million
   people will see it during the World Cup break" - a solid number, no
   promise, buyer concludes. Ours: "600K of this IP's fans are in Taiwan,
   6 in 10 match your customers, similar pairings lifted sales X%." Numbers,
   no promise, one step closer to the wallet.
7. Auditability asymmetry: ads are unfalsifiable (seeing can never be traced
   to buying - safe from blame, locked out of learning). Ours WILL be
   audited (campaign ends, sales counted). Discipline: claim only what
   survives the audit - counts, overlaps, historical facts, never forecasted
   promises. Ads win by being unfalsifiable; we win by being auditable and
   right.
8. Visible failure is the raw material: a failed collab shows (the Toy Story
   mug that sat; clearance stickers), a failed ad never does. Visibility
   scared brand budgets (HEYTEA, the 41% pullback) - and it is exactly what
   makes the ODDS TABLE buildable. Lottery frame: winners are public, losing
   tickets silent, so nobody knows the odds; we are the only ones counting
   both directions. [Supported: the corroboration sweep's zero-public-failure
   finding; the shelf audit as the counting instrument.]
9. Pitch evolution: today's pitch is social proof ("Pizza Hut is doing it") -
   legitimate but incomplete (answers "is this normal," not "will it work for
   ME"). Upgrade in layers: keep social proof -> add fit evidence (fan count,
   customer overlap, identity match - the existing assessment) -> close with
   honest odds (directional comparables now, real benchmark rows later).
10. Cold start for new IPs: price by CLASS, insurance-style - similar-trait
    IPs' track records stand in until the IP earns its own rows, comparison
    always visible, labeled estimate. The shelf data (53 IPs by style and
    category) is the class table forming.
11. Match, then human: class/fit matching proposes brand candidates; the
    human agent takes it from there. Supply side low-touch (package-tracking
    visibility, approve list, ask-the-AI); demand side human-led and
    AI-armed - resources follow the money.
12. IP LIFECYCLE STRATEGY (Gary, end of day): every IP needs an overall
    strategy based on its status. Some command no fee - "marketing
    licensing": license free or near-free for publicity, exposure, retail
    presence, AND the performance data. The data flywheel and the IP's
    growth path are the same motion: early deals buy odds-table rows; proven
    IPs graduate to fee terms. [Tested tonight as stress-test Q8.]

## One-line compressions (exact-wording candidates for the audit)
- Ads sell "how many will see it"; we sell "how much more likely they'll buy it."
- The score doesn't close the deal - it protects whoever said yes.
- Ads win by being unfalsifiable; we win by being auditable and right.
- Everyone sees the jackpot winners; nobody counts the losing tickets - we
  count both, and counting both is what tells you the odds.
- New IPs are priced by class until they earn their own odds.
- Prove that people will buy your product because of this IP on it. (Gary -
