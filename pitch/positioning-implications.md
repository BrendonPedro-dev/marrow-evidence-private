# Positioning Implications — what the research changes in the VC pitch

*Pitch file — this is interpretation, not new research. Every factual claim traces back to
the four `/research/` files (`negosh-apac.md`, `quantified-licensing-landscape.md`,
`consumer-product-gap.md`, `data-availability.md`) and to the re-graded assumptions in
`assumption-audit.md`. Nothing new is asserted about any company here. Tags: **[CONFIRMED]**
= multi-source research backs it; **[ASSUMED]** = reasonable inference, not proven.*

**Purpose:** The audit did three things to the pitch — it (1) killed a naive line
("competitors are qualitative"), (2) handed us two named products that look like threats but
are actually demand validation (IPverse, Negosh-Japan), and (3) sharpened the wedge to one
sentence ("the last unquantified segment"). This file says how to *present* that in the room:
the reframe, the narrative order, the objection pre-empts, and 3–5 exact sentences Gary can
say, each tagged.

---

## 1. The one-line repositioning

**Before the audit:** "Licensing is done on gut; we bring data." — *unsafe.* A VC names
Circana, CharaBiz, Alifish, or IPverse and the line collapses. `assumption-audit.md §(a)`.

**After the audit:** "Licensing is drowning in data — everywhere except the one decision that
matters. We quantify the pairing." — *defensible.* The market being full of quantification is
now an **asset** to the story, not a contradiction: it proves buyers already pay for licensing
data, and it isolates the gap to a single decision. `quantified-licensing-landscape.md §3–6`,
`consumer-product-gap.md §1`.

The shift: **from "we add data to a gut-feel market" → to "we close the one gap in an
already-data-rich market."** The second is harder to rebut and flatters the VC's instinct that
big markets aren't empty.

## 2. Turn the two "threats" into demand validation

The audit surfaced three products a VC could raise as "isn't someone already doing this?" The
first two (IPverse, Negosh-Japan) should be **volunteered by Gary first**, framed as proof the
market is forming — not dodged. The third (Sidechat IP, §2c) is a 2026 look-alike to dispatch
cleanly if a VC who walked Licensing Expo 2026 raises it.

### 2a. Yodo1 IPverse (in-game IP × brand collabs)
- **What it actually is** `quantified-licensing-landscape.md §2`: a free data-intelligence
  platform for *in-game* IP collabs — recommendation-from-historical-collabs + popularity
  quadrants (DeepMatch proposal generation), unveiled ChinaJoy Jul 2025, free public launch
  ~Jan 2026. It is **not** an outcome-validated fit predictor, and it does **not** touch
  physical consumer products.
- **The reframe — demand validation, not competition:**
  > **[CONFIRMED]** "A $-backed games publisher just built a fit-matching engine and gave it
  > away free — that's the strongest signal in the market that quantified IP matching is
  > coming. They did it for *in-game* collabs, where the interaction data is born digital.
  > The physical-product side — the bigger, older market — has no equivalent. IPverse is our
  > proof the category is real, in the one vertical that isn't ours."
- **Why this works:** IPverse validates the *thesis* (fit can be quantified and buyers want it)
  while confirming the *gap* (it's digital-native and games-only). It de-risks the market
  question and leaves the physical wedge untouched. `consumer-product-gap.md §2b`.
- **Guardrails:** ❌ Never call IPverse a direct competitor (different vertical). ❌ Never
  attribute in-game collab counts (~80k events / ~20k brands) to PBC's segment. ❌ Never
  confuse Yodo1's ipverse.com with the Kowloon 2021 blockchain "IPVERSE" — different company,
  attribute every time. `quantified-licensing-landscape.md §2, entity-collision guard`.

### 2b. Negosh-Japan / InfoLens
- **What it actually is** `negosh-apac.md §3–4`: Negosh's *only* substantiated APAC presence is
  Japan, **partner-operated** by InfoLens Inc. (Tokyo). Deal volume is **undisclosed**. The
  broader Negosh roadmap is visibly pivoting toward digital/AI (Spaceport, Made AI, negosh.ai),
  not APAC geographic expansion.
- **The reframe — demand validation + open ground, not a beachhead:**
  > **[CONFIRMED]** "The US marketplace everyone points to — Negosh — bothered to enter Japan,
  > which tells you APAC licensing is worth entering. But they did it through a single local
  > partner, disclosed no deal volume, and never came to Taiwan, Korea, China, or Southeast
  > Asia. Their *presence* validates the region; their *thinness* is our opening."
- **Why this works:** Negosh entering Japan is evidence APAC matters; Negosh being Japan-only,
  partner-dependent, and traction-silent is evidence the region is under-served — both cut in
  PBC's favor without claiming Negosh failed. `assumption-audit.md §(b)`.
- **Guardrails:** ❌ Never say "Negosh failed / has low traction in Japan" — traction is
  **UNKNOWN**, say undisclosed. ❌ Never quote any Negosh brand/licensee count as traction —
  the counts are inconsistent across their own surfaces (800+/1000+ vs 500+/400 vs 250+/200+)
  and are *inventory*, not deals. `negosh-apac.md §3`.

### 2c. Sidechat IP (pre-deal AI scoring in licensing — but the wrong object)
- **What it actually is** `consumer-product-gap.md §2h`: Sidechat IP ("Sidechat for IP
  Licensing," shown at Licensing Expo 2026; a Spaceport Technologies product per its own page —
  **UNVERIFIED, vendor-stated, no independent coverage/customers/traction**) markets an AI
  **"deal intake scoring"** feature that runs *before* signing: inbound license requests are
  captured and **ranked by "deal quality"** in ~60 seconds. But the page says it scores
  **revenue history, IP compliance, and completeness of the *applicant*** — it triages which
  inbound licensee leads are strongest/most legitimate. The rest (deal/royalty monitoring,
  marketplace infringement scanning, contract drafting, pipeline CRM) is standard type-B admin.
- **The reframe — timing validation, wrong object:**
  > **[CONFIRMED]** "If a VC brings up Sidechat IP — yes, someone is finally putting AI scoring
  > at the pre-deal moment in licensing, in 2026. That validates our *timing*. But read what it
  > scores: it ranks inbound *applicants* by revenue history and compliance — which licensee
  > lead is legit. It does not score whether a given character and a given product will sell
  > *together*. Even the newest AI entrant scores the wrong object. The pairing decision is
  > still open."
- **Why this works:** Sidechat IP is the strongest surface-level "isn't this already built?"
  objection, and it *helps* the pitch once dissected — it proves pre-deal AI scoring in
  licensing is now a marketed category (category-timing validation) while confirming the
  pairing-fit gap is still unfilled (it scores applicant deal-quality, not IP × product fit).
  `consumer-product-gap.md §2h, §3`.
- **Guardrails:** ❌ Never call Sidechat IP a proven competitor — its claims are **UNVERIFIED**
  (vendor-stated only); don't attribute any traction to it. ❌ Never confuse it with the
  unrelated "Sidechat" anonymous-campus app (side.chat / sidechat.lol). ⚠️ Its page attributes
  it to "Spaceport Technologies," a name that also appears in the Negosh Dec-2024 timeline
  (`negosh-apac.md`) — do **not** assert any connection; it is unconfirmed and irrelevant to the
  reframe.

## 3. The "last unquantified segment" framing

This is the wedge sentence and it is the strongest thing the audit produced. It works because
the audit mapped where quantification *has* landed and where it *hasn't*, and the pattern is
clean:

| Vertical | Is fit quantified pre-deal? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Influencer × brand | **Yes** (Sprout Brand Fit Score, Traackr, HypeAuditor) | Interaction data born digital `quantified-licensing-landscape.md §2` |
| In-game IP × brand | **Yes-ish** (Yodo1 IPverse, unvalidated) | Collab/engagement data born digital `quantified-licensing-landscape.md §2` |
| **Character IP × physical consumer product** | **No player found** | Performance data is fragmented, retrospective, self-reported `data-availability.md` |

- **The framing:** quantification lands **first where the data is digital** and **last where it
  is physical** — physical consumer-product licensing is the **last major matching market still
  run on relationships and gut.** `assumption-audit.md §(a)`, `data-availability.md §7`.
- **Exact wording:**
  > **[CONFIRMED]** "Fit scoring has already conquered the digital-native matching markets —
  > influencers, in-game collabs. It stops exactly where the data goes physical. Character-on-
  > product licensing is the last big matching market still run on relationships. That's not a
  > coincidence — it's a data-plumbing problem, and it's the segment we're built for."
- **The load-bearing honesty caveat** (`assumption-audit.md §(c)`, `data-availability.md §7`):
  the reason it's unquantified is *also* the reason it's hard — the data exists but is
  fragmented, retrospective, and self-reported, so it isn't wired into the pre-deal decision.
  Present the difficulty as the moat, not as a solved problem.
  > **[ASSUMED]** "The reason nobody's quantified this segment is the same reason it's
  > defensible: the performance data exists but it's scattered across retailers, panels, and
  > royalty reports, and nobody has wired it into the *pre-deal* decision. Doing that wiring is
  > the hard part — and it's the moat."

## 4. Narrative order for the room (5 beats)

The audit implies a specific sequence — lead with the validated market, isolate the gap, own
the geography, then be honest about the one open question.

1. **Market is real & forming** → volunteer IPverse: a funded player gave away a fit engine
   (demand proof). `§2a`
2. **Data is everywhere except the pairing** → the "clusters before/after, never on the
   decision" map. `assumption-audit.md §(a)`
3. **The last unquantified segment** → the digital-first / physical-last table. `§3`
4. **APAC/Taiwan is open for *this layer*** → Negosh Japan-only via partner, undisclosed
   traction; Taiwan has no fit-scorer; China has matchmakers (Alifish/Tencent) but not fit
   prediction. `assumption-audit.md §(b)`
5. **The one honest unknown** → whether a fit score changes brand behavior is UNTESTABLE by
   desk research; the raise funds the live-pipeline test. Present as strength, not gap.
   `assumption-audit.md §(c)`

Beat 5 is the credibility move: naming the untestable assumption *before* the VC does converts
a weakness into a signal of rigor.

## 5. The 3–5 sentences Gary can say (copy-ready, tagged)

Each is drawn verbatim-in-spirit from the sourced files; deliver in this order.

1. **[CONFIRMED]** "Licensing isn't short on data — it's drowning in it. Popularity scores and
   brand valuations sit *before* the deal, royalty and sell-through tracking sit *after* it.
   The one decision in the middle — which IP pairs with which product — is still made on gut.
   That's the slot we quantify." `assumption-audit.md §(a)`

2. **[CONFIRMED]** "Fit scoring already won the digital-native markets — influencers, in-game
   collabs; Yodo1 just gave an in-game matching engine away free. It stops the moment the data
   turns physical. Character-on-product is the last big matching market still run on
   relationships." `§2a`, `§3`

3. **[CONFIRMED]** "The US marketplace people cite — Negosh — is in APAC in exactly one country,
   Japan, through one local partner, with deal volume they've never disclosed. No Taiwan,
   Korea, China, or Southeast Asia. In Taiwan, licensing is still trade shows and government
   directories. That layer is open, and we start there." `§2b`, `assumption-audit.md §(b)`

4. **[ASSUMED]** "China isn't empty — Alibaba's Alifish and Tencent run licensing platforms at
   scale — but they do attribute matchmaking and after-the-fact sales tracking, not pre-deal
   fit prediction, and they're China-domestic. We take Taiwan and APAC-ex-China, where the fit
   layer is genuinely unoccupied." `assumption-audit.md §(b)`

5. **[ASSUMED]** "Here's the one thing we can't prove from a desk: whether a fit score actually
   changes which deals get signed. That needs the live pipeline — and that's exactly what this
   raise funds. We'd rather show you the experiment than pretend it's already settled."
   `assumption-audit.md §(c)`

*Use 1–3 as the spine; add 4 if a VC probes China/competition; use 5 to close on rigor.*

## 6. What NOT to say (carried from the audit, for the pitch deck reviewer)

- ❌ "Competitors are all qualitative / nobody uses data in licensing." False — Circana,
  CharaBiz, Brand Finance, Alifish sell numbers today. `assumption-audit.md §(a)`
- ❌ "We're the only ones who quantify fit anywhere." IPverse and Sprout quantify a *form* of
  fit in other verticals, and Sidechat IP (2026) even scores pre-deal in licensing — but of
  applicant deal-quality, not pairing fit. Say "no player we found scores IP × physical-product
  *pairing* fit," never a blanket "no one quantifies anything."
- ❌ "APAC is greenfield / no one does licensing in Asia." Japan, China, Korea all have mature
  ecosystems — reputation-damaging in a Taipei room. `assumption-audit.md §(b)`
- ❌ "Negosh failed in Japan." Traction is **undisclosed**, not known-low. `negosh-apac.md §3`
- ❌ Quoting any competitor's inventory/collab counts as if they were PBC's traction or a
  competitor's deal volume.
- ❌ Padding the behavior-change claim (assumption c) with Circana/CharaBiz/IPverse proxies —
  a sharp VC catches the substitution. `assumption-audit.md §(c)`

## 7. One-paragraph summary for the deck's positioning slide

> Physical consumer-product licensing is the **last major matching market still run on
> relationships**. Fit scoring has already been adopted where data is born digital — influencer
> marketing, in-game collabs (Yodo1's IPverse, given away free, is the proof the category is
> real). It stops where the data turns physical: character-on-product performance data exists
> but sits fragmented across panels, retailer portals, and self-reported royalty statements,
> never wired into the *pre-deal* pairing decision. In APAC that gap is wide open — the one US
> marketplace in the region (Negosh) is Japan-only through a partner with undisclosed traction,
> and Taiwan has no fit-scoring player at all. PBC quantifies the pairing decision for
> character × physical product, starting in Taiwan/APAC. **[CONFIRMED for market/geography;
> ASSUMED that a fit score changes brand behavior — the hypothesis this raise funds.]**
