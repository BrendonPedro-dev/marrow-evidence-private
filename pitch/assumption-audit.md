# Assumption Audit — the PBC/Marrow VC pitch, re-graded against evidence

*Pitch file — this is interpretation. The factual basis lives in `/research/`
(`negosh-apac.md`, `quantified-licensing-landscape.md`, `consumer-product-gap.md`,
`data-availability.md`). Every claim here traces back to those files; nothing new is
asserted about a company that isn't sourced there.*

**Purpose:** Take the three load-bearing assumptions the pitch currently leans on, replace
each with the strongest version the desk research actually supports, and give **exact
wording that is safe to say to a Taipei VC** this month. Each revised claim is tagged
**[CONFIRMED]** (multi-source research backs it), **[ASSUMED]** (reasonable inference, not
proven), or **[UNTESTABLE BY DESK RESEARCH]** (cannot be settled without the live pipeline).

The one-line discipline for the room: **do not overclaim absence.** The research supports
"no player was *found* that does this," backed by direct evidence-of-absence in the
incumbent SaaS and broad no-evidence-found elsewhere — it does **not** support "no such
tool exists anywhere." Say the first, never the second.

## Assumption (a): "Competing pitches / platforms are qualitative — they don't quantify fit"

### What the research actually found
- The consumer-product **licensing-ops** stack (Flowhaven, Octane5 BrandComply, Brainbase,
  Dependable Solutions, Xelacore/Fabacus, MyMediabox, RoyaltyZone) is **uniformly post-deal
  administration** — contracts, rights, royalty tracking, approvals, DAM. **None markets a
  *validated pre-deal fit-score.*** For **Flowhaven** this is *evidence of absence* (its own
  platform page was read directly, no fit/matchmaking feature). **Brainbase is a precise
  partial exception** to watch: its **Marketplace** product does offer partner discovery,
  curated/AI recommendations, and similarity-based matchmaking ("manufacturers who have
  worked with similar partners") — but **no validated fit *score* predicting how a specific
  IP × partner will perform**, the same recommendation-vs-prediction line that separates
  IPverse and Alifish from real fit prediction. For the rest it is *no evidence found* at
  search level. `quantified-licensing-landscape.md §3`, `consumer-product-gap.md §2a`.
- Pre-deal **fit prediction does exist — but only in adjacent digital-native verticals**:
  Yodo1's **IPverse** (in-game collabs) and Sprout Social's **Brand Fit Score**
  (influencer marketing). Both are **unvalidated as outcome predictors** —
  recommendation-from-history and alignment scoring respectively — and neither covers
  physical consumer products. `quantified-licensing-landscape.md §2`.
- There **is** quantification adjacent to the segment that is *not* qualitative: **Circana**
  measures licensed-property footprints, **CharaBiz** scores character popularity, **Brand
  Finance** values brands and royalty rates, **Alibaba Alifish** does attribute-based
  matchmaking. These are real, numeric, and sold today — but they are **popularity ranking,
  valuation, admin, or licensee-discovery**, not a **pre-deal pairing-fit forecast**.
  `quantified-licensing-landscape.md §4–6`.

### The correction the pitch needs
The blunt "competitors are qualitative / gut-feel" line is **partly wrong and unsafe** — it
invites a VC to name IPverse, Alifish, or Circana and puncture it. The defensible claim is
narrower and stronger: the market is full of quantification, but **all of it sits either
side of the pre-deal pairing decision** — popularity/valuation *before*, admin/sell-through
*after* — and **the pairing decision itself is still made qualitatively** for character ×
physical consumer product.

### Exact wording safe to say to a VC
> **[CONFIRMED]** "There's plenty of data in licensing — but it clusters at the edges of the
> deal. Popularity scores and brand valuations sit *before* it; royalty tracking and
> sell-through reporting sit *after* it. The one decision in the middle — *which* IP and
> *which* licensee to pair — is still made on relationships and gut. That's the slot we
> quantify."

> **[CONFIRMED]** "The closest anyone gets to scoring fit is in games and influencer
> marketing — Yodo1's IPverse for in-game collabs, Sprout's Brand Fit Score for creators.
> Both are real products. Neither touches physical consumer products, and neither publishes
> any validation that their score predicts an outcome. So even the analogues are recommendation
> engines, not proven predictors."

> **[ASSUMED]** "We haven't found anyone scoring pre-deal fit for character × physical
> product — and we looked at the incumbents' own product pages, not just their marketing."
> *(Tag as assumed, not confirmed: desk research can't rule out a stealth startup or an
> undisclosed in-house agency tool — see the honesty guardrail below.)*

### Do NOT say
- ❌ "Competitors are all qualitative / nobody uses data." False — Circana, CharaBiz, Brand
  Finance, Alifish all sell numbers. Easily rebutted.
- ❌ "We're the only ones who quantify fit." Overclaims absence; IPverse and Sprout quantify a
  *form* of fit in other verticals.
- ❌ "The incumbent platforms are pure admin with no matchmaking." Not quite — **Brainbase
  Marketplace** does recommend/discover partners by similarity. Concede it, then draw the
  line: recommendation from history ≠ a *validated pre-deal fit score*. If a VC names
  Brainbase, agree it exists and move to the score-vs-recommend distinction.
- ❌ "No such tool exists." Say "no player we found," always.

## Assumption (b): "APAC / Taiwan is open ground"

### What the research actually found
- **Negosh's only substantiated APAC presence is Japan, and it is partner-operated** by
  InfoLens Inc. (Tokyo). Beyond Japan — **no evidence of any Negosh presence in Taiwan,
  Korea, China, or SEA.** No Chinese/Korean language support found; Japanese was added via
  InfoLens (single-source date, flagged UNVERIFIED). `negosh-apac.md §4–5`.
- **Negosh's Japan deal volume is UNKNOWN** — no press release or platform page discloses a
  number. The model is *partner-as-operator*, not a Negosh subsidiary, and Negosh's
  2024–2025 direction visibly shifts toward **digital/AI** (Spaceport, Made AI, negosh.ai)
  rather than APAC geographic expansion. `negosh-apac.md §3, §6`.
- **Taiwan specifically:** the ecosystem is **showcase/directory + government support**
  (TAICCA, TCBLA, CCPA "Taiwan IP Showcase") — **no Taiwan platform found that quantifies
  IP × product fit.** `quantified-licensing-landscape.md §6`, `consumer-product-gap.md §2d`.
- But "open ground" needs a caveat: **China is not open** in the fit-adjacent sense —
  **Alibaba Alifish** runs attribute-based big-data matchmaking at ~$4.1B retail scale
  (#1 APAC by one License Global ranking), and **Tencent T-JOY** runs a licensing program.
  These are not pre-deal fit predictors, but they are well-resourced incumbents occupying
  the *matchmaking* adjacency in the largest APAC market. `quantified-licensing-landscape.md §6`.

### The correction the pitch needs
"APAC/Taiwan is open ground" is **true for the specific thing PBC does** (quantified
pre-deal fit for character × physical product) and **true that Negosh isn't there** — but
loose if it implies APAC has no strong players at all. The safe version separates the two:
**the fit-intelligence layer is unoccupied across APAC including Japan; the broader
licensing market is not empty — it has incumbents (Alifish, Tencent, InfoLens/Negosh-JP),
which is demand validation, not a contradiction.**

### Exact wording safe to say to a VC
> **[CONFIRMED]** "Negosh — the US marketplace people point to — is in APAC in exactly one
> country: Japan, and only through a local partner, InfoLens. No Taiwan, Korea, China, or
> Southeast Asia presence we could find, and no Chinese or Korean interface. Their disclosed
> Japan deal volume is zero — meaning *undisclosed*, not that we know it's small."

> **[CONFIRMED]** "In Taiwan the entire IP-licensing infrastructure is trade shows,
> directories, and government programs — TAICCA, TCBLA, the Taiwan IP Showcase. Nobody here
> is scoring fit with data. That's home turf we can own first."

> **[ASSUMED]** "China isn't empty — Alibaba's Alifish and Tencent both run licensing
> platforms at scale. But they do attribute matchmaking and post-hoc sales tracking, not
> pre-deal fit prediction, and they're China-domestic. We start in Taiwan/APAC-ex-China
> where the layer is genuinely open." *(Assumed: characterizing competitors' roadmaps is
> inference; what's confirmed is what they market today.)*

### Do NOT say
- ❌ "APAC is a greenfield / nobody is doing licensing in Asia." False and reputation-damaging
  in a Taipei room — Japan, China, Korea all have mature licensing ecosystems.
- ❌ "Negosh failed in Japan" or "Negosh has low traction in Japan." **Unknown.** Say the deal
  volume is undisclosed. `negosh-apac.md §3` is explicit that traction is UNKNOWN.
- ❌ Quoting any Negosh brand/licensee count as traction — the counts are inconsistent across
  Negosh's own surfaces (800+/1000+ vs 500+/400 vs 250+/200+) and are *inventory*, not deals.

## Assumption (c): "Quantified fit changes brand behavior" (the ROI / adoption thesis)

### Verdict: **[UNTESTABLE BY DESK RESEARCH]**

This is the assumption that a fit score, once offered, will actually **change what brands and
licensors do** — that they'll pay for it, act on it, and get better outcomes. **Desk
research cannot settle this, and this file will not pretend otherwise.** No amount of
competitor mapping or data-availability analysis tells you whether a Taiwanese licensor will
sign a different licensee because of a PBC score.

What the research *can* offer is **one structural precedent and its limit — nothing more:**
- **Precedent (weak, honestly labeled):** influencer marketing was a gut-feel matching
  market until the interaction data went digital, and then a cluster of fit/affinity scoring
  products appeared and got bought (Sprout Brand Fit Score, Traackr, HypeAuditor, GRIN),
  backed by academic congruence-scoring work (Semerádová & Weinlich 2024).
  `quantified-licensing-landscape.md §2, §8`. **This shows a market *can* adopt fit scoring
  once data is digital — it does NOT show PBC's specific score changes a physical-licensing
  brand's behavior.** Different vertical, different buyer, different data maturity.
- **The honest gap:** in influencer marketing the underlying data was *born digital*; in
  physical licensing the performance data is fragmented, retrospective, and self-reported
  (`data-availability.md`). So the precedent's *enabling condition* is exactly the thing
  physical licensing lacks — which is why the analogy motivates the bet but cannot validate it.

### What this means for the pitch
- **Present (c) as the thing the raise pays to test, not as a proven claim.** The correct
  posture to a VC is: "This is our core hypothesis and it is *untested*; here is the cheapest
  experiment that tests it." That is more credible than a padded case.
- **Do not pad it with weak proxies.** Specifically, do **not** cite Circana property
  footprints, CharaBiz popularity, or IPverse's collab counts as evidence that *fit scoring
  changes behavior* — none of them measures adoption of a pre-deal fit score, and a sharp VC
  will see the substitution.

### Exact wording safe to say to a VC
> **[UNTESTABLE — say it as a hypothesis]** "Whether a fit score actually changes which deals
> get signed — that's the one thing we can't prove from a desk. It needs the live pipeline.
> The precedent we lean on is influencer marketing: it was gut-feel until the data went
> digital, and then fit-scoring tools got bought. We're betting the same happens in physical
> licensing — and the raise is to run that test, not to claim we've already won it."

> **[UNTESTABLE]** "Our honest position: the market and the data gap are confirmed. The
> behavior change is the hypothesis. We'd rather show you the exact experiment that de-risks
> it than pretend it's settled."

### The de-risking experiment (what actually tests (c) — flagged as proposal, not finding)
> **[PROPOSAL / ASSUMED]** A live pipeline test in Taiwan/APAC: score a set of real IP ×
> licensee candidate pairings *before* deals are signed, let a licensor act (or not) on the
> ranking, and measure whether score-ranked pairings a) get chosen and b) out-sell
> gut-chosen ones. Only this closes the loop `data-availability.md §6` says nobody has
> closed. *(This is a suggested test design, not evidence — do not present it as traction.)*

## The honesty guardrail (read before the meeting)

Three lines that keep every claim above defensible if a VC pushes:
1. **Absence is "not found," never "does not exist."** Direct evidence-of-absence in incumbent
   SaaS + broad no-evidence-found elsewhere; stealth startups and undisclosed in-house agency
   tools cannot be ruled out. `consumer-product-gap.md §3`.
2. **Negosh Japan traction is UNKNOWN — say undisclosed, quote no number.** `negosh-apac.md §3`.
3. **The data exists — concede it.** The claim is not "there's no data," it's "the data exists
   and no one has wired it into the pre-deal decision." `data-availability.md §7`.
