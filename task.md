CONTEXT (do not skip): From one brand interview (KFT), one creator interview
(Jolly Gee), and the founder's market read:
- HYPOTHESIS 1 (brand side): brands judge IP collaborations on ONE question -
  "will this make us money?" Awareness is secondary or unmeasurable. The real
  buyer is the marketing manager who must build the internal business case
  ("spend this, make that") to convince their boss.
- HYPOTHESIS 2 (creator side): creators mainly want outcomes, not involvement -
  "if they can outsource it and make money, they will." The conventional agency
  model, done correctly, lets the creator stay uninvolved and still earn. This
  DIRECTLY CHALLENGES Marrow's original design philosophy (giving creators more
  agency/control than the conventional model): would creators actually want
  more control, or do they just want proof the agency is any good - deals
  closed, visible process, better margins? Founder's refinement: perhaps what
  creators want is package-tracking-level visibility - a simple way to see a
  deal is going smoothly, plus the ability to ask questions about what is
  happening (e.g. an AI chat interface) - WITHOUT any operational burden.
- AFTERNOON REFINEMENTS (Gary + Brendon, 2026-07-17, all hypothesis-tier):
  ads sell distribution, the IP is the reason inside; collab value splits
  asymmetrically (the IP wins from being seen, the brand only from buying, so
  brands are buying conversion probability for THEIR audience); licensing
  lacks the ad industry's justification system (shared metrics that make
  spend defensible without proof); collab failure is visible (unsold merch)
  while ad failure never is - which scared budgets but makes an odds table
  buildable; new IPs could be priced by CLASS (similar-trait IPs' track
  records) until they earn their own data; and some IPs command no fee at
  all - "marketing licensing": license free or near-free for exposure plus
  performance data, as a deliberate IP lifecycle stage.
- COMPLICATION from our n=1 creator interview: she happily outsources, but her
  one validated agency pain was zero deals closed + zero visibility under an
  exclusive contract, and her stated fix was wanting to stay involved in
  important conversations. So visibility demands may appear only AFTER being
  burned - test whether that pattern generalizes.
