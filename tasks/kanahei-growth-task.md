# TASK: Kanahei - how one independent-origin IP grew across markets

## Why this research (context, do not restate in output)

PBC's thesis is fit-not-fame. Kanahei (カナヘイ) is a test case Gary wants
studied: an illustrator-origin IP (Kanahei is a single named Japanese
illustrator, active since the flip-phone era) that grew from personal work
into a multi-market licensing presence (LINE stickers, Piske & Usagi,
retail collabs across Asia). The question is HOW that growth actually
happened market by market - the mechanisms, the partners, the sequence -
because that is the playbook shape PBC sells to creators at a smaller
scale.

This is a single-IP longitudinal study, NOT a census. Census rules on
evidence still apply; census FORMAT does not.

## Scope

ONE IP: Kanahei / カナヘイ, including the character properties (Piske &
Usagi / ピスケ＆うさぎ, and any other named properties of hers that carry
their own licensing activity).

Markets, in priority order: Japan (home), Taiwan, Hong Kong, Korea,
Thailand, China (mainland - include even though outside the census ten,
since her growth path may run through it; mark it clearly as
outside-census-scope), then any other market where verifiable licensing
activity exists. Skip a market honestly if nothing verifiable surfaces -
absence recorded as absence.

Time: from first commercial licensing activity to present. Establish the
origin story briefly (illustrator → stickers → characters) only as far as
sources support it.

## Questions the output must answer, per market

1. ENTRY: when did verifiable commercial activity first appear in this
   market, and through WHAT - a licensed product, a brand collab, an
   agent/distributor deal, LINE stickers, a pop-up, retail import?
2. MECHANISM: who carried her into the market - a licensing agent (name
   it), a retailer, a brand initiating, LINE as platform, her own studio?
   The agency layer is the finding PBC cares most about: which agencies
   or rights-holders represent Kanahei where, as far as public record
   states it.
3. GROWTH SHAPE: what followed entry - one-off collabs, sustained
   merchandise lines, cafes/pop-ups, themed retail? List the documented
   collaborations with brand names and years (this part census-style:
   each row needs a source).
4. SCALE SIGNALS: audience/reach evidence per market where it exists
   (social following by platform, LINE sticker rankings, press-stated
   figures) - source every number, and follower-proxy caveats apply as
   in the census work.
5. WHAT DIDN'T HAPPEN: markets or formats she visibly has NOT entered,
   where the absence is itself informative - stated as absence-of-evidence
   with the search effort noted, never as a claim she was rejected.

## Cross-market synthesis (after the per-market sections)

- The sequence: which markets came in what order, and what evidence says
  about why (LINE's footprint? agent territories? fan-base-first vs
  retail-first?).
- The mechanism pattern: does her growth run through agents, platforms,
  or direct brand deals - and does the mechanism differ by market?
- The PBC-relevant read, clearly separated as interpretation: what this
  path suggests about how an independent-origin character IP scales
  regionally - stated as "this case suggests", never generalized to a
  market claim from one IP.

## Hard rules (standing, restate nothing, violate nothing)

- Every factual claim carries a source (URL or named publication + date).
  No source, no claim - drop it rather than hedge it.
- No fabricated pairings: every brand collab named must be verified from
  its own source, not recalled. The Marugame rule applies.
- Never estimate or state rights_owner, territory, exclusivity, or deal
  terms as fact - agency relationships are reported only as far as the
  public record states them, with the source.
- Follower/audience figures carry their platform, date-of-reading, and
  the current-proxy caveat where the figure is today's number applied to
  historical activity.
- Chinese/Japanese sources are in scope and preferred where they are the
  original record - quote the original with a translation.
- No pooled or cross-market percentages. This is one IP's story, not a
  market statistic.
- Checked-and-rejected hypotheses get recorded as such so no later reader
  re-derives them as findings.
