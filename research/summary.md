**Compiled:** 2026-07-15, closing the five-question hardening sweep.

## The one-paragraph answer

**The claim "nobody quantifies IP x product fit for physical consumer-product licensing" survived all five sweeps, in Chinese, Japanese, Korean, and English - but only in its precise form.**
Plenty of players quantify the IP (popularity, heat, value), several recommend partners (from history, attributes, or filters), one scores brand-relative IP selection for marketing collabs (Miaozhen), and consultants sell fit judgment by the hour.
Nobody found sells a validated prediction that a specific IP on a specific physical product will succeed.
The pitch must therefore say "nobody quantifies the pairing" - not "nobody has data" or "nobody does matching" - and be ready to name and distinguish the near-misses below, because an informed associate can find every one of them.

## Tier 1 - real offerings a VC could name; distinguish, don't deny

Ranked by how threatening the objection sounds when raised.

1. **Yodo1 IPverse** (ipverse.com, free, launched ~Jan 2026) - the only company marketing itself as AI fit-matching for IP licensing.
   Distinction: in-game/digital collabs vertical; mechanism is popularity rankings + historical-collab patterns + recommendation generation (DeepMatch); no published methodology or accuracy validation; nothing on physical product lines.
2. **秒针系统 Miaozhen IPPI** (China) - a measurement giant selling a brand-relative IP-selection score (社交影响/品牌助力/潜力预估 including category-and-brand relevance) inside its IP联名 solution.
   The closest sold analogue to pairing-fit scoring found anywhere in the sweep.
   Distinction: scores social-buzz inputs for short-cycle 联名 marketing campaigns, delivered as consulting + dashboards, vendor-claimed with zero outcome validation; not licensing programs, not sales outcomes, not physical-product fit.
3. **Sprout Social "Brand Fit Score"** (Apr 2025) - a named, shipped alignment score, but for influencer marketing; a rules/alignment score over content themes and audience interests, with a competitor publicly noting the methodology is opaque.
   Useful to the pitch as precedent that fit-scoring gets built once interaction data is digital.
4. **原仓IP数据 Yuancang** (yuancangip.com, China) - "AI推荐 数据引擎" tagline, 20k+ IP analytics DB, digitized tendering.
   Distinction: everything quantified is IP-side (heat, sales, audience, sentiment); the honest line is "原仓 quantifies the IP, nobody quantifies the pairing."
   Watch item: it is the most likely to move into pairing scoring.
5. **Sidechat IP / Spaceport** (spaceport.xyz) - real, VC-backed, AI in licensing.
   Distinction: its AI scores applicant deal-quality for licensors triaging inbound, not IP x product fit; and Spaceport is the same company as Negosh's 2024 digital partner, so treat as one ecosystem player.
6. **Brainbase Marketplace** - partner discovery with similarity-based recommendations ("manufacturers who have worked with similar partners"); no fit score; parent Jonas Software runs it as ops-SaaS.
7. **Nevium / Brandar / Valen Group** (US consulting) - "brand fit assessment" is literally on their service lists, including Nevium's "Brand Score Tool."
   Distinction: expert analysis delivered in reports; no product, no algorithm, no validation; this is the manual practice the product would replace.
8. **DoLicense** - claims ML brand-licensee matching; only sources are a mutually-affiliated vendor cluster; unverified, no press, no funding trail.
   Name it only if raised; label it unverified.

## Tier 2 - future-tense and admissions: incumbents see the gap and say so

These are the strongest "segment is open" citations because they come from the incumbents themselves.

- **JD's IP licensing head 高雷 (2022):** big-data matching is a *future* goal; licensing is a "相对感性" (gut-feel) industry.
  The single best quote in the corpus.
- **WITZ co-CEO 김정민 (2023):** IP-value measurement and licensee-customized IP recommendation are services "출시를 앞두고 있다" (awaiting launch); three years on, WITZ ships contract digitization, counterfeit detection, and a voice-IP pivot.
- **Sumitomo Corporation (2022):** diagnosed licensing matching as "アナログな方法" (trade shows and introductions), built CHARATARO as the answer - a filterable directory - and the PoC died; the domain is now squatted.
- **Alifish (2016-2021):** executive rhetoric about IP指数 and big-data matching; the live site today exposes a catalog and a contact form.
- **Academic review (JBR 2023):** licensing research itself is fragmented, with refined performance criteria and "sounder empirical insights" still on the future agenda - even the literature has no validated outcome model.

## Tier 3 - not threats; classify and move on

- **Licensing-ops SaaS** (Flowhaven, Octane5, Dependable, MyMediabox, Xelacore, RoyaltyZone): post-deal admin, uniformly.
- **IP-side data sellers**: Q Scores Cartoon Q (US), CharaBiz DATA and Video Research Kids/ex (Japan), 艺恩/Endata and CBNData/LeadLeo reports (China), Parrot Analytics (content demand) - popularity and demand inputs, human makes the pairing call.
- **Valuation/royalty firms** (Brand Finance, Interbrand, RoyaltyRange/RoyaltyStat, Kroll): value or arm's-length rate in isolation.
- **Marketplaces/directories/brokers**: Taiwan's entire ecosystem (udn, IPCP, 卡洛特, TAICCA, TCBLA), KOCCA's 1:1 fair matching, Innerboos, JD 京纪圈, Tao IP Library, generic concept testers (Kantar, Ipsos - per-case surveys, not a licensing product), Story/PIP Labs (on-chain licensing plumbing).

## Demand-validation reads (for the pitch narrative)

1. Three separate incumbents on three sides of the market (platform: JD; SaaS: WITZ; trading house: Sumitomo) each publicly framed quantified matching as the obvious next thing, and none shipped it - the gap is seen, wanted, and unfilled.
2. Paid manual substitutes exist at both ends: consultants sell fit judgment (Nevium, Brandar), and brands buy IP-side data (Q Scores, CharaBiz) to make the call themselves - people already pay for worse versions of the answer.
3. The adjacent verticals prove the build pattern: fit scoring appeared in influencer marketing (Sprout), in-game collabs (IPverse), and 联名 campaigns (Miaozhen IPPI) exactly where interaction data went digital first; physical-product licensing is the remaining vertical, and its data layer (POS, sell-through) is the hardest to assemble - which is the moat argument, see `data-availability.md`.

## Confidence and limits

- Every named characterization above is backed by a URL trail in its verdict file; single-source and vendor-only claims are flagged there (notably: Miaozhen IPPI capabilities, 原仓's AI tagline, Innerboos's matching feature, DoLicense entirely).
- The ecosystem-wide negatives are "not found under the documented queries" (each verdict file lists them verbatim), not exhaustive audits; Japan's agency layer (ShoPro, Sony Creative Products) and Korea's in-house IP operators (IPX, Kakao) were checked at search level only.
- Nothing in the sweep found a validated outcome-prediction model anywhere, including among the tier-1 names - "no evidence of validation" was true of every fit-ish claim encountered.
